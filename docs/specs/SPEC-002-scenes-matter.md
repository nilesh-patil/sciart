### belousov_zhabotinsky  (Reactions, M10, M12, M15, M16)
- **Intent**: a Belousov–Zhabotinsky dish seen from above. Thin, pale oxidation fronts sweep a dark medium. Each trails a tail that cools through orange, red and violet to black as the medium recovers. Broken wavefronts curl at their free ends into spirals: counter-rotating pairs, or a single spiral where one end is pinned to the dish wall. A pacemaker nucleus pulses slower target rings, which the spiral wave trains gradually push back. Every 62 s a light quench, as in the photosensitive Ru-catalysed BZ (Kuhnert, Agladze & Krinsky 1989), puts every wave out, and fresh broken fronts restart the dish. `gray_scott` shows Gray–Scott blobs that grow in place and `liesegang_rings` shows static bands; here every feature travels and the resting medium is black.
- **Model**: Barkley (1991) excitable medium on a no-flux (Neumann) rectangle: `∂u/∂t = ∇²u + ε⁻¹ u(1 − u)(u − u_th)`, `u_th = (v + b)/a`, `∂v/∂t = u − v`, with `a = 0.75`, `b = 0.06`, `ε = 0.02` (rigidly rotating spirals; the EZ-Spiral default). `u` stands for the autocatalyst HBrO₂. `v` stands for the oxidised catalyst (ferriin / Ru(bpy)₃³⁺), whose reduction releases the Br⁻ inhibitor; this is the two-variable Oregonator picture. The node spacing is fixed at `h = 0.5` space units (su), so the pattern scale is fixed in nodes. Each sub-step `τ = 0.0125` time units (tu) is Lie-split into three stages. (1) Explicit diffusion, using the isotropic 9-point Laplacian (Patra–Karttunen, as in `gray_scott`) with mirrored ghost nodes. The stencil sums mirror pairs first, `(W+E)`, `(N+S)`, `(NW+NE)` and `(SW+SE)`, so the update is bitwise left–right symmetric. (2) The reaction, with Barkley's (1991) implicit update on the decaying branch, `u ← u / (1 − (τ/ε)(1 − u)(u − u_th))` if `u < u_th`, and an explicit growth branch, `u ← u + (τ/ε) u(1 − u)(u − u_th)` otherwise; the growth branch stays bounded because `τ/ε ≤ 1` (Barkley and EZ-Spiral take it implicitly too). (3) `v ← v + τ(u − v)`. Because `(10/3)·τ/h² = 0.17 ≤ 1`, `τ/ε = 0.625 ≤ 1` and `τ ≤ 1`, every stage is a convex combination or a shrinking factor. So `u, v ∈ [0, 1]` holds exactly for any `b ≥ 0`, with no clamps. Measured on this exact scheme (NumPy prototype):
  - planar pulse speed `c = 3.58` su/tu, which is 0.85 `c_N`, where `c_N = √(2/ε)(½ − b/a) = 4.2` is the Nagumo limit at `v = 0`; the continuum value at `h = 0.1` is ≈ 3.9, and the O(ε) recovery effect accounts for the rest of the gap;
  - spiral period 3.95–4.2 tu;
  - spiral wavelength λ ≈ 25 nodes;
  - excited plateau ≈ 8 nodes long.

  Pacing is `RATE = 1.5` tu per simulated second, exactly 2 sub-steps per 1/60 s, through an accumulator that handles any `dt` (30 sub-steps at `dt = 0.25`). Seeding draws from `ctx.rng` once per cycle and places up to `k = clamp(round(nodes / 2000), 1, 4)` broken fronts. Each front is a straight segment:
  - its length is `L ∈ [1.2, 2.0]·λ` at a uniform random angle;
  - its midpoint is uniform in the central 60 % of each axis;
  - if a free end falls within 4 nodes of a wall, or the segment comes within λ/2 of an earlier segment, `L` shrinks in 10 % steps down to 0.8 λ; if it still does not fit, the draw is rejected. The distance between segments is 0 if they cross, else the nearest endpoint-to-segment distance. Each painted band reaches at most 6 nodes from its segment, so bands never touch or cross. A front still rejected after 32 tries is dropped, and a crowded dish gets fewer fronts rather than two tangled ones (≤ 1 in 10 on the tested lattices). The first front has no earlier segments to avoid; if even it fails the walls, the last draw is shrunk as far as needed, but not below 3 nodes (a shorter front cannot launch a wave, BZ-A2), and otherwise clipped to the lattice;
  - with probability 0.3 the segment is instead extended to a wall from its end nearer one, which leaves one free end and gives a single spiral. Extending the far end would draw a front across the whole dish and leave no room for the others;
  - it is a 2-node strip of `u = 1` backed by a 6-node strip whose `v` ramps 0.69 → 0.36 away from the front. Every `v ≥ 0.315` gives `u_th > ½`, so the strip blocks backward propagation, and the ramp already shades as a tail.

  There is 1 pacemaker (2 when nodes ≥ 8000), at a normalised position in `[0.15, 0.85]²`. It first fires 1 tu into each cycle and then every `T_pm = 6` tu (≈ 1.5 × the spiral period), setting `u = 1` where `v < 0.1` in a disc of radius 2.5 nodes. The higher-frequency source wins (Zaikin & Zhabotinsky 1970; Winfree 1972), so the spiral domain grows into the target pattern. The story is a looping `Timeline [("waves", 60 s), ("quench", 2 s)]`. During the quench, `b` ramps 0.06 → 0.45 with `ease::smoothstep` over 0.5 s, so `u_th ≥ 0.6 > ½`. Fronts retreat, excited regions collapse once `v > a − b = 0.3`, pacemakers stay off, and `v` decays as `e^{−t}`. There is no excited node 1 s into the quench, and max `v` is ≈ 0.047 at its end (3 tu of `e^{−t}` decay), which shades nothing (`s = 0.55·(v/0.69)² < 0.06`). At the cycle boundary `b` resets and a new seed set is painted over the dark field.
- **Motion**: fronts advance ≈ 11 nodes/s, and a lattice node is ≈ 1 pixel column. Spirals turn every ≈ 2.8 s. At t = 0 the seeded fronts already read as short comets. By 1 s their free ends visibly curl, and by 5 s the spirals have formed. Over the minute, spiral wave trains push the target rings back toward their nucleus. On small dishes the takeover is quick: the 80×48 lattice at 80×24 is ≈ 3 × 2 wavelengths and fronts cross it in ≈ 7 s, so the spirals reach the pacemaker within seconds, after which it only fires into gaps between their fronts (5–9 of its 15 beats per cycle find recovered medium in its disc, mean 7.6 over seeds 1–15 and 42); the rings play out longer on large dishes. The quench fades the dish to black within ≈ 1 s and it stays dark for ≈ 1 s, then new fronts appear, brightening from dark over 0.5 s (display only: the model paints them at full strength). The very first seeding shows at once. The loop is 62 s.
- **Resize**: the lattice is `grid::lattice_for(Size(w, 2h), 20_000, 1.25)`, since Pixels are square, `w × 2h`. That gives 197×100 at 200×50 and exactly 80×48 at 80×24: below the cap the per-axis clamp makes the lattice the pixel grid itself. Above the cap, nodes are stretched relative to pixels by at most √1.25 (≤ 12 %). An unchanged lattice leaves the state untouched. Otherwise `u` and `v` are `resample_bilinear`ed into the new lattice (its convex weights keep `[0, 1]`), and the pattern relaxes to its natural λ within a few rotations. Pacemaker and seed positions are stored normalised in `[0, 1]²`. The first resize seeds. Resize also rebuilds per-axis sampling tables (source index and weight per pixel column and per pixel row). Shading happens in `update` and `resize`, with no `RefCell`: `u` and `v` are sampled bilinearly per pixel (the identity when the lattice equals the pixel grid) and shaded into a `raster::Pixels` without allocating; `render` only blits it and writes the label.
- **Inks**: `Role::Seq` encodes the *phase of the excitation cycle*, not a concentration. Let `r = clamp(v / (a − b₀), 0, 1)` with the unquenched `b₀ = 0.06`, so the quench ramp never brightens the tails. Excited cells (`u ≥ ½`) get `s = 1 − 0.45·r`, which is palest at the fresh front and orange at the back of the plateau. Refractory cells get `s = 0.55·r²`, going red → violet → dark as the medium recovers. `s < 0.06` gives `Ink::BLANK`, so resting medium is the terminal background. Every other pixel is `ink::seq(s)`. The prototype measured ≈ 3–6 % of pixels pale (`s ≥ 0.85`) and ≈ 40–52 % lit, so there are thin bright edges, dim tails and real dark gaps. The label is `Text`.
- **Look** *(M16, GEN-R25; supersedes the Inks and ASCII below and the half-block shading in Resize; no longer pinned by GEN-R22)*: no bands, no ground: each wave is braille threads one dot thick on black, traced on the lattice by marching squares (`Dots::contour_by`, CORE-R105; one node per 2×2 dots below the cap, the lattice rule unchanged) and drawn once per frame in `prepare` (GEN-R20). The crest is the level `u = ½` in the cells whose mean `v` is below 0.25, i.e. the leading edge of each front (the back of the excited plateau, where `v` is near `a − b`, is not drawn): a bright `Hot` 230 thread. Behind it, where all four corners have `u < ½` (recovering medium), two `Cold` tail lines trail the crest at the levels `v = 0.42` (`Cold` 190) and `v = 0.2` (`Cold` 150; round 2, was 120), so the recovery reads as a fading wake with black between the lines; resting medium and the quenched dish are black. A reseed's fade-in scales the levels (a line below level 12 is not drawn). ASCII falls back by braille dot count.
- **ASCII**: the `Pixels::blit` closure takes the larger Seq level of the two pixels: `≥ 217` → `@` (`s ≥ 0.85`), `≥ 179` → `#` (0.70), `≥ 102` → `+` (0.40), `≥ 51` → `:` (0.20), any other non-blank pixel → `.`, and two blank pixels → nothing. Fronts read as `@@##++::..` comets curling on white space.
- **Label**: right-aligned on row `h − 1` with `draw::label_right` (a blank cell each side, so it never touches the field; GEN-R13); there is no label when h < 4 or w < 14. The room is `title::readout_room`, which never exceeds half the row (CORE-R76). `{word}` is the beat: `waves`, or during the quench `light quench` (room ≥ 12) or `quench`. The name is not repeated: the title names the reaction.
  - room ≥ 44: `{word}  Barkley a=0.75 b=0.06 eps=0.02`;
  - room ≥ 20: `{word}  b=0.06`;
  - room ≥ 6: `{word}`.

  `b` is printed live to 2 decimals, so it reads 0.45 during the quench: the on-screen sign that the quench is a loss of excitability. The label is ASCII and written into a stack buffer without allocation.
- **Perf**: at 200×50 the lattice has 19.7k nodes × 2 sub-steps of ~30 f32 flops each, plus 20k bilinear pixel samples, the shading and the blit. Measured ≈ 0.31 ms (criterion, 2026-09-27: 310 μs) per `update(1/60)` + `render` at 200×50 (aim < 1 ms). `dt = 0.25` costs ≈ 2 ms (30 sub-steps). Reseeding paints ≤ 4 segments (≈ 2k nodes). There are no allocations after resize.
- **Acceptance**: `BZ-A1` `bz_step` maps any field with `u, v ∈ [0, 1]` back into `[0, 1]` with no clamping in the kernel (64 seeded random 24×16 fields, 50 sub-steps each, at `b = 0.06` and at `b = 0.45`), and the rest state `(0, 0)` is an exact fixed point; `BZ-A2` excitability is all-or-none on a 64×64 lattice: a disc of radius 4 nodes at `u = 0.9·b/a` (`v = 0`) decays to `max u < 1e-3` within 2 tu, while `u = 1` discs of radius 1.5 and 4 nodes each launch a ring whose outermost excited node (`u ≥ ½`) lies ≥ 12 nodes from the centre at 2 tu (prototype 13.0 and 17.0); `BZ-A3` a planar pulse on a 3×160 strip (scene `h` and `τ`; the `u = ½` front is fitted over nodes 40–120) travels at `c ∈ [0.75, 0.92]·c_N` with `c_N = 4.2` (prototype 0.85), and a pacemaker of period 6 tu at the strip's end emits fronts spaced `c·T_pm/h ± 10 %` apart, excluding the leading front (prototype 42–44 nodes against 43); the core drifts ≈ 1 node per turn away from the walls, hence the window), with the probe ≥ λ from the window's mean tip (≈ 1–1.5 s in a debug build); `BZ-A5` a horizontal broken front with two free ends, centred on a 72×48 lattice, becomes a counter-rotating pair: at every sub-step up to 12 tu, `u` and `v` are bitwise mirror-symmetric about the line between columns 35 and 36, and at 12 tu the half-plane behind the seed holds excited nodes (`u ≥ ½`) on both sides of that line; `BZ-A6` the lattice never exceeds 20 000 nodes or the pixel grid in either axis (displays 1×1 to 400×120), a resize between two displays in the same aspect bucket that are both at or above the cap (240×60 → 250×62) leaves `u` and `v` bitwise unchanged, and any seeded resize sequence keeps `u, v ∈ [0, 1]`; `BZ-A7` at the end of the 2 s quench beat `max u < 1e-3` and `max v < 0.06` (prototype ≈ 0.047; every pixel is then blank, since `s = 0.55·(v/0.69)² < 0.06`), no pacemaker fires during the quench, each cycle re-seeds deterministically from `ctx.rng` (same seed ⇒ same fronts and pacemakers; consecutive cycles differ), on lattices of at least 64×40 (50 seed sets each at 64×40, 80×48, 197×100 and 100×64) every segment that is not wall-anchored keeps both ends ≥ 4 nodes from the walls, no two segments come within λ/2 of each other, and at most 1 in 10 fronts is dropped for want of room, while a 20×8 lattice still gets its one front; ### catalytic_surface  (Reactions, M13, M16)
- **Intent**: a platinum surface seen atom by atom as carbon monoxide burns on it: CO and oxygen land, meet, and leave as CO₂ in tiny flashes; as the CO fraction in the gas is swept, the surface swings from oxygen-choked to reactive to CO-choked, with islands nucleating and flipping.
- **Model**: Ziff–Gulari–Barshad lattice model on a capped square lattice (≤ 200×100): pick a site; with probability `y` try CO adsorption (one empty site), else O₂ dissociative adsorption (two neighbouring empty sites); adjacent CO + O react to CO₂ which leaves (flash). Rejection-free batches: ≤ 12k attempts per update. `y` sweeps along a `Timeline` through the two kinetic phase transitions (`y₁ ≈ 0.39`, `y₂ ≈ 0.525`), with slow CO desorption so the CO-poisoned phase can recover (a 0.4 % desorption channel per attempt: CO always leaves, O a tenth of the time, so the O-poisoned phase also recovers). The gas is fed as a jet onto the centre: the local `y` falls by 0.14·r from the centre (r the distance over the half-diagonal), so each transition sits on a ring that moves with the sweep. The jet's `y` follows a looping 36 s Timeline *(changed, M16 round 2: was 36.5 s)*: 5 s at 0.545 (CO islands nucleate at the centre), 7 s up to 0.65 (the CO disc grows to the corners), 2.5 s held, 9 s down to 0.53 (O eats the disc back along a ring front), 5 s held (reactive), 4.5 s down to 0.48 (the O layer closes in from the corners; was 0.43 with a 0.5 s hold, which closed nearly every front and left a dead frame) and 3 s back: the O-poisoned caption is a ≈ 5 s beat with O covering ≈ 80–88 % of the surface, and somewhere on the surface the local `y` is always inside the window.
- **Motion**: the loop opens part-way through the fall from 0.65 (at ≈ 19 s, jet `y` ≈ 0.59): orange CO and blue O domains already meet along a sparking front at t = 0, and the CO disc then recedes as it does every loop; fronts move as `y` sweeps; sparks throughout; the loop takes 36 s.
- **Resize**: lattice regridded (ADR-0012, nearest site) and the gradient re-laid; the first lattice starts split the way the opening `y` splits it (CO where the local `y` exceeds `y₂` = 0.525, ≈ 40 % of the sites, O around it, 10 % vacancies by a fixed hash), is pre-rolled 12 sweeps at that `y`, then runs a tenth of a second of frames so the front already sparks.
- **Inks** (M15, ADR-0018: the lattice is the art, in flat semantic domains like M11's `ising_model`; were `Ramp(Starlight)`/`Ramp(Sienna)`): one half-block pixel per site, nearest (1:1 up to the cap). Empty platinum is the terminal's black, O a dark `Cold` blue (level 20), CO a dim `Hot` orange (70) and its islands' edge sites (CO with a non-CO neighbour, periodic) bright `Hot` (200), so the edges, where the reaction runs, carry the light and the two species read as a complementary pair. A deterministic 4 % sample of the CO₂ events blinks its site white, `Star` 255 while its brightness is above half and 170 below, fading with τ = 0.1 s (gone in ≈ 0.3 s), exposed per update and drawn once per frame (GEN-R20). The O-covered surface is the one filled ground in this batch: it is the lattice itself, as M11's Ising domains are. No blur, glow or time smoothing. ASCII: a density ramp by brightness (`raster::ascii_ramp_cell`). Readout `CO fraction y=0.54  CO2 <count>` (or `y=0.54`).
- **Look** *(M16, GEN-R25; supersedes the M15 inks above)*: no ground and no half-block pixels: only the island boundaries are drawn, as braille threads one dot thick on black. The species field (CO +1, O −1, empty platinum 0) is box-blurred over 2 sites each way (clipped at the lattice edges, so lone sites, vacancies and the screen edge draw nothing) and contoured by marching squares (`Dots::contour`, CORE-R105): the `+0.30` level, on the CO side of every front, is a `Hot` 210 thread and the `−0.45` level, on the O side, a `Cold` 235 thread *(round 2: CO level lowered from +0.45 so small CO islands in the reactive window show warm; Cold raised from 200, which read navy beside Hot)*, so each CO/O front is a complementary pair of threads with the reacting (mostly empty) zone black between them, the CO disc a `Hot` ring inside a `Cold` one, O islands `Cold` loops. The CO₂ blinks are single `Star` dots at their site (255, then 170, gone in ≈ 0.3 s, as before). In the short O-poisoned beat the O layer still holds a few dozen holes, drawn as `Cold` loops, with the blinks between them: no frame is dead (CAT-A10). ASCII by braille dot count.
- **M23 look** (IMP-15): each reaction front is a bright thread with a dim second contour just inside it (`Hot` 95 at +0.18, `Cold` 120 at −0.33); the late uniform-CO beat still reads sparse and is tracked as a backlog spec decision (B-92).
- **Title**: `Catalysis · CO burns on platinum`; captions `oxygen-poisoned`, `reactive`, `CO-poisoned`.
- **Stylisation**: a lattice-gas model (ZGB), not Pt(110) with its surface reconstruction; the desorption channel is a stylisation so both poisoned phases recover within the loop (plain ZGB poisons irreversibly); only a sample of the reactions is drawn as sparks; the PEEM images are greyscale, the colours are false.
- **Acceptance**: `CAT-A1` at `y = 0.3` the surface fills with O (coverage ≥ 0.9 after the relaxation time) and at `y = 0.6` with CO; `CAT-A2` at `y = 0.45` the CO₂ production rate stays > 0 and coverages stay mixed; `CAT-A3` adsorption never overfills a site and O₂ needs two empty neighbours; `CAT-A4` attempts per update never exceed the cap; `CAT-A5` captions follow the surface: `oxygen-poisoned` when O coverage ≥ 0.8, `CO-poisoned` when CO coverage ≥ 0.8, else `reactive`; the sweep reaches all three; `CAT-A6` over a loop the surface is poisoned (either caption) ≤ 14 s and CO₂ production never stalls (> 0.01 per site per sweep in every half second), and the gas gradient falls by `GRADE` from the centre to the corners; `CAT-A7` *(changed, M16)* nothing is drawn as half-blocks or backgrounds; a blink is one `Star` dot, 255 at full and 170 a step dimmer as it fades, gone below its threshold; at 80×24 the front blinks from the first frame and, with no new events, every blink is gone within 18 updates (0.3 s). `CAT-A8` the loop opens mid-window (jet `y` in [0.52, 0.60]) on balanced domains (CO holds 35–55 % of the occupied sites), with sparks already lit and the `reactive` caption. `CAT-A9` *(new, M16)* threads only at fronts: a lattice split into a CO half and an O half draws `Hot` dots only on the CO side within 3 sites of the front and `Cold` only on the O side, and a uniform O or CO cover with 10 % vacancies draws nothing; `CAT-A10` *(new, M16 round 2)* no dead frame: at 160×45, sampled every 0.25 s over a whole loop, at least 2 % of the field's cells are lit.

### chemical_garden  (States of Matter, M13, M18)
- **Intent**: a seed crystal of a metal salt dropped in water glass: a membrane forms, swells with water by osmosis, bursts, and a jet of salt solution rises and sheaths itself in new membrane: blue tubes grow upward in jerks into a garden; a final act shows a hydrothermal vent chimney, where such mineral membranes are *hypothesised* to have housed early life.
- **Model**: tubes as a fixed-capacity segment pool (≤ 600) with tips; each tip has an internal osmotic pressure that rises at a rate ∝ concentration difference (C: precipitation) and bursts when it exceeds a membrane strength (seeded ±30 %); a burst emits a buoyant jet (P: buoyancy) that extends the tip upward by a random-walk-biased step and precipitates a new membrane segment; occasional branching; precipitate particles (pool 800) flake and sink. Vent act: a warm plume rising from a chimney with mineral walls growing (same model, different parameters) and a caption naming the hypothesis.
- **Motion**: bursts every 0.3–2 s per tip; garden grows 30 s; vent act 20 s; reset.
- **Resize**: tank geometry in tank units; pools kept.
- **Inks** (M15, ADR-0018: M11's roles in flat steps; were muted named ramps anti-aliased by coverage): a black tank. Each salt keeps its colour in a semantic role: copper blue `Cold`, cobalt pink `Accent`, iron rust `Hot`, nickel green `Particle`. Tubes are flat capsule strokes of radius 0.5–1 px (the chimney wall's five columns 0.75 px, so the stack reads as mineral strands, not a block): a pixel is set when its centre lies within the radius + ¼ px. Levels are flat steps: an old tube 120, a fresh one (first 4 s) 200, the growing tips and seed crystals 255 (a tip swells from a point to a 1-px disc once its pressure passes 70 % of the membrane strength); a garden fading out drops to 120, then 60 for its last half. The floor is one `Dim` 60 pixel row; jets and flakes single pixels in their salt at 90; vent smoke single `Dim` 110 pixels (parcels with life ≥ 0.4), `Hot` 150 at the orifice. Gas bubbles are simulated but not drawn. ASCII: tubes `|` (above 90) or `,`, floor `_`, smoke `.`.
- **Title**: `Chemical garden · osmosis bursts`; captions `osmotic bursts`, `life's vents?` (a question: the vent link is a hypothesis).
- **Stylisation**: phenomenological burst model (no fluid solver); the origin-of-life link is a hypothesis (stated in the caption and here).
- **Acceptance**: `GRD-A7` *(changed, M15)* thin stepped tubes on a black tank: at 80×24 and 200×50, seed 7, at t = 5, 20 and 40 s, every lit pixel is at one of the scene's eight step levels, lit pixels cover 0.2–20 % of the tank, and no cell shows an `o` glyph; `GRD-A1` a tip's pressure rises monotonically between bursts and drops after one; `GRD-A2` tube tips only move upward on average (mean dy < 0 upward over 30 s); `GRD-A3` the segment pool never exceeds capacity and the scene resets when full; `GRD-A4` the vent act's caption is `life's vents?` (a question, never a claim); `GRD-A5` precipitate particles fall; `GRD-A6` a renewed garden fades out over 5 s while the new one grows, and the tank never shows an empty frame (act changes included).

### electron_orbitals  (States of Matter, M10, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: the hydrogen atom as its quantum states actually look. A Monte-Carlo cloud of electron positions is drawn from |ψ_nlm|² and turns slowly in 3-D. Lobes are coloured by the sign of the wavefunction, and the nucleus glints through the nodes. Between states the atom passes through a real superposition, and the charge cloud sloshes from lobe to lobe in a calm breath: the true Bohr beat, slowed by a factor the label states. That oscillating dipole is how the atom emits or absorbs Lyman-α, Hα or Paschen-α light. The cloud is centred. The r₉₀ surface of 4f spans 0.75 of the height, and smaller n is drawn smaller but never as a dot. Point density is matched to each orbital's on-screen size, so 1s is a speckled ball, not a solid disc, and 4f's lobes are not sparse. The eye reads lobe shape first, then the colour of the sign, then the corner label. Nothing else is on screen.
- **Model**: atomic units, a₀ = 1. `ψ = R_nl(r)·Y(r̂)` with `R_nl = √((2/n)³(n−l−1)!/(2n(n+l)!)) e^{−ρ/2} ρ^l L^{(2l+1)}_{n−l−1}(ρ)`, ρ = 2r/n (generalised Laguerre by its three-term recurrence). Real harmonics on the unit direction (x, y, z): `s = 1/√(4π)`, `p_z = √(3/4π) z`, `p_x = √(3/4π) x`, `d_z² = √(5/16π)(3z² − 1)`, `d_xz = √(15/4π) xz`, `d_xy = √(15/4π) xy`, `f_z³ = √(7/16π)(5z³ − 3z)`, `f_xyz = √(105/4π) xyz`. The gallery is a closed dipole-allowed loop (Δl = ±1): 1s → 2p_z → 3d_z² → 4f_z³ → 3d_xz → 4f_xyz → 3d_xy → 2p_x → 1s. Energies are `E_n = −R_H/n²` with `R_H = R∞hc·μ/mₑ = 13.5983 eV` (reduced mass). Lines are `λ = hc/ΔE` with hc = 1239.842 eV·nm: Lyα 121.57 nm, Hα 656.47 nm, Pα 1875.6 nm (vacuum). Sampling is exact and separable, because every real harmonic here is a product Θ(θ)·Φ(φ): `|ψ|² d³r = [r²R² dr]·[Θ² d cosθ]·[Φ² dφ]`. Each factor is tabulated once as a 1024-knot inverse CDF (r ∈ [0, 1.5·r₉₉.₉], cos θ ∈ [−1, 1], φ ∈ [0, 2π)). One sample is three linear-interpolated lookups driven by `ctx.rng` uniforms. Each point also stores fixed uniforms `u_i` (visibility) and `v_i` (mixture), plus ψ of its own orbital and of its partner (f32 storage). Superposition a → b: `Ψ = c_a ψ_a e^{−iE_a t} + c_b ψ_b e^{−iE_b t}` with `c_a = cos χ`, `c_b = sin χ`, so `|Ψ|² = c_a²ψ_a² + c_b²ψ_b² + 2c_a c_b ψ_a ψ_b cos ωt`. Display sampler (exact intensity, per-orbital density): orbital k uses a prefix of `M_k = clamp(round(0.8·π(4ρ_k)²), 256, 16384)` iid points, where ρ_k is its on-screen r₉₀ radius in rows (View), which gives ≈ 0.4 visible points per dot inside r₉₀. For a → b: `M(χ) = (c_a²/M_a + c_b²/M_b)⁻¹`, `p_a = M(χ)c_a²/M_a` and `p_b = M(χ)c_b²/M_b`, so p_a + p_b = 1. Point i < M_a of slot S_a is proposed iff `v_i < p_a`, and point i < M_b of S_b iff `v_i < p_b`, so proposals have intensity exactly `M(χ)·q` with `q = c_a²ψ_a² + c_b²ψ_b²`. A proposed point is visible iff `u_i < w/2`, where `w = |Ψ|²/q = 1 + 2c_a c_b ψ_a ψ_b cos ωt / q ∈ [0, 2]` (AM–GM), evaluated in f64 (q ≤ 0 or non-finite ⇒ hidden). The visible points therefore have expected density exactly `(M(χ)/2)·|Ψ(t)|²` at every instant, and their count glides from M_a/2 to M_b/2. Display fade: on screen a point is proposed iff `v_i < p_k·τ(r)`, where τ is 1 inside `r_in` and falls linearly to 0 at `r_out`. The window is (r₈₈, r₉₅) of the states on screen (the wider (r₉₀, r₉₇) left lone single-dot braille cells a few columns out from the lobes, which read as grain rather than cloud), blended geometrically (`exp(lerp(ln r_a, ln r_b, c_b²))` componentwise, exactly a's at c_b² = 0 and b's at 1) and shared by both slots, so the displayed density is exactly `τ(r)·(M(χ)/2)|Ψ|²`, and the far dust ends inside the frame. Given a proposal, `v_i/p_k` is a fixed uniform independent of `u_i`, so the fade is shimmer-free. Because u and v are fixed per point, the cloud changes only where thresholds are crossed: no shimmer, no pops. At the endpoints c² (and therefore p) is set to exactly 0 or 1. The centroid follows `⟨r⟩(t) = sin 2χ·d_ab cos ωt` with `d_ab = ⟨a|r|b⟩`: 0.7449, 2.452, 5.188, 2.995, 3.867, 3.867, 2.123 and 0.7449 a₀ around the loop. Time is slowed, and the label says by how much: the beat is the true Bohr frequency ΔE/h (h = 4.135667696×10⁻¹⁵ eV·s; Lyα 2.466 PHz, Hα 456.7 THz, Pα 159.8 THz) divided by a round per-line factor S = 4×10¹⁵ / 10¹⁵ / 5×10¹⁴, so `f_disp = ΔE/(h·S)` = 0.62 / 0.46 / 0.32 Hz for Lyα / Hα / Pα: a calm breath with 1.8 / 1.4 / 1.0 cycles per 3 s beat, still ordered by ΔE as the physics is. (The previous `2.0 Hz·√(ΔE/ΔE_Lyα)` compression made 1s–2p_z flip every 0.25 s, a vibrating blob 2–4× faster than anything else in the scene.) ω = 2π f_disp with `t` counted from the rise start; a pair that is not a gallery α line would have no line, no factor and no beat. Timeline per orbital: `hold` 6 s (pure; this is χ = π/2 of the (prev, a) pair and χ = 0 of the (a, next) pair, which give the same visible set), `rise` 2 s (χ 0 → π/4, smoothstep), `beat` 3 s (χ = π/4, full sloshing), `fall` 2 s (χ π/4 → π/2). That is 13 s per orbital and a 104 s loop, starting at a seeded orbital's hold. Two-slot ring: during a's hold the free slot is filled with S_next, and S_a's partner ψ is rewritten to ψ_next (≤ 256 points per 1/60 s of dt; any remainder completes at the rise boundary). View: orbital (x, y, z) maps to world (x, z, −y), so orbital z is screen-up. One world unit is the orbital's r₉₀ times `g_k = (r₉₀,k/r₉₀,4f)^{0.3}` (r₉₀ = 2.66 / 7.99 / 15.80 / 25.99 a₀ for 1s / 2p / 3d / 4f), so `ρ_k = R·g_k` with `R = fit_radius(stage, 0.75)`, which is 0.50 R, 0.70 R, 0.86 R and R. The stage is every row above the label (all rows when the label is hidden), and the nucleus sits in its middle cell with the projected origin at that cell's centre, so a nodal plane through the nucleus empties one whole row. During transitions ln r₉₀ and ln g are lerped with the weight `glide(c_b²)`, so the larger state of the pair sets the scale while it holds at least half the weight: `1 − (1 − 2w)₊²` into a larger state, `(2w − 1)₊²` out of one. A plain c_b² would draw the larger state bigger than in its own hold (× 1.47 for 1s → 2p_z at w = ½) and push its dust off the frame. Camera: `geom::Camera` with distance 6 and a per-state pose (α_k yaw offset, p_k pitch, rock amplitude, phase φ_k), lerped component-wise with weight c_b²: 1s, 2p_z, 3d_z² and 4f_z³ (0, 0.15, 0.6, 0); 2p_x and 3d_xz (0, 0.15, 0.25, π/2); 4f_xyz (π/4, 0, 0.8, 0); 3d_xy (0, 1.05, 0.6, 0). ### fractal_growth  (States of Matter, M6, M12, M15; renamed M11 from `crystal_growth`)

### gray_scott  (Reactions, M6, M15; upgraded M10; renamed M11 from `chemical_reaction`, M17)
- **Intent**: upgrade of the M6 scene; this section replaces the M6 one, and the scene moves to `Category::Chemistry` (CORE-R77). It shows living reaction–diffusion patterns (Pearson's self-replicating spots, not a Turing instability) from the Gray–Scott system `U + 2V → 3V, V → P` (Gray & Scott 1983; Pearson 1993). One culture walks through five regimes of Pearson's (F, k) plane: spots that bud and divide, a short beat of branching coral, then three regimes that never settle (loops, a swarm of spots and short worms, and moving spots). Each regime melts into the next, so the pattern reorganises instead of being replaced. The field is drawn in half-block pixels on the sequential colormap: pale-yellow cores in orange bodies inside faint violet depletion halos, floating on the terminal's own black, which stays the majority of the screen in every regime a visit opens in. Nothing else is drawn except one corner label. At 80×24 there are about forty mitosis spots, each ≈ 5 columns × 2½ rows. At 200×50 there are about sixty, each ≈ 10 columns × 5 rows, round with graded edges. The scene stays distinct from `belousov_zhabotinsky` (travelling spiral fronts) and `liesegang_rings` (fixed concentric bands) because its forms are near-stationary blobs and stripes that divide, branch and reorganise.
- **Model**: `∂u/∂t = D_u∇²u − uv² + F(1 − u)` and `∂v/∂t = D_v∇²v + uv² − (F + k)v`, with `D_u = 2 D_v` and `D_v = 0.03` nodes² per reaction time unit. Kept from M6: the isotropic 9-point Laplacian (Patra–Karttunen), a periodic lattice, and an explicit Euler sub-step τ = 0.5 inside the positivity bounds `τ(10/3·D_u + 1 + F) ≤ 1` and `τ(10/3·D_v + F + k) ≤ 1`. The bounds are const-asserted for every preset and checked along the whole walk (CHEM-A5); the step is a convex combination plus non-negative sources, so u, v ≥ 0 and u ≤ 1, and decaying diffusive modes stay decaying (τ·D·16/3 ≪ 2). Every preset has its own **reaction rate** (reaction time units per simulated second, see the walk): 400 for mitosis and coral (800 sub-steps/s, ≈ 13.3 per frame), 70 for loops, 110 for swarm and 150 for spots. A morph lerps the rate by the same weight m as F and k, and τ stays 0.5, so the rate only sets how many sub-steps a frame runs (at most 200 per `update`, the 400 rate at dt = 0.25) and the positivity bounds are unaffected. ### ising_model  (States of Matter, M10, M12, M15, M16, M17)
- **Intent**: the ferromagnetic phase transition as a living mosaic. A quench turns static into two colours of domain that swell, round off and straighten. Reheating melts them into a dim boil, and a slow cool through the critical point leaves clusters of every size breathing (critical opalescence). *(changed, M16)* Only the domain walls are drawn, so the eye follows the shrinking bubbles and the straightening walls, and the froth of walls that boils up near the critical point.
- **Model**: 2-D Ising `H = −J Σ⟨ij⟩ sᵢsⱼ`, `sᵢ = ±1`, `J = k_B = 1`, zero field, periodic (torus) boundaries. The lattice is capped: `L = grid::lattice_for(Size(w, 2h), 16 384, 1.25)` in square pixel units, so 200×50 → 178×91 (≈ 1.1 px per spin) and 80×24 → 80×48 (below the cap the lattice is the pixel grid itself). Kinetics are single-spin Glauber heat-bath (Glauber 1963), random-sequential, with one `u64` per attempt from a scene-local SplitMix64 seeded once from `ctx.rng` at the first resize (GEN-R7). Bits 32–47 and 48–63 pick `x = (r₁₆·Lw)>>16` and `y = (r₁₆·Lh)>>16` (≤ 0.3 % non-uniform, harmless because every heat-bath move leaves the Boltzmann distribution invariant). The local field is `h = Σ_nn sⱼ ∈ {−4,−2,0,2,4}`; set `sᵢ = +1` iff the low 32 bits are `< thr[h]`, where `thr = round(p₊·2³²)` as u64 (so `p = 0, 1` are exact) and `p₊(h) = 1/(1 + e^{−2h/T})`. The 5-entry table is rebuilt once per update with T clamped to `[0.05, 100]` (NaN ⇒ T_c). This is the same model-A equilibrium, universality and coarsening class as Metropolis; it replaces checkerboard Metropolis because deterministic sublattice sweeps flip every ΔE = 0 spin at high T (a period-2 strobe) and because random-sequential updates give smooth fractional sweeps per frame at any lattice size. The rate is `max(60·N/16 384, 1)` Monte Carlo sweeps (MCS) per simulated second (N = lattice sites; one MCS per 1/60 s step at the cap), realised as `⌊acc⌋` attempts from an accumulator gaining `rate·N·dt`. Domain size relative to the lattice scales as `√(MCS/N)`, so coarsening looks the same at every terminal size. Temperature is in units of J/k_B with `T_c = 2/ln(1+√2) = 2.269 185 314` (Onsager 1944). References: `m₀(T) = (1 − sinh(2/T)⁻⁴)^{1/8}` below T_c and 0 above (Yang 1952); exact energy per spin `e(T) = −coth 2β·[1 + (2/π)·k′·K(k)]` with `k′ = 2 tanh² 2β − 1` (signed complementary modulus, computed directly to avoid cancellation), `K = π/(2·AGM(1, |k′|))`, and the product `k′·K` taken as its limit 0 when `k′ = 0`, so `e(T_c) = −√2`. T/T_c follows a looping `Timeline` of 58 s: `coarsen` 20 s at 0.60 → `reheat` 8 s, smoothstep 0.60→1.25 → `hot` 5 s at 1.25 (ξ ≈ 2–3, a boil rather than a per-frame fizz) → `cool` 15 s, `1 + 0.25(1−p)²` (eases into T_c so most of the phase sits near criticality) → `critical` 8 s at 1.00 → `quench` 2 s, smoothstep 1.00→0.60; it is continuous at every boundary. The first resize seeds a T = ∞ state (i.i.d. ±1), so t = 0 is the textbook quench. Magnetisation `M = Σs` is tracked incrementally and recomputed on resize.
- **Motion**: within 1 s (≈ 60 MCS at the cap) static condenses into domains 10–20 spins across; coarsening follows `ℓ ∝ t^{1/2}` (Allen & Cahn 1979; Bray 1994). Over `coarsen`, about 1200 MCS grow domains to lattice scale: bubbles shrink and vanish, walls straighten, and the phase often ends in a few domains or one stripe (≈ 1/3 of torus quenches end in stripes). In `reheat`, walls roughen and thermal clusters eat the domains as T crosses T_c. `hot` is a dim boil of small clusters. In `cool`, clusters grow fractal and slow down (critical slowing, z ≈ 2.17; Nightingale & Blöte 1996). `critical` breathes at all scales up to `t^{1/z}`. `quench` feeds straight into `coarsen`, so later cycles coarsen from critical clusters and the first from noise.
- **Resize**: if `lattice_for` returns the same lattice, only the display maps are rebuilt (cell-centre nearest-index tables px→lattice, `Vec<u16>`, allocated in `resize`) and the spins are untouched. A different lattice (bucket change, or any change below the cap) triggers `grid::resample_nearest` into a scratch grid and a swap (ADR-0012); spins stay exactly ±1 and M is recomputed. Above the cap the lattice-to-pixel ratio is not an integer (200×50: 178×91 → 200×100 px), so about every 8th pixel column and 11th pixel row repeats a lattice line; cell-centre sampling spreads the repeats evenly, and the resulting ±1 px wall jitter is accepted (an integer ratio would halve the lattice). `resize` never draws from `ctx.rng` after the first seeding. T comes from scene time, so a resize never desyncs the schedule.
- **Inks**: every pixel is `Role::Div` with `v = s·A[n_al]`, `A = [0.06, 0.12, 0.22, 0.38, 0.72]`, where `n_al ∈ 0..4` counts aligned lattice neighbours; the ten possible inks are built once per scene from A and indexed by `(s > 0)·5 + n_al`, so render does no float work. The table is convex because even at 1.25 T_c most spins still have 3–4 aligned neighbours (⟨sᵢsⱼ⟩ ≈ 0.45), so a linear map could not dim the hot phase: only n_al = 4 is bright. Domain interiors are ±0.72 (astro: orange up, blue down). Walls are ±0.22–0.38, a darker seam that outlines domains without drawing lines. Thermal or isolated spins are ±0.06–0.12, so the hot phase sinks toward the background and ordering visibly brightens the screen. The label is `Role::Text` 200.
- **Look** *(M16, GEN-R25; domain walls only; supersedes the Inks and ASCII below and the pixel display maps in Resize; no longer pinned by GEN-R22)*: black ground, no half-blocks: only the domain walls, as braille threads one dot thick. Each spin's block magnetisation `b` (the mean of its periodic 3×3 block, in steps of 2/9) is traced over the lattice by marching squares (`Dots::contour`, CORE-R105; nodes map to dots as the lattice to the old pixels) at `+0.56` (`Hot` 220, the up side of every wall) and `−0.56` (`Cold` 210, the down side) *(round 2: was ±4/9; just above the block value 5/9, so a wall's two threads sit further apart and never pass through a node)*, so each wall is a warm and a cool thread side by side, bubbles of one sign are rings inside the other's colour, and a lone thermal spin (its block stays at ±7/9) draws nothing. The torus seam is a wall like any other where the spins differ across it. Spin flips blink: a spin whose value changed during the latest update, in an ordered neighbourhood (`|b| ≥ 5/9` after the flip, a thermal flip inside a domain, not the boil), is one dot at its site for that update, `Hot` 110 if it flipped up and `Cold` 110 if down (round 2, was 140) (bound to updates, not frames: GEN-R20). After the quench the walls coarsen and straighten; near and above T_c they roughen into a froth of small rings. ASCII falls back by braille dot count. The label is unchanged.

### liesegang_rings  (Reactions, M10, M12, M15)

Each tube keeps its own tempo: the concentrated one packs bands tight (forming its first band later and deeper), the dilute one wide and slow, so the Matalon–Packter law reads as a visual polyrhythm. - **Model**: per tube, a 1-D reaction–diffusion system with nucleation-and-growth precipitation. The ingredients are Ostwald's (1897) supersaturation theory, the nucleation threshold of Keller & Rubinow (1981) and the aggregation threshold of Chopard, Luthi & Droz (1994). The reactions are A + B → C (soluble product, idealised 1:1) and C → D (immobile precipitate), as in Liesegang's 1896 experiment of AgNO₃ diffusing into K₂Cr₂O₇-loaded gelatin to precipitate silver dichromate. The equations are `∂a/∂t = a'' − k·a·b`, `∂b/∂t = b'' − k·a·b`, `∂c/∂t = D_c·c'' + k·a·b − P`, `∂d/∂t = P`, `P = λ·max(c − c_s, 0)·[nᵢ]`. The step time is stored in `t_nuc[i]`. Parameters (dimensionless) are `D_a = D_b = 1`, `D_c = 0.3`, `k = 1`, `λ = 0.05`, `c_s = 0.01`, `c_g = 0.037`, `c* = 0.04`, `b₀ = 0.05`, and five presets `a₀ ∈ {0.7, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0}` (a₀/b₀ = 14–60). One unit ≈ 0.2 mm and one time unit ≈ ℓ²/D_A ≈ 27 s (D_A ≈ 1.5·10⁻⁹ m²/s), so the displayed 100 units are 2 cm grown in about 9 h, which are real Liesegang scales. It uses a 3-point Laplacian and explicit Euler with `τ = 0.15`; positivity holds exactly since `τ(2 + k·a₀,max) = 0.75 ≤ 1` and `τ(2D_c + λ) ≤ 1`. ### molecule_viewer  (Reactions, M10, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: a slowly tumbling ball-and-stick model of a real molecule, lit like a physical model on a dark bench. Near atoms are bright and far atoms recede. The gallery runs water, methane, ethanol, benzene, caffeine, then buckminsterfullerene. Each molecule grows out of a point, turns, and collapses back into the point before the next one grows.
- **Model**: static tables in the module. Each holds element, position (Å) and bonds with an order of `Single`, `Double` or `Aromatic` (1.5). Benzene uses `Aromatic`; caffeine uses Kekulé single and double bonds. Planar molecules are stored with their plane in world xy (normal along z), so yaw takes them through face-on and edge-on views. Geometries:
  - water: r(OH) 0.9572 Å, ∠HOH 104.52° (Benedict, Gailar & Plyler 1956).
  - methane: T_d, r(CH) 1.087 Å.
  - ethanol: trans (anti H–O–C–C) conformer, r(CC) 1.512, r(CO) 1.431, r(OH) 0.971 Å (NIST CCCBDB experimental); the remaining C–H bonds and angles come from CCCBDB, or are tetrahedral with r(CH) 1.09 Å.
  - benzene: D₆h, r(CC) 1.397 Å, r(CH) 1.084 Å (Herzberg 1966).
  - caffeine C8H10N4O2: the 3-D conformer of PubChem CID 2519 (24 atoms, 25 bonds, planar purine), with double bonds C2=O, C6=O, C4=C5 and C8=N9.
  - C60: generated at build, not typed in. Each of the 30 edges of an icosahedron carries two atoms at fractions `t` and `1 − t`. The icosahedron's vertices are the cyclic permutations of (0, ±1, ±φ); its edge is 2, so it is scaled by `a/2`. With `a = 2·r₅₆ + r₆₆` and `t = r₅₆/a`, the pentagon (5:6) bonds are r₅₆ = 1.458 Å and the hexagon-fusing (6:6) bonds, which lie along the old edges, are r₆₆ = 1.401 Å (Hedberg et al. 1991, gas-phase electron diffraction). Every atom then sits 3.562 Å from the centre.

  Every table is recentred on its centre of mass (H 1.008, C 12.011, N 14.007, O 15.999 u).

  Balls and bonds:
  - Ball radius is 0.20 × the Bondi (1964) van der Waals radius (H 1.20, C 1.70, N 1.55, O 1.52 Å), which keeps ≥ 0.5 Å of visible stick on C60's 1.40–1.46 Å bonds, so the cage reads as rings of balls rather than a heap of blobs.

It skips every cell that `draw::disc` would paint for either end ball, so sticks run from ball edge to ball edge.

A sphere of radius `r_eff` seen from `5·r_eff` projects to at most `5/√24` ≈ 1.021 of its radius, and the ≈ 3 % on top covers floored bodies and rounded cells, so no orientation leaves the frame; `s(t)` is the gallery scale (Motion).   Painter's order: in every `render`, all atoms and bonds (≤ 150 items; a bond's depth is the mean of its end depths) are sorted far → near. ### radical_chain  (Reactions, M13, M16)
- **Intent**: hydrogen and oxygen in a vessel: at low pressure a few radicals wander and die on the walls, and nothing happens; step over the explosion limit and every radical makes two, the population doubles again and again, and the vessel flashes pale blue, leaving water behind.
- **Model**: radicals (pool 4000) random-walk in a vessel (a rounded rectangle); per step a radical branches (H + O₂ → OH + O, O + H₂ → OH + H: one becomes two) with probability `k_b(T)·p·g` (Arrhenius; ∝ p, a collision with O₂; `g` the local unburnt gas), terminates at the walls (on contact) or by three-body recombination with probability `k_t·p²` (H + O₂ + M: ∝ [O₂][M]); heat released per event deposits into a coarse temperature grid (≤ 100×50) that relaxes toward the wall temperature, feeding back into `k_b` (thermal runaway). Water product accumulates as a density field. Pressure `p` sweeps along a `Timeline` across the first explosion limit (at p ≈ 1 in these units); the second limit, where three-body recombination (∝ p²) overtakes branching (∝ p), lies at p = K_B0/K_T3 = 12, far above the sweep. The diffusion coefficient falls as 1/p, calibrated so the wall loss of the slowest mode equals the branching rate at p = 1 (the first limit). A radical–radical recombination term K_RR·n/capacity quenches the flash, and fuel is consumed per branching, globally and in a coarse unburnt-gas grid (4.5 branchings spend a cell; burnt gas adds 6/s of recombination), so the explosion runs as a front (a Fisher–KPP pulse) from the spark and burns out behind it. Initiation runs only in the slow phase and once the vent is below the limit (fresh gas flows back in at 1.5/s). The loop is 14.5 s: slow 2.5 s at p = 0.6 (25 seed radicals), a rise over 1.5 s to p = 2.2, burn 5 s (*M16*: was 6.5 s, which left the vessel empty once the drawn afterglow was gone), vent 5.5 s (p falls below the limit within its first third); when the burn begins (p = 2.2) an ignition spark (a Gaussian hot spot, +1.6 in T, with 30 radicals, replacing the stray ones, near the centre) starts the flame there.
- **Motion**: slow chains with fading trails (radicals wander, fork and die at the walls) for the first seconds; a flame kernel at the spark within ≈ 1 s of the burn, a front of forking threads sweeping the vessel by ≈ 4 s; the flame dies out behind it, the vessel rests dark for ≈ 1–2 s while it is vented, and fresh slow chains start (*M16*: the drawn afterglow is gone).
- **Resize**: vessel geometry in vessel units; grids regridded; pools kept.
- **Inks** (M15, ADR-0018: M11's roles in flat steps; were `Ramp(Plasma)` radicals and trails over a smooth `Ramp(Ember)` flash): a black vessel, its wall a dim dotted outline (every third wall dot, `Dim` 90). While fewer than 300 radicals are alive, each is a small plus of braille dots (a disc of radius 1 dot) in `Wave` 255 (pale blue, the subject) over its chain trail (a `Glow` stroke from its last position, kept ×0.72 per update and toned) drawn flat in `Cold` 110 where the toned trail reaches 70. The flash is the gas's heat: `2·(T − 1) + 0.9·afterglow` per heat node (the afterglow decays with τ = 1.8 s), softened by one [1 2 1] pass, Catmull-Rom sampled (CORE-R102) and toned; from toned level 48 (single reactions' heat stays black) it is a `Seq` field in four equal flat steps, levels 70, 130, 185, 235 (violet edge, red, orange, a yellow core), like M11's reaction fields, and inside the flame it wins over the trails. The water mist and the ambient glow are gone. `explosion` latches once the pool passes an eighth of its capacity or T exceeds the spark by 1.5. ASCII by level. The trails are exposed per update and the picture rasterised once per drawn frame (GEN-R20).
- **Look** *(M16, GEN-R25; supersedes the M15 Inks above)*: a branching chain tree on black, all braille dots. Each radical is a `Sun` 255 dot at the head of its thread: its last 6 drawn positions, sampled every 0.06 s (≈ 0.36 s of path), where the drawn position is its position eased with τ = 0.25 s (*round 2*: were 8 and 0.12 s) (a raw random walk is a scribble; smoothed, a thread), coloured by age: `Hot` 230 for the two newest segments, `Hot` 150 for the next three, `Accent` 90 for the tail. While more than 70 radicals live (*round 2*: was 240), only a fixed hashed share of about 70 is drawn (by identity, so a drawn thread stays drawn), so the flame stays a tree of threads, not a wash. A radical lost at the wall leaves a brief `Cold` 200 dot (radius 0.8 dots, 0.4 s) at its last point inside. The wall stays a faint dotted `Dim` 90 outline. The flash fill, the chain-trail glow and the afterglow are gone (temperature, fuel and water remain model state). No half blocks; ASCII by dot count. *Round 2 (M16)*: the vessel is never dark. Initiation (14 radicals/s at full fuel, was 6) runs through the slow phase and the rise; a fresh fill seeds 60 radicals (was 25); at the spark the stray chains are quenched (they no longer branch and die out as before) instead of removed, so the flame still grows from the spark alone; and once the flame has burnt out (a pool that peaked above 200 is below 20) the vessel is vented and refilled at once: the loop jumps to its next cycle (fresh gas at the wall temperature, 60 seed radicals), so the caption is `slow reaction` again with no dark stretch.
- **Title**: `Explosion · radicals multiply`; captions `slow reaction`, `chain branching`, `explosion` (they follow the state: `slow reaction` while p is below the limit; above it `chain branching` until the explosion latches, once the pool passes an eighth of its capacity or T exceeds the spark by 1.5, then `explosion` until the vent takes p back below the limit).

### snowflake_growth  (States of Matter, M21)
- **Intent**: a single snow crystal grows from a hexagonal seed; its growth rings show how the tips outrun the faces; each loop the humidity changes, so the next crystal is a plate, a sectored plate or a stellar dendrite.
- **Model**: Reiter's hexagonal cellular automaton (2005): each site has water `s`; a site is *receptive* if frozen (`s ≥ 1`) or touching a frozen site. Per step: receptive sites gain `γ` (vapour addition); non-receptive water diffuses (`s ← s + α/2 · (⟨s⟩₆ − s)`); a site freezes when `s ≥ 1`. Background `β` (humidity) and `γ` come per loop from a fixed table of forms. The lattice is a fixed hexagonal disc (radius capped, independent of terminal size) computed on one 1/12 wedge and mirrored, which keeps exact six-fold symmetry and costs a twelfth; growth stops at the cap or a time limit, the crystal holds, then sublimates (frozen sites vanish from the tips inwards) and the next form starts.
- **Motion**: the crystal grows outward over ~15 s; hold; sublimate; regrow.
- **Resize**: lattice unchanged; its hex-to-dot projection (aspect-correct on square braille sub-cells) and scale rebuilt.
- **Look** *(GEN-R25)*: the growth rings as 1-dot isochrones: freeze time is banded into rings (9 for the plate, 5 for the sectored plate, 4 for the dendrite, so the busier forms are not filled with lines; banded by time, so rings bunch where growth slows), coloured in flat steps `Cold` core → `Wave` → `Particle` newest rims (levels alternating 255/205), so they read like tree rings; a frozen dot is lit where a neighbouring dot is in an older ring or outside the crystal (each ring's inner edge and the outline), not every frozen site (that filled the plate with moiré); while it grows the outline is the advancing front, `Particle` 255. Dots are sampled from the hexagonal cell that holds them (round on square braille sub-cells). Vapour as very sparse `Dim` 110 stipple (fixed thresholds, density `0.06·min(s/β, 1.3)` ≤ 0.08, ∝ `s` within a loop and scaled by the form's humidity so the dry dendrite loop is as visible as the plate, fading out before the lattice edge), shown in every phase, so every frame (sublimation and rest included) has at least 8 % of its cells lit. No half-blocks.
- **Title**: `Snowflake · tips grow fastest`; captions `plate`, `sectored plate`, `stellar dendrite`.
- **Stylisation**: Reiter's model is a phenomenological CA, not molecular kinetics; the three forms' parameters were tuned from previews on this lattice, not read off Reiter's published diagram, nor mapped to measured temperature and humidity (the Nakaya diagram only in spirit).
- **Acceptance**: `SNOW-A1` the frozen set is six-fold symmetric (invariant under 60° rotation and reflection on the lattice) at every step; `SNOW-A2` a diffusion step conserves the orbit-weighted total water over all sites, receptive sites acting as sinks that receive what flows in (within 1e-12, with no water at the held boundary); `SNOW-A3` at the same frozen count the `plate` is compact (perimeter² / area < 60) and the `stellar dendrite` branched (> 400), with the sectored plate between, and the dendrite grows side branches: along the lattice lines 3 and 4 sites off each tip axis its frozen sites form at least 12 separate runs, where the plate forms one; `SNOW-A4` freeze time never decreases from the seed outward along the six tip axes, and along the six 30° face rays it falls by at most 6 steps between neighbours (a sectored plate's faces are bridged late from its faster tips); `SNOW-A5` `caption()` names the current form and loops through the table.
- **As built**: lattice radius 96, growth capped at 0.75 of it; forms at α = 1: plate β = 0.95, γ = 0.05; sectored plate β = 0.6, γ = 0.05; stellar dendrite (fern-like) β = 0.35, γ = 0.0001; growth at 5 / 20 / 160 steps per second, about 14 s each; hold 7 s, sublimate 3 s (newest sites first), rest 0.8 s.

### spinodal_decomposition  (States of Matter, M13)
- **Intent**: a well-mixed grey liquid suddenly unmixes like oil and water: fine worm-like domains appear everywhere, then coarsen, the small ones swallowed by the large ones; a second act at an off-critical mix breaks into droplets that ripen.
- **Model**: `cahn_hilliard` (shared `pub(crate)`): Oono–Puri cell-dynamics system on a capped periodic lattice (≤ 16 000 nodes): `Γ = A tanh ψ − ψ + D(⟨⟨ψ⟩⟩ − ψ)`, `ψ ← ψ − M(⟨⟨Γ⟩⟩ − Γ)`, with `⟨⟨X⟩⟩ − X = 0.3·Δ₉X` (a Δ₉-based variant of the Oono–Puri average) from the isotropic 9-point Laplacian (`grid::laplacian9`, wrap), `A = 1.3`, `D = 0.5`, `M = 1`; tanh by the `soft_clip` Padé approximant. - **Title**: `Unmixing · domains coarsen`; captions `spinodal`, `droplets ripen`.
- **Stylisation**: cell-dynamics (Oono–Puri) rather than the full Cahn–Hilliard PDE; lattice units.
- **Acceptance**: `SPIN-A1` the mean of ψ is conserved to f32 rounding: 1e-8 per step and 1e-6 over 300 steps; `SPIN-A2` the characteristic length (first zero of the correlation function) grows, with a log-log slope in [0.2, 0.45] over the act; `SPIN-A3` in the off-critical act the minority phase forms ≥ 10 separate droplets whose count then falls; `SPIN-A4` ψ stays within [−1.2, 1.2]; `SPIN-A5` regridding keeps the mean; `SPIN-A6` *(changed, M15)* on a straight interface at 30° the line has no gap (every pixel within 0.3 px of it is drawn), nothing beyond 1.5 px, `Hot` on the ψ > 0 side and `Cold` on the other (more than 5 pixels each); deep in either phase (ψ = ±ψ*) the pixel is blank; and the domains are formed 1 s after a quench at 120×40 (some |ψ| > ψ*/2); `SPIN-A7` *(new, M15)* the first frame of either act at 120×40 shows the quench as speckle: over 100 cells each of `Hot` 110 and `Cold` 110 and nothing else, under half the art cells lit.
