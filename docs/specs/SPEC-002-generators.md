# SPEC-002: Generators & registry

**Crate**: `crates/sciart-generators` · **Milestones**: M1 (registry, starfield), M4–M7 (batches), M10 (Batch E) · **Status**: M1, M4–M7 (Batches A–D) implemented; M10 (Batch E, `chemical_reaction` upgrade) in progress

Ports the Python stability contract (`docs/archive/issue-01-simulator-stability.md`, R1) to Rust
and extends it for real-time animation.

## Registry
- **GEN-R1** `registry() -> &'static [Entry]` lists every generator; `Entry { meta: &'static Meta, build: fn() -> Box<dyn Generator> }`. Names are unique, `snake_case`, and sorted.
- **GEN-R2** `find(name) -> Option<&'static Entry>` is exact-match on `meta.name`.
- **GEN-R3** Adding a generator requires exactly: one module file + one line in the registry macro. The contract suite picks it up automatically.

## Stability contract (every registered generator)
- **GEN-R4** Never panics for any `Size` with `w, h >= 1` across any sequence of `resize`, `update(dt)` (`0 <= dt <= 0.25`), and `render` calls — including resizes between any two calls.
- **GEN-R5** Dimension-dependent state is rebuilt in `resize`; `update`/`render` must tolerate the smallest size (1×1).
- **GEN-R6** Determinism: same seed + same call sequence ⇒ byte-identical `Canvas::to_lines()`.
- **GEN-R7** No wall-clock reads (`Instant`, `SystemTime`) and no global RNG (`rand::rng()`/`thread_rng`) inside generators; time comes from `dt`/`ctx.time`, randomness from `ctx.rng`. Enforced by `clippy.toml` `disallowed-methods`.
- **GEN-R8** Performance: `update(1/60)` + `render` < 5 ms at 200×50 in release (criterion bench per generator).
- **GEN-R9** Motion: over 1 simulated second at 200×50 the rendered frame changes (a scene is an animation, not a still). Verified by the contract suite.
- **GEN-R10** Unicode glyphs must have an ASCII fallback (`Glyph::new(u, a)`); ASCII mode output is pure ASCII.
- **GEN-R11** After warm-up (build, resize, ≥ 1 step and one render), `render` performs zero heap allocations, and `update(1/60)` performs zero allocations in steady state unless the scene is listed with a reason in `UPDATE_ALLOCATES` of `crates/sciart-generators/tests/alloc_free.rs` (empty today). Resizing may allocate. Verified at 200×50 and 80×24, in both glyph modes, by that test (ADR-0009).

## Per-generator spec template
Each scene gets a short section appended below when its batch starts:

```
### <name>  (category, batch)
Intent: what the viewer should see/feel.
Model: state, physics/maths, parameters.
Motion: what changes over time and at what rate.
Resize: what is rebuilt.
Inks: roles used.
Acceptance: scene-specific assertions beyond GEN-R4..R10.
```

### starfield  (Basic, M1)
- **Intent**: drifting parallax starfield with twinkling.
- **Model**: `N = clamp(area * 0.08, 1, 4000)` stars with `x, y ∈ [0, size)`, `layer ∈ {1..4}` (4 = nearest), `phase ∈ [0, 2π)`, `rate ∈ [1.5, 4]` rad/s.
- **Motion**: `x += layer * SPEED * dt` wrapping at width (parallax: nearer = faster); brightness = layer base + twinkle `sin(phase + rate * t)`.
- **Resize**: star field regenerated for the new area.
- **Inks**: `Role::Star`, level from brightness.
- **Acceptance**: (`STAR-A1`) star count follows the formula; (`STAR-A2`) nearer layers move farther than farther layers over the same dt.

## Batch A — analytic / time-driven scenes (M4)
Shared helpers: `sciart_core::draw` (CORE-R22..R27). All lengths below are in **rows**; x is stretched by `ASPECT`. `R = draw::fit_radius(size, frac)`.

### solar_system  (Astronomy, M4)
- **Intent**: the eight planets circling the Sun at physically ordered speeds, with an asteroid belt.
- **Model**: orbit radii `R(0.95) · {.14,.22,.30,.40,.60,.74,.87,1.0}`; Kepler's third law `ω(a) = (2π/12 s) · (a/a⊕)^-1.5` (Earth year = 12 s); random initial phases; 60 belt particles at `0.48–0.54` of the outer radius.
- **Motion**: every body advances `ω·dt`. **Resize**: radii recomputed, phases kept.
- **Inks**: Sun `Sun`, planets `Planet`, orbits `Dim`, belt `Particle`.
- **Acceptance**: `SOL-A1` ω strictly decreases outward and Earth's period is 12 s; `SOL-A2` every planet stays on-canvas at any time (200×50); `SOL-A3` the Sun's core sits on the center cell.

### pulsar  (Astronomy, M4)
- **Intent**: a lighthouse neutron star — two opposite radiation cones sweeping, dipole field loops, a flash each time a beam faces the viewer.
- **Model**: spin period 2.5 s, beams at `θ(t)` and `θ+π`; cone half-angle 0.12 rad; dipole field lines `r = L·sin²φ` about the beam (magnetic) axis for `L ∈ {0.35, 0.6, 0.85}·R(1)`; the viewer sits at angle `-π/2` (screen up) and a flash lasts while a beam is within 0.2 rad of it.
- **Inks**: beams `Wave` (level falls off with distance and off-axis angle), core `Hot`, field `Dim`, flash halo `Accent`.
- **Acceptance**: `PUL-A1` the beams are exactly π apart and advance by `2π/2.5` rad/s; `PUL-A2` exactly two flashes per rotation; `PUL-A3` field-line points satisfy `r = L sin²φ`.

### moon_phases  (Astronomy, M4)
- **Intent**: a lit sphere going through a full lunation, with craters and the phase name.
- **Model**: synodic cycle 30 s, phase `p ∈ [0,1)`; light direction `L = (sin 2πp, 0, −cos 2πp)`; surface normal `n = (nx, ny, √(1−nx²−ny²))`; illumination `n·L`; illuminated fraction `(1 − cos 2πp)/2`. Scene time starts at a seeded offset in `[0, 30)` s so runs open on different phases (still deterministic per seed).
- **Inks**: lit `Star` (level ∝ illumination), limb of the dark side `Dim`, label `Text`.
- **Acceptance**: `MOON-A1` new moon (p=0) has no lit point on the visible hemisphere, full moon (p=0.5) lights every interior point; `MOON-A2` names: 0 New Moon, .25 First Quarter, .5 Full Moon, .75 Last Quarter, .97 New Moon; `MOON-A3` illuminated fraction is 0, ½, 1, ½ at p = 0, ¼, ½, ¾.

### exoplanet_transit  (Astronomy, M4)
- **Intent**: a planet crossing a limb-darkened star while the light curve draws the dip.
- **Model**: star radius `Rs`, planet `rp = max(0.12 Rs, 0.5)`, impact parameter `b = 0.3 Rs`, period 10 s along `u ∈ [−2.2 Rs, 2.2 Rs]`; flux `1 − A(d; Rs, rp)/(π Rs²)` with `A` the exact circle–circle overlap area; limb darkening `I(μ) = 1 − 0.6(1 − μ)` for shading only. The light-curve panel (right 38 % of the screen, only if ≥ 12×6 cells) plots one full period with a marker at the current phase.
- **Inks**: star `Sun`, planet `Planet` (off-disk) / blank silhouette (on-disk), curve `Accent`, axes `Structure`.
- **Acceptance**: `TRAN-A1` flux = 1 when the disks don't touch; `TRAN-A2` flux = `1 − (rp/Rs)²` when the planet is fully inside; `TRAN-A3` overlap is symmetric in its radii and non-increasing in `d`.

### satellite_orbit  (Space Exploration, M4)
- **Intent**: a rotating Earth (day/night, oceans, continents, ice caps) with satellites on inclined orbits passing in front of and behind it.
- **Model**: Earth radius `Re = R(0.45)`, spin period 20 s, procedural continents from seeded sinusoids on (lon, lat); 5 satellites with `a ∈ [1.25, 2.2]·Re`, inclination `∈ [0.2, 1.2]` rad from the screen plane (radii and inclinations drawn stratified so orbits spread out), node angle random, `ω = (2π/6 s)·(a/Re)^-1.5`. 3-D position `(a cosθ, a sinθ cos i, a sinθ sin i)` rotated by the node angle in the screen plane; `z < 0` is behind.
- **Inks**: ocean `Cold`, land `Particle`, ice `Text`, night side dimmed, satellites `Accent`, orbit paths `Dim`; extras: sun-side atmosphere glow `Cold`, sparse background stars `Dim`.
- **Acceptance**: `SAT-A1` a satellite is hidden iff `z < 0` and its screen distance from Earth's center is `< Re`; `SAT-A2` ω decreases with `a`; `SAT-A3` `|position| = a`.

### doppler_effect  (Physics & Chemistry, M4)
- **Intent**: a moving source compressing wavefronts ahead (blueshift) and stretching them behind (redshift).
- **Model**: wave speed `c = 10` rows/s, source speed `v = 0.5c` left→right (wraps with margin), one wavefront every `T = 0.35 s`, each front a circle of radius `c·(t − t₀)` around its emission point; fronts are dropped once larger than the screen diagonal.
- **Inks**: each front point is coloured by its direction θ from its own emission point — `Cold` (blueshifted, `cos θ > 0`) else `Hot`; on the axis this is exactly "ahead of the source ⇒ `Cold`", and it stays correct off-axis and after the source wraps; source `Accent`.
- **Acceptance**: `DOP-A1` in steady state the spacing of fronts along the axis is `(c − v)T` ahead and `(c + v)T` behind; `DOP-A2` the number of live fronts is bounded by `diag/(cT) + 2`.

### wave_interference  (Physics & Chemistry, M4)
- **Intent**: two coherent point sources whose ripples add up into bright and dark bands.
- **Model**: sources at `(0.25 w, h/2 ± s/2)`, `s = 0.35 h` rows; wavelength `λ = max(3, h/6)` rows; `ω = 3` rad/s; amplitude `a = sin(k d₁ − ωt) + sin(k d₂ − ωt)`, glyph from `(a + 2)/4`.
- **Inks**: `Wave`, level ∝ `(a + 2)/4`; sources `Accent`; title `Text` (only if `h > 8`).
- **Acceptance**: `WAVE-A1` on the perpendicular bisector the amplitude is `2 sin(kd − ωt)`; `WAVE-A2` where the path difference is `λ/2` the amplitude is ≈ 0 at all times.

### cosmic_microwave_background  (Astronomy, M4)
- **Intent**: the Planck-style oval sky map: smooth hot/cold anisotropies that slowly shimmer (not per-frame noise).
- **Model**: an isotropic Gaussian random field by spectral synthesis, `f = (1/N) Σ sin(kᵢ·(x/ASPECT, y) + φᵢ + ωᵢ t)` with N = 96 equal-power modes; `|kᵢ|` stratified-sampled so their density in `ln ℓ` follows `D_ℓ·B_ℓ²` — `D_ℓ` an approximate Planck 2018 ΛCDM TT spectrum (Sachs–Wolfe plateau, first acoustic peak at ℓ = 220 ≈ 5× plateau, trough, 2nd/3rd peaks, damping), `B_ℓ² = exp(−(ℓ/400)²)` a "cell beam", ℓ ∈ [10, 760] — mapped to the screen as `|k| = (2π/6)·ℓ/220` rad/row (first peak = 6-row wavelength); directions from a jittered golden-ratio sequence over [0, π) along the ℓ-sorted modes; phases uniform; `|ωᵢ| ∈ [0.08, 0.35]` rad/s with random sign (slow shimmer, no drift); display saturates at `|f| = 2.7σ`, `σ = 1/√(2N)` (Planck colour bar ±300 μK ≈ ±2.7 σ_CMB); drawn only inside an ellipse covering 94 % of the screen (Mollweide-like); caption `CMB  T=2.725K  dT~1e-5` on the last row when `h > 6`.
- **Inks**: `f > 0` ⇒ `Hot`, else `Cold`, level ∝ `min(|f|/2.7σ, 1)`.
- **Acceptance**: `CMB-A1` `|f| ≤ 1`; `CMB-A2` smooth — horizontally adjacent cells differ by at most `Σ|kₓᵢ| / (ASPECT·N)`, and adjacent-cell correlation is ≥ 0.9 horizontally and ≥ 0.7 vertically; `CMB-A3` a 200×50 frame contains both hot and cold cells; `CMB-A4` isotropic, not stripy — the direction tensor `Σ k̂k̂ᵀ` has anisotropy ≤ 0.05 and the two-point correlation at equal aspect-corrected lags agrees across directions within 0.1 (mode ensemble at 0°/90°/±45°, 1–6 rows; and sampled on a 200×50 screen, horizontal vs vertical, 1–3 rows); `CMB-A5` CMB-like spectrum — `D_ℓ` peaks at ℓ ∈ [200, 240] with `D_220/D_10 ∈ [4.5, 6]`, a trough then a second peak, and damping; the most populated Δln ℓ = 0.5 band of modes is centred at ℓ ∈ [140, 300].

### gravitational_lensing  (Astronomy, M4)
- **Intent**: background stars drifting behind a massive lens, each seen as two images, and a galaxy that periodically lines up into an Einstein ring.
- **Model**: point lens with Einstein radius `θE = R(0.35)`; lens equation `β = θ − θE² θ/|θ|²`; for a source at `β` the images are `θ± = β̂ (|β| ± √(|β|² + 4θE²))/2`; 160 seeded stars drifting at 0.6 rows/s (wrapping); an extended source (radius 1.5 rows) crossing near the lens every 12 s, rendered by inverse ray-shooting within `2.5 θE`. Images with `|μ| < 0.05` are not drawn (in practice only far minor images, which otherwise clutter the Einstein disc; real ones are ≈ `u⁻⁴` faint).
- **Inks**: images `Star` (level by magnification), ring/arcs `Accent`, lens `Structure`.
- **Acceptance**: `LENS-A1` both images map back to `β` through the lens equation; `LENS-A2` as `β → 0` both images approach radius `θE`; `LENS-A3` for `|β| ≫ θE` the primary image ≈ `β` and the secondary → 0.

### constellation_map  (Astronomy, M4)
- **Intent**: a star chart that traces one constellation at a time, then names it, over a twinkling sky.
- **Model**: 6 constellations (Orion, Ursa Major, Cassiopeia, Cygnus, Crux, Scorpius) as normalized star coordinates + edges, fitted to 80 % of the screen preserving shape; each is shown for 12 s: lines trace in over the first 4 s, the name appears after tracing; background stars `0.02·area` twinkle.
- **Data**: star positions from real J2000 RA/Dec and visual magnitudes, gnomonic projection about the figure centre (east left, north up); only stars on the conventional stick figure are included (e.g. ε Cru omitted).
- **Inks**: constellation stars `Star`, lines `Structure`, tracing pen tip `Accent`, name `Text`, background `Dim`.
- **Acceptance**: `CON-A1` every edge references valid stars and every star is on at least one edge; `CON-A2` tracing progress 0 draws no segment, 1 draws all, and drawn length is monotone in progress; `CON-A3` the constellation index advances every 12 s and wraps.

## Batch B — particle systems (M5)
Shared helpers: `sciart_core::particles` (CORE-R28..R32). Pools are sized in `resize` from the area and are
bounded (GEN-R8); particles are respawned rather than reallocated. Positions are in **rows** unless stated
(x on screen = `cx + x·ASPECT`). Physics uses `dt` from `update` only.

### black_hole  (Astronomy, M5)
- **Intent**: an Interstellar-style black hole: a dark shadow, a thin bright photon ring, and a tilted accretion disk whose far side is lensed up over the shadow; the approaching side glows brighter.
- **Model**: shadow radius `rs = R(0.14)`; ISCO `r_in = 2.2 rs`; disk out to `R(0.95)`; inclination 78° (disk ellipse flattening `cos i`); particles on circular orbits `ω(r) = kepler_omega(gm, r)` with the ISCO period fixed at 2.5 s; relativistic beaming for shading only: level ∝ `(1 + 0.55·β_los)³` with `β_los` the line-of-sight velocity fraction. Particles slowly drift inward (`dr/dt = −0.02 r`); inside `r_in` they plunge and are respawned at the outer edge. The far half of the disk is drawn at its weak-field lensed image positions (`θE² = 2·R_s·depth`): the bright primary is lifted into an arc over the shadow, a faint secondary appears below; there is no separate unlensed copy. Pool `clamp(area/6, 24, 1600)`, initially uniform in `ln r` (the steady state of the drift).
- **Inks**: disk `Hot` (level from radius and beaming), photon ring `Accent`, lensed arc `Hot` dimmer, shadow = blank.
- **Acceptance**: `BH-A1` ω strictly decreases with r and the ISCO period is 2.5 s; `BH-A2` no disk particle is drawn inside the shadow when it is behind the hole (far side, projected distance < rs); `BH-A3` the approaching half is brighter on average than the receding half; `BH-A4` the pool size stays constant after warm-up (respawn, no growth).

### galaxy_collision  (Astronomy, M5)
- **Intent**: two spiral galaxies passing each other and pulling out tidal tails and bridges (Toomre & Toomre 1972).
- **Model**: restricted three-body: two cores (masses `M1 = 1`, `M2 = 0.8`) on a bound, eccentric, softened (`ε = 0.3` of disk radius) orbit integrated with kick-drift-kick leapfrog at `dt` sub-stepped to ≤ 1/120 s; massless test stars (`≈ 0.04·area`, max 900, split ∝ mass) start on circular orbits `v = √(G M r² / (r² + ε²)^{3/2})` in disks of radius `Rd`, one prograde and one inclined/retrograde, projected to the screen. Cores start at apocentre of an orbit with `a = 3.5 Rd`, `e = 0.63` (Kepler period mapped to 24 s). A cycle is 40 s (approach, pericentre ≈ 12 s, tails, second close passage ≈ 36 s — a conservative restricted three-body model cannot merge) then restarts from a re-seeded configuration (galaxy 2 spin 40–140°); the camera eases to frame both cores.
- **Inks**: galaxy 1 stars `Cold`, galaxy 2 `Hot`, cores `Accent`; star level by local density or distance to core.
- **Acceptance**: `GAL-A1` the core pair conserves energy within 1 % over a full cycle (leapfrog); `GAL-A2` initial star speeds equal the softened circular speed; `GAL-A3` after pericentre some stars are farther than `2·Rd` from both cores (tails form); `GAL-A4` the cycle restarts at 40 s.

### stellar_nursery  (Astronomy, M5)
- **Intent**: a glowing emission nebula with dark dust pillars where gas streams onto protostars that ignite, flare bipolar jets, then shine.
- **Model**: nebula brightness = slowly drifting domain-warped sum of sines (≤ 6 terms, drawn only where above a threshold); 2–3 dust pillars as tapered vertical shapes that occlude; 3–5 protostars at seeded positions; a gas-particle pool (`≈ 0.03·area`, max 600) spawned in the nebula, accelerating toward the nearest protostar (`a = gm / (d² + ε²)`), absorbed within 0.8 rows, adding mass; a protostar ignites when mass ≥ `M_ign` (brightness ramps over 1 s) and then emits short-lived jet particles along a seeded axis; after 25 s of shining it fades (2.5 s) and is replaced by a new protostar elsewhere. Gas is stored in polar coordinates about its target: only the radial motion follows the softened gravity, the swirl is kinematic (pure rotation), which keeps NUR-A1 exact; new gas targets the nearest *accreting* protostar. Ignited stars ionise a growing bubble of nebula; pillar rims glow `Hot` as ionisation fronts; jets are pulsed (Herbig–Haro knots); jet pool `clamp(area/60, 4, 240)`.
- **Inks**: nebula `Wave`/`Hot` blend by level, pillars `Structure` (dark), gas `Particle`, protostars `Star`, jets `Accent`.
- **Acceptance**: `NUR-A1` a gas particle's distance to its target protostar is non-increasing until absorption (for dt ≤ 1/60); `NUR-A2` protostar mass is non-decreasing while it accretes and ignition happens exactly when mass first reaches `M_ign`; `NUR-A3` particle pools never exceed capacity.

### brownian_motion  (Physics & Chemistry, M5)
- **Intent**: a large pollen grain jittering among fast solvent molecules, leaving a fading random-walk trail, with a live mean-squared-displacement readout.
- **Model**: grain obeys the Langevin/Ornstein–Uhlenbeck equation `dv = −γ v dt + σ √dt ξ` (γ = 2 s⁻¹, σ chosen so the diffusion constant `D = σ²/(2γ²)` gives ~3 rows/√s), exact OU update for the velocity; reflecting walls; trail of the last 180 positions (`Trail`); `≈ 0.02·area` (max 400) visible solvent molecules moving ballistically with Maxwell–Boltzmann velocities and reflecting walls, occasionally flashing where they "hit" the grain; readout `D^ (MSD@2s) = …   D = … rows^2/s   t = …s` (ASCII): `D̂` from the time-averaged MSD at a 2 s lag with the exact OU inertial correction (a single grain's `r²/4t` from the start never converges); on narrow terminals trailing parts (t, then the true D) are dropped, never clipped, and the readout is hidden when even `D^ (MSD@2s) = …` does not fit. The joint position–velocity OU transition is sampled exactly; walls are exact specular reflections via folding (method of images).
- **Inks**: grain `Accent`, trail `Structure` fading by age, molecules `Particle`, readout `Text`.
- **Acceptance**: `BRO-A1` the stationary velocity variance per axis converges to `σ²/(2γ)` (seeded statistical test, 5 %); `BRO-A2` the ensemble MSD at time t is `4Dt` within 10 % for t ≫ 1/γ (seeded, many walkers); `BRO-A3` the grain and molecules always stay inside the box; the trail length is ≤ 180.

### molecular_dynamics  (Physics & Chemistry, M5)
- **Intent**: a Lennard-Jones fluid in a box that is slowly heated and cooled so it melts into a gas and condenses back into droplets/crystals.
- **Model**: reduced LJ units, `U(r) = 4(r⁻¹² − r⁻⁶)` shifted at cutoff 2.5, in **2-D**; constant reduced density ρ = 0.25, inside the 2-D LJ vapour–liquid dome (r_c = 2.5 truncated-shifted: T_c ≈ 0.46, ρ_c ≈ 0.33–0.35, triple point ≈ 0.40); per-terminal layout: rows per σ `s = clamp(√(ρ·A₁/240), 1.1, 2.5)` and `N = clamp(round(ρ·A₁/s²), 8, 240)` with `A₁` the interior area in σ² at one row per σ (neighbouring crystal rows stay ≥ 1 row apart; 80×24 gets N ≈ 159; above ≈ 250×50 the scale clamps and ρ falls); one simulated second = 6 τ; velocity-Verlet with `dt_md ≤ 0.004` sub-steps per frame; cell list for O(N) forces; reflecting walls; atoms start on a triangular crystal; below `r = 0.8` the potential continues linearly so forces stay finite; a 30 s temperature cycle `T = 0.25 + 0.75·u²`, `u = (1 − cos 2πt/30)/2` (below T_c for about half the cycle, ≈ 2 T_c at the peak), by gentle velocity rescaling (Berendsen, τ = 0.5 s).
- **Inks**: atoms `Cold`→`Hot` by kinetic energy; box `Structure`; readout `T = …` `Text`.
- **Acceptance**: `MD-A1` the force equals `−dU/dr` (finite-difference check) and vanishes beyond the cutoff; `MD-A2` with the thermostat off, total energy drifts < 1 % over 5 simulated seconds; `MD-A3` atoms always stay inside the box; `MD-A4` the thermostat moves temperature toward its target; `MD-A5` the layout keeps ρ within 5 % of 0.25 wherever `s` is not clamped, and at 80×24 (seeded) the mean coordination within 1.5 σ in the cold phase (28–33 s) is > 2× that around the peak (14–19 s) and > 3.5.

### particle_accelerator  (Physics & Chemistry, M5)
- **Intent**: a collider ring where two counter-rotating bunches accelerate, meet at the detector, and burst into a shower of curling tracks.
- **Model**: ring = ellipse `R(0.8)`; bunches at angles `−π/2 + φ(t)` and `−π/2 − φ(t)` (mirror images about the vertical axis), so they meet only when `φ ≡ 0` (the detector, top) or `φ ≡ π` (a second, quieter interaction point, bottom); `dφ/dt` ramps linearly from 0.5 to 6 rad/s over 10 s; after the ramp the bunches collide at the detector each time they meet; each collision spawns 20–40 shower tracks with random momentum `p`, charge ±1, moving in a uniform magnetic field so each follows a circle of radius `r = p/B` (curling clockwise for +, counter-clockwise for −), losing momentum as `dp/dt = −0.8 p` (closed-form inward spiral, exact for any dt; non-relativistic: speed = p, radius = p/B) and fading after ≤ 2 s. Pool capped at 128. Collisions flash; the bottom interaction point only glints. Cycle 20 s; the first shower comes after the 10 s ramp.
- **Inks**: ring `Structure`, bunches `Accent` with short trails, tracks `Hot` (+) / `Cold` (−), detector `Text` label.
- **Acceptance**: `ACC-A1` the two bunches are always mirror images about the vertical axis and meet only at the two interaction points; `ACC-A2` a shower track's curvature radius equals `p/B` and its turning sign equals its charge; `ACC-A3` showers only spawn at the detector after the ramp and the pool never exceeds capacity.

### rocket_launch  (Space Exploration, M5)
- **Intent**: a launch from countdown to orbit: vapour venting, ignition, a rising rocket with a flickering flame, billowing exhaust that cools into smoke, and stage separation.
- **Model**: 22 s cycle — countdown 4 s (vapour puffs, "T−3 … 1" text), ignition 1 s (hold-down, exhaust spreads along the pad), flight: first stage burns 8 s with thrust `F`, exhaust speed `ve`, mass flow `ṁ = F/ve`, `a = F/m − g`; stage separation (the spent stage tumbles and falls away), second stage burns until the rocket exits the top; the view is fixed on the pad until altitude exceeds 60 % of the height, then the camera follows (ground scrolls away). Exhaust particles (`≈ 0.08·area`, max 1500) emitted backward with spread, slowed by drag, rising buoyantly as they cool from `Hot` to grey smoke. Rocket drawn from a small ASCII sprite (≥ 5 rows tall at h ≥ 20, a 1-cell glyph when tiny). Units are scaled for the screen (toy vehicle, `ve` ≈ 110–165 m/s; rows-per-metre chosen so 60 % height is reached 5 s after liftoff); the rocket equation is exact with `t` and `m0` measured from liftoff. Countdown vapour and the separation puff use a separate vapour pool, so "exhaust only while burning" holds strictly. Captions `LIFTOFF`, `STAGE SEPARATION`, `STAGE 2 IGNITION` and a telemetry line (T+, ALT, VEL) are shown.
- **Inks**: rocket body `Text`, flame `Hot`/`Accent`, smoke `Dim`, ground/tower `Structure`, countdown `Text`.
- **Acceptance**: `ROC-A1` during first-stage burn the velocity matches the rocket equation `v = ve ln(m0/m) − g t` within 1 %; `ROC-A2` exhaust spawns only while an engine burns, and the pool never exceeds capacity; `ROC-A3` altitude is non-decreasing from liftoff until stage separation and the cycle restarts at 22 s.

## Batch C — grids, automata & stochastic processes (M6)
Shared helpers: `sciart_core::grid` (CORE-R33..R36) plus `particles` and `draw`. Grids are rebuilt (or `resize`d) in `resize`.

### crystal_growth  (Physics & Chemistry, M6)
- **Intent**: a frost/mineral dendrite growing from a seed by diffusion-limited aggregation (Witten–Sander), glowing at its growing tips, then melting away and regrowing.

### action_potential  (Biology, M10)
- **Intent**: a nerve impulse made visible. A stylised neuron rests in the dark: soma, branching dendrites, a long unmyelinated axon and a fan of synaptic terminals, all drawn as dim membrane outlines. A stimulus fires the soma, and a bright pulse of depolarisation runs down the axon with a cool after-hyperpolarisation wake behind it. It lights the terminals, which release a small puff of transmitter. An oscilloscope inset draws the classic spike waveform at the moment the pulse passes the recording electrode. A looping protocol shows four things in turn: a single spike, a train that slows as it runs into its own refractory wake, two spikes annihilating in collision, and the all-or-none threshold.
- **Model**: Hodgkin & Huxley (1952) squid giant axon, in the modern rest = −65 mV convention (Dayan & Abbott 2001).
  - **Cable equation**: `C_m ∂V/∂t = (a / 2R_i) ∂²V/∂x² − ḡ_Na m³h (V − E_Na) − ḡ_K n⁴ (V − E_K) − ḡ_L (V − E_L) + I_stim`, with `C_m = 1 μF/cm²`, `ḡ_Na = 120`, `ḡ_K = 36`, `ḡ_L = 0.3 mS/cm²`, `E_Na = +50`, `E_K = −77` and `E_L = −54.387 mV`.
  - **Rates** (ms⁻¹, V in mV): `α_m = 0.1(V + 40)/(1 − e^{−(V+40)/10})`, `β_m = 4 e^{−(V+65)/18}`, `α_h = 0.07 e^{−(V+65)/20}`, `β_h = 1/(1 + e^{−(V+35)/10})`, `α_n = 0.01(V + 55)/(1 − e^{−(V+55)/10})`, `β_n = 0.125 e^{−(V+65)/80}`. All are scaled by `φ = 3^{(T − 6.3)/10}` with `T = 6.3 °C`, so `φ = 1`. The removable singularities at −40 and −55 mV go through an `x/(1 − e^{−x})` helper with a series branch.
  - **Geometry**: axon radius `a = 238 μm` and `R_i = 35.4 Ω·cm` (HH 1952) give `D = a/(2 R_i C_m) = 0.336 cm²/ms`. The cable is `L = 10 cm` in `N = 200` compartments (`Δx = 0.5 mm`, ≈ 1/14 of the 7 mm resting length constant), with sealed (no-flux) ends.
  - **Sub-step (10 μs)**: first a Rush–Larsen (1978) exponential update of m, h and n, which is exact for frozen V and stays in `[0, 1]`. Then backward-Euler V with the ionic conductances frozen (Hines 1984), solved as a tridiagonal M-matrix by the Thomas algorithm into preallocated scratch. This is unconditionally stable; explicit Euler would need a step ≤ 3.7 μs and RK4 ≤ 5 μs. Velocity error is 0.5 % against a converged fine-grid run.
  - **Clock**: it counts integer sub-steps (`h = 0.01 ms`, through an accumulator with 1e-9 slack), so how frames chop time cannot change results. Slow motion is 4 ms of axon time per simulated second (250×).
  - **Measured physiology** (converged): propagated peak +37.9 mV; after-hyperpolarisation minimum −75.9 mV; V > −40 mV for 2.3 ms at a point (a ≈ 2.9 cm footprint); conduction velocity 12.3 m/s at 6.3 °C and 18.7 m/s at 18.5 °C (HH computed 18.8 m/s and measured 21.2 m/s); threshold ≈ 90 μA/cm² for a 0.3 ms pulse over the first 0.25 cm.
  - **Protocol**: a looping `Timeline` of 18 s (= 72 ms). `single` 3 s: a soma pulse at 0.2 s. `train` 8 s: pulses at 3.0, 5.5 and 8.0 s, 10 ms apart. `collision` 4.5 s: soma and terminal end stimulated together at 13.0 s, the neurophysiologist's collision test. `threshold` 2.5 s: a 0.5× pulse at 16.0 s. Strong pulses are 270 μA/cm² (3× threshold) for 0.3 ms over the first (or last) 0.25 cm, and the weak pulse is 45 μA/cm².
  - **Refractory timing**: every strong pulse starts ≥ 9.5 ms after the last spike passed its site. That is beyond the ≈ 6–8 ms refractory limit, so every strong pulse fires, but inside the relative refractory period, so each train spike runs slower than the last (measured 10.9, 10.1 and 9.8 m/s, against 12.3 isolated). The model state runs continuously across loops, and the second loop repeats the first to within 0.01 s.
  - **Weak pulse**: it lands on the after-hyperpolarisation left by the collision and lifts the soma to ≈ −61 mV (≈ −57 mV from rest). It spreads passively ≈ 1 cm and fades.
  - **Stimulus markers**: each pulse lights a stimulus marker for 0.25 s, at a level proportional to its amplitude, so the weak kick visibly happens. The marker is `>` left of the soma, and `<` just before the terminals for the far-end pulse.
  - **Stylisation**: the soma, dendrites and terminal arbor are drawn, not simulated; the real giant axon is a syncytium grown from the stellate ganglion. The soma shows V of compartment 0, and the terminal arbor fans out the last 0.8 cm of the cable.
  - **Vesicles**: at each 0 mV up-crossing at the cable end, every bouton emits 4 vesicle dots (`ParticlePool`, capacity 32). They drift 1–2 cells across the cleft and fade within 0.4 s.
- **Motion**: at t = 0 the neuron rests, showing only its outline. The stimulus marker and soma flash at 0.2 s. By 1 s the pulse is a third of the way along the axon (GEN-R9). It reaches the terminals at ≈ 2.3 s, with a bouton flash and a vesicle puff. The trace scrolls continuously (a 20 ms = 5 s window) and draws each spike as the pulse passes the electrode (compartment 100, 5 cm, ≈ 1.1 s after the stimulus). ### aurora  (Astronomy, M10)
- **Intent**: a subauroral night, looking north over a still lake. A quiet arc low in the north brightens suddenly and breaks into folding, rayed curtains that climb and fill the sky, green below and a diffuse red fringe above, with a pink hem along the lower border at the substorm's peak. It then fades into softly pulsating patches. The focal point is the lower border of the brightest curtain, which is the sharpest and brightest edge in the frame. A black spruce ridge and its rippling reflection anchor the bottom, and the sparse stars never compete. At 200×50 the shore is row 40. At scene start the arch's crest stands ≈ 18° up (≈ 12 rows above the shore) with both ends sagging into the horizon, and at the expansion peak the curtains fill rows ≈ 2–38. At 80×24 the shore is row 19 and the crest starts ≈ 6 rows above it.
- **Model**: *View*: every object lives in sky angles (azimuth α, east positive; elevation e) or in world km, projected equirectangularly onto a full-screen `Pixels` lattice (P = 2h pixel rows). The horizon row is `y_h = round(0.80·P)` with a lake below it, or `round(0.92·P)` with no lake when h < 14. The scale is `f = max(y_h/62°, W/π)` px/rad, with `x = W/2 + f·α` and `y = y_h − f·e`, so the frame is ≈ 155° × 62° at 200×50 and ≈ 130° × 62° at 80×24. *Curtains*: 1–3 thin vertical sheets aligned east–west. Sheet c lies at northward ground distance `D_c(s,t) = D0_c(t) + ξ_c(s,t)` of the along-arc coordinate s (east positive). It is sampled at M = 480 fixed points `s_j = 300 km·tan α_j`, with α_j uniform in ±85°. The displacement is `ξ = Σ_{k=1..4} w_kj·a_k sin(κ_k s + θ_k)` with λ = 900/240/70/18 km (bend/surge/fold/curl), `a_1 = 40 km` and `a_k = F(t)·σ_k/κ_k` for slope amplitudes σ = 0.5/1.3/0.8. The weight `w_kj = clamp(λ_k/(2Δs_j) − 1, 0, 1)` fades a mode out wherever it has fewer than 4 samples per wavelength, so flank folds never alias. Phases integrate as `θ_k −= κ_k c_k dt`, so mode k drifts at velocity c_k, with c = +0.3, −v_s(t), +1.2, −0.8 km/s (negative is westward; v_s is the westward-travelling surge). Speed changes therefore never make the pattern jump. Each sample lies at ground distance `d = hypot(s, D)` and azimuth `α = atan2(s, D)`. An altitude z maps to `e = atan((z − d²/2R⊕)/d)` (flat earth plus curvature drop, R⊕ = 6371 km). A straight east–west arc is therefore an arch that sags into both horizons. The sheet doubles back on screen wherever `ξ' > cot|α|`; for the fold band's slope of 1.3 that happens only beyond |α| ≈ 38°, which is how real folds read in the oblique sky. *Precipitation*: `Φ = A_c(t)·B·((1 − r) + r·ρ/ρ̄)`. B is a westward-drifting bulge `1 + b(t)·exp(−((α − α_b)/w_b)²)`. The ray field is `ρ = ⅓Σ_m max(0, cos(κ_m(s − v_m t) + φ_m))⁶` with λ_m = 23/37/61 km and `v_m = v_r(t)·{1, −0.6, 0.8}`. Its mean is ρ̄ = 5/32, so switching rays on moves precipitation into the rays without changing its average; the ray-averaged flux is `Φ̄ = A·B`. The mean energy E0(t) sets the lower border `z_b = 131 − 25·log10(E0/1 keV)` km, clamped to [85, 150]. This is a fit to the peak energy-deposition altitudes of Rees (1963). *Emission* at altitude z has three components. Green OI 557.7 nm is `G = Φ·S·g(z − z_b)` with `g(u) = (1 − e^{−(u+2)/2})·e^{−u/H}` for u ≥ −2 km (else 0). It peaks ≈ 3 km above z_b, and `H = 22 km·(1 + 0.8 r ρ)` so that rays reach higher. Green is prompt (the O(¹S) lifetime is 0.74 s). Red OI 630.0 nm is `R = R_j·S·0.4·exp(1 − y − e^{−y})/(1 + e^{−(z−190)/12})` with y = (z − 240)/50 km: a Chapman production layer times collisional quenching of O(¹D) below ~200 km. The red source relaxes exactly as `R_j ← Φ + (R_j − Φ)·e^{−dt/τ_R}` with τ_R = 3 s. That is the 110 s radiative lifetime compressed only enough to keep the lag visible (on the 144× substorm clock it would be 0.76 s), so the red is smooth, rayless and lingers. The pink N₂ 1PG hem marks type-B aurora, where fast electrons reach heights at which O(¹S) is quenched. It is `P = Φ·S·clamp((E0 − 8)/12, 0, 1)` for z_b − 4 ≤ z ≤ z_b + 1 km, and it always includes the layer's lowest lit pixel, so it never vanishes at small sizes. The slant factor `S = min(1/|n̂·l̂|, 6)` uses the sheet normal n ∝ (−ξ', 1, 0) (east, north, up) and the line of sight l̂ to the sample's lower border. It is the path length through a thin sheet, so folds seen edge-on become bright caustic "rays". *Simplifications*: sheets and rays are vertical (field-aligned; valid for the northern sky seen from a subauroral site) and arcs are strictly east–west. *Raster*: consecutive samples are splatted as column spans into per-column layers {d, z_b, H, G/R/P amplitudes}, at most 4 per column (a fifth merges into the nearest d). A fold that doubles back gives a column several layers, and they add. Pixel row y samples each layer at `z = d·tan e_y + d²/2R⊕`. This uses a per-resize tan e row table and 1-km altitude LUTs: green in u with H quantised to 16 steps, and red in z. The sum is multiplied by the extinction `exp(−0.12(1/sin max(e, 2°) − 1))` and tone-mapped as `1 − e^{−E/E_sat}` through a LUT. A pixel is lit when its tone exceeds a 4×4 Bayer threshold scaled to ≈ 0.06. Its role is red rather than green when R/(G+R) exceeds the same Bayer value, which gives a stippled blend with no hard band. *Setting*: a far-shore ridge (3-octave value noise in α, 0.8–4° high) is topped by seeded spruce spires (1.5–4° apart, 1.2–3° tall, half-width 0.28× height). Silhouette pixels are never lit. A faint 3–4 px `Dim` skyglow band above the ridge (Bayer-faded) makes the silhouette read. The lake mirrors the sky about y_h at 0.45× level. Its ripple is `δx = round(0.08 d·sin(0.9d − 1.7t + 0.13x))`, where d is the number of pixel rows below the shore, so the wavefronts travel toward the viewer. Pixels drop out where that sine exceeds 0.85. Stars come from a seeded 1200-star catalogue in (α, e) with magnitudes. They are shown brightest-first until 2 % of sky cells hold one, so the limiting magnitude adapts to resolution. They twinkle more toward the horizon and are drawn only in cells whose two pixels are empty.

### belousov_zhabotinsky  (Chemistry, M10)
- **Intent**: a Belousov–Zhabotinsky dish seen from above. Thin, pale oxidation fronts sweep a dark medium. Each trails a tail that cools through orange, red and violet to black as the medium recovers. Broken wavefronts curl at their free ends into spirals: counter-rotating pairs, or a single spiral where one end is pinned to the dish wall. A pacemaker nucleus pulses slower target rings, which the spiral wave trains gradually push back. Every 63 s a light quench, as in the photosensitive Ru-catalysed BZ (Kuhnert, Agladze & Krinsky 1989), puts every wave out, and fresh broken fronts restart the dish. `chemical_reaction` shows Gray–Scott blobs that grow in place and `liesegang_rings` shows static bands; here every feature travels and the resting medium is black.
- **Model**: Barkley (1991) excitable medium on a no-flux (Neumann) rectangle: `∂u/∂t = ∇²u + ε⁻¹ u(1 − u)(u − u_th)`, `u_th = (v + b)/a`, `∂v/∂t = u − v`, with `a = 0.75`, `b = 0.06`, `ε = 0.02` (rigidly rotating spirals; the EZ-Spiral default). `u` stands for the autocatalyst HBrO₂. `v` stands for the oxidised catalyst (ferriin / Ru(bpy)₃³⁺), whose reduction releases the Br⁻ inhibitor; this is the two-variable Oregonator picture. The node spacing is fixed at `h = 0.5` space units (su), so the pattern scale is fixed in nodes. Each sub-step `τ = 0.0125` time units (tu) is Lie-split into three stages. (1) Explicit diffusion, using the isotropic 9-point Laplacian (Patra–Karttunen, as in `chemical_reaction`) with mirrored ghost nodes. The stencil sums mirror pairs first, `(W+E)`, `(N+S)`, `(NW+NE)` and `(SW+SE)`, so the update is bitwise left–right symmetric. (2) Barkley's semi-implicit reaction: `u ← u / (1 − (τ/ε)(1 − u)(u − u_th))` if `u < u_th`, else `u ← u + (τ/ε) u(1 − u)(u − u_th)`. (3) `v ← v + τ(u − v)`. Because `(10/3)·τ/h² = 0.17 ≤ 1`, `τ/ε = 0.625 ≤ 1` and `τ ≤ 1`, every stage is a convex combination or a shrinking factor. So `u, v ∈ [0, 1]` holds exactly for any `b ≥ 0`, with no clamps. Measured on this exact scheme (NumPy prototype):
  - planar pulse speed `c = 3.58` su/tu, which is 0.85 `c_N`, where `c_N = √(2/ε)(½ − b/a) = 4.2` is the Nagumo limit at `v = 0`; the continuum value at `h = 0.1` is ≈ 3.9, and the O(ε) recovery effect accounts for the rest of the gap;
  - spiral period 3.95–4.2 tu;
  - spiral wavelength λ ≈ 25 nodes;
  - excited plateau ≈ 8 nodes long.

  Pacing is `RATE = 1.5` tu per simulated second, exactly 2 sub-steps per 1/60 s, through an accumulator that handles any `dt` (30 sub-steps at `dt = 0.25`). Seeding draws from `ctx.rng` once per cycle and places `k = clamp(round(nodes / 2000), 1, 4)` broken fronts. Each front is a straight segment:
  - its length is `L ∈ [1.2, 2.0]·λ` at a uniform random angle;
  - its midpoint is uniform in the central 60 % of each axis and ≥ 1.5 λ from earlier midpoints;
  - if an end falls within 4 nodes of a wall, `L` shrinks in 10 % steps down to 0.8 λ; if it still does not fit, the draw is rejected. After 32 rejected tries the last draw is used, clipped to the lattice;
  - with probability 0.3 the segment is instead extended from one end to the wall it points at, which leaves one free end and gives a single spiral;
  - it is a 2-node strip of `u = 1` backed by a 6-node strip whose `v` ramps 0.69 → 0.36 away from the front. Every `v ≥ 0.315` gives `u_th > ½`, so the strip blocks backward propagation, and the ramp already shades as a tail.

  There is 1 pacemaker (2 when nodes ≥ 8000), at a normalised position in `[0.15, 0.85]²`. It first fires 1 tu into each cycle and then every `T_pm = 6` tu (≈ 1.5 × the spiral period), setting `u = 1` where `v < 0.1` in a disc of radius 2.5 nodes. The higher-frequency source wins (Zaikin & Zhabotinsky 1970; Winfree 1972), so the spiral domain grows into the target pattern. The story is a looping `Timeline [("waves", 60 s), ("quench", 3 s)]`. During the quench, `b` ramps 0.06 → 0.45 with `ease::smoothstep` over 0.5 s, so `u_th ≥ 0.6 > ½`. Fronts retreat, excited regions collapse once `v > a − b = 0.3`, pacemakers stay off, and `v` decays as `e^{−t}`. In the prototype there is no excited node 1 s into the quench, and max `v` is 0.012 at its end. At the cycle boundary `b` resets and a new seed set is painted over the dark field.
- **Motion**: fronts advance ≈ 11 nodes/s, and a lattice node is ≈ 1 pixel column. Spirals turn every ≈ 2.8 s. At t = 0 the seeded fronts already read as short comets. By 1 s their free ends visibly curl, and by 5 s the spirals have formed. Over the minute, spiral wave trains push the target rings back toward their nucleus. The quench fades the dish to black within ≈ 1 s and it stays dark for ≈ 2 s, then new fronts appear. The loop is 63 s.
- **Resize**: the lattice is `grid::lattice_for(Size(w, 2h), 20_000, 1.25)`, since Pixels are square, `w × 2h`. That gives 197×100 at 200×50 and exactly 80×48 at 80×24: below the cap the per-axis clamp makes the lattice the pixel grid itself. Above the cap, nodes are stretched relative to pixels by at most √1.25 (≤ 12 %). An unchanged lattice leaves the state untouched. Otherwise `u` and `v` are `resample_bilinear`ed into the new lattice (its convex weights keep `[0, 1]`), and the pattern relaxes to its natural λ within a few rotations. Pacemaker and seed positions are stored normalised in `[0, 1]²`. The first resize seeds. Resize also rebuilds per-axis sampling tables (source index and weight per pixel column and per pixel row). `render` then samples `u` and `v` bilinearly, or by direct index when the lattice equals the pixel grid, and shades into a `RefCell<raster::Pixels>` (`render` takes `&self`, as in `crystal_growth`) without allocating.
- **Inks**: `Role::Seq` encodes the *phase of the excitation cycle*, not a concentration. Let `r = clamp(v / (a − b₀), 0, 1)` with the unquenched `b₀ = 0.06`, so the quench ramp never brightens the tails. Excited cells (`u ≥ ½`) get `s = 1 − 0.45·r`, which is palest at the fresh front and orange at the back of the plateau. ### boids_flocking  (Biology, M10)
- **Intent**: a starling murmuration at dusk. About a thousand specks pour and fold like smoke above the roost, and the flock's texture shifts as it banks. Every 14 s a falcon, the only warm glyph on screen, stoops into it. A bright agitation wave then runs through the flock faster than any bird flies, and the murmuration bulges away from the attack and closes behind it. This is Potts' (1984) "chorus line": birds copy a neighbour's escape manoeuvre instead of each reacting to the falcon on its own.
- **Model**: Reynolds (1987) boids in 2-D (the sky plane) on the square braille dot lattice `W × H = 2w × 4h` dots (CORE-R41; 1 dot ≈ 0.3 m at scale σ = 1). Population `N = clamp(round(0.1·w·h), 24, 1000)` (80×24 ⇒ 192, 200×50 ⇒ 1000). Length scale `σ = clamp(√(8wh / (80 N)), 0.5, 4)`, which is 1 whenever N is uncapped, so the flock always covers the same share of the sky. Every length, speed and acceleration below is multiplied by σ, except the wall margin `m`. **Neighbourhood**: the topological `k = 7` nearest within a metric horizon `R = 12` dots (3.6 m), after Ballerini et al. (2008, `n_c ≈ 6.5`) and Hemelrijk & Hildenbrandt (2011, StarDisplay). A uniform spatial hash with cell size `R/2` is rebuilt every substep by a counting sort (counts → exclusive prefix sum → scatter in index order) into preallocated `start[C+1]` / `items[N]` buffers. Positions are clamped into edge cells; this is exact because floor-then-clamp never separates two points closer than one cell. A query visits the 5×5 block centre-out and skips any cell whose nearest point is farther than the current 7th-best distance. It keeps the k best in a fixed `[(d², idx); 7]` insertion array, with ties broken by index. The lists `nbr[N][7]` are built once per substep and shared by forces, contagion and rendering. **Acceleration of bird i** is the sum of five terms. (1) Separation `A_s Σ_{j∈N_i, d<r_s} (1 − d/r_s)(x_i − x_j)/d` with `A_s = 150` dots/s² and `r_s = 4` dots (1.2 m). A pair with `d < 1e-6` pushes along ±x instead, and the lower index goes −x. (2) Alignment `β(v̄_N − v_i)` with `β = 5` s⁻¹. (3) Cohesion `γ(x̄_N − x_i)` with `γ = 1` s⁻². (4) Speed relaxation `(v₀ − |v|)/τ_v · v̂` with `v₀ = 30` dots/s (9 m/s, starling cruise) and `τ_v = 0.5` s. (5) Roost attraction `κ(x_roost − x_i)` with `κ = 0.3` s⁻², which gives a period of `2π/√κ ≈ 11.5` s. The roost moves on a Lissajous path `centre + (0.25 W sin 2πt/23, 0.2 H sin 2πt/37)`. Soft bounds add `A_b·min(((m − d_edge)/m)², 4)` inward (`A_b = 200` dots/s²) within `m = 0.1·min(W, H)` dots of an edge. The total is capped at `|a| ≤ 250` dots/s². After the velocity update, the velocity is rotated by heading noise `gaussian(0, √(2 D_θ h))` with `D_θ = 0.1` rad²/s, and `|v|` is clamped to `[0.5, 2]·v₀`. **Integration**: synchronous semi-implicit Euler at `h = 1/60` s (all accelerations come from the old state and go into `vel_next`, then the buffers swap), with `ceil(dt/h)` substeps (≤ 15) and the remainder carried in an accumulator. A specular wall at the box edges `[0, W] × [0, H]` reflects the position and negates the normal velocity. It is only a safety net; the soft bounds do the work (BOID-A4). **Falcon**: a looping 14 s `Timeline`. Patrol 7 s: slow 4 s circles near whichever upper corner is farther from the flock, at 0.8 v₀ and turning ≤ 2 rad/s. Stoop 2.5 s: speed eased from 0.8 to 3 v₀ with `ease::in_out_cubic`, steering toward the flock centroid predicted 0.5 s ahead, turning ≤ 3 rad/s. Pursue 2 s: 2 v₀, turning ≤ 1.5 rad/s, so the birds can out-turn it. Climb 2.5 s: 1.5 v₀ back to patrol. It never removes birds, and its position is clamped to the box. **Escape wave**: Hemelrijk et al. (2015) showed that agitation waves arise only when birds repeat an escape manoeuvre, and that the dark band comes from orientation, not density. Each stoop is an attack with an id, and alarms can fire only inside that attack's stoop and pursue windows. A calm bird fires at most once per attack, in one of two ways. Directly: the falcon is within `r_flee = 12` dots, and the turn sign points away from it. By contagion: a topological neighbour fired at least `L = 2` substeps (33 ms) earlier and is still alarmed; the sign is copied from the lowest-index such neighbour. Fire steps are stored, so the outcome does not depend on update order. An alarmed bird (age < `T_a = 0.35` s = 21 substeps) adds a lateral `160` dots/s² with its sign and relaxes toward `1.3 v₀`. While the falcon is inside `r_flee` it also flees at `200(1 − d/r_flee)` dots/s². The front advances about one neighbour spacing every L substeps, which is faster than any bird flies; Procaccini et al. (2011) measured wave speeds often above the flock's own speed. **Banking**: coordinated-turn roll `φ = atan(|a_⊥| / (gσ))` with `g = 32.7` dots/s² (9.81 m/s²), smoothed by an EMA (τ = 0.1 s). An alarmed bird's 160 dots/s² turn banks it past 75°. **Start**: a disc of radius `min(√(12N/π)·σ, 0.3·min(W, H))` at the roost with uniformly random headings at v₀, so each visit opens with order emerging from chaos. All randomness comes from `ctx.rng` in index order.
- **Motion**: birds cross ≈ 15 cells/s at 200×50, so every step changes the frame. Within 1 s the random disc visibly breaks into aligned streams, and by ≈ 5 s it is one flock (BOID-A3). The flock swings around the wandering roost with a period of ≈ 11 s. Stoops begin at t ≡ 7 s (mod 14). The wave crosses the flock in under 1 s, and the flock re-closes within ≈ 2 s. The scene never resets, and its state is bounded.
- **Resize**: positions and the falcon are scaled by `(W'/W, H'/H)` and velocities by `σ'/σ`. When N shrinks, the first N' birds are kept. When it grows, new birds are cloned from bird `j mod N` with the same velocity and a `gaussian2` jitter of `r_s·σ'` (clamped into the box), and they start calm. σ, the hash and `Dots` are rebuilt. Bird arrays are allocated at capacity 1000 in `build`, so only the hash `start` array and `Dots` reallocate. Timeline time is untouched.
- **Inks**: calm birds are `Role::Structure` (grey-blue dusk silhouettes) at level `80 + 105·min(φ/60°, 1)`, which spans 80–185. Alarmed birds are `Role::Star` at 200–255, fading with alarm age. Because `Dots` keeps the brightest dot per cell, the wave always wins over calm neighbours. The falcon is `Role::Hot` 240, the only warm object, with a 0.3 s dot streak in `Role::Hot` 90. Label `Role::Text` 110.
- **ASCII**: each bird is a heading-aware stroke on `Dots`. A level bird lights 2 dots: the head at x and the tail at `x − 1.5σ v̂`. A bird banked past 35° adds its two wing dots at ±1 dot perpendicular to the heading, for 4 dots. Alarmed birds are banked, so the wave is literally denser, which matches Hemelrijk's orientational bands. The `Dots` fallback (1 `.`, 2 `:`, 3–4 `+`, ≥ 5 `#`) turns the flock into a `.`/`:` haze with a `+`/`#` core and a `+`/`#` band where the wave passes. The falcon is an 8-way heading glyph `→ ↘ ↓ ↙ ← ↖ ↑ ↗` chosen by `round(atan2(vy, vx)/(π/4))`, with ASCII `> \ v / < \ ^ /`. It is drawn with `Canvas::put` over the blit.
- **Label**: `N 192  k=7` (ASCII, preformatted in `resize`) at the bottom-left, drawn only when `w ≥ 32` and `h ≥ 10`.
- **Perf**: at 200×50 (N = 1000, hash of 67×34 cells of 6 dots), `update(1/60)` is one substep. Hash rebuild ≈ 10 µs. Pruned kNN over ≈ 60–120 candidates per bird ≈ 0.15–0.3 ms, or ≈ 0.4 ms in an over-compressed core with median nn 2 dots. Forces, noise and integration ≈ 0.06 ms. Contagion < 0.01 ms. Rasterising ≤ 4000 dots into `Dots` inside `update` ≈ 0.02 ms. `render` blits 10 k cells in ≈ 0.04 ms. The total is ≈ 0.3–0.45 ms, against the < 1 ms aim. At dt = 0.25 (15 substeps) it is ≈ 5 ms, but GEN-R8 is measured at dt = 1/60. Steady state is allocation-free.
- **Acceptance**: `BOID-A1` for seeded clouds (N ∈ {1, 7, 8, 500}, including coincident points and points outside the box), the spatial hash's `k_nearest(i, 7, R)` returns exactly the brute-force set of ≤ 7 nearest within R, excluding i, sorted by (d², index); `BOID-A2` `N`/`σ` follow the formulas (80×24 ⇒ 192/1.0, 200×50 ⇒ 1000/1.0, 400×100 ⇒ 1000/2.0, 1×1 ⇒ 24/0.5), N is constant across updates, a resize from 200×50 to 80×24 keeps the first 192 birds with positions scaled by (W'/W, H'/H), and a resize from 80×24 to 200×50 yields 1000 birds with every clone within `3·r_s·σ'` of its scaled source; `BOID-A3` order emerges: in the seeded 100×30 scene (N = 300) with the falcon disabled, global polarisation `Φ = |Σ v̂|/N` is < 0.2 at t = 0, and over 1 s samples in t ∈ [8, 20] s the median local alignment `⟨v̂_i · mean v̂_{N_i}⟩` is ≥ 0.9 and the median Φ is ≥ 0.75 (wild flocks reach ≈ 0.96, Cavagna et al. 2010) (~1 s in debug); `BOID-A4` over 20 s at 80×24 with attacks (seed 42), every bird is finite and inside `[0, W] × [0, H]` after every substep, speed stays in `[0.5, 2]·v₀σ`, the specular wall acts on < 2 % of bird-substeps after t = 5 s, and at every 1 s sample after 5 s the median nearest-neighbour distance is in `[0.35, 1.0]·r_s` (1.4–4 dots ≈ 0.4–1.2 m; ### dna_helix  (Biology, M10)
- **Intent**: the double helix of Watson and Crick's 1953 figure: two backbones winding right-handed around a ladder of coloured base pairs and turning in space. Then a replication fork runs through it. A helicase ring unzips the ladder, and each separated strand is copied into a daughter helix that is half old (grey) and half new (magenta): the leading strand continuously, the lagging strand in Okazaki fragments.
- **Model**: B-DNA in Å, with world +y up (CORE-R45 maps it to screen up) and the axis along +x.
  - **Helix geometry**:
    - Twist is `Ω = 2π/10.5` per bp, i.e. 34.29° (Wang 1979), with a rise of 3.4 Å, so the pitch is 35.7 Å.
    - The phosphorus backbone radius is `R = 8.9 Å` (Arnott & Hukins 1972).
    - The Watson angle is `θ_W(x, t) = φ(t) + 2πx/35.7 Å`. Crick is `θ_C = θ_W + Δ`, with `Δ = 2.2 rad` from C1′–C1′ = 10.5 Å at r = 5.9 Å (Saenger 1984). That gives axial groove widths of 12.5 Å (minor) and 23.2 Å (major).
    - A backbone point is `(x, y₀ + R cos θ, R sin θ)` about its duplex axis at height `y₀`. θ increases with x, so the helix is right-handed. Watson runs 5′→3′ along +x.
  - **Layout**: base pair `i` sits at `xᵢ = x_L + (i − 4)·3.4 Å`, where `x_L` is the world x at column 0. Four pairs lie beyond each screen edge, so the camera sway never exposes a blunt end.
  - **Rungs**: a chord at `xᵢ` between the two backbones, split at its midpoint so each half shows that strand's base.
  - **Sequence**: `N_MAX = 192` Watson bases in a stack array. The seeded GC target is `p ∈ [0.35, 0.65]`, and exactly `round(p·192)` positions are G or C (each half-and-half at random), placed by a Fisher–Yates shuffle from `ctx.rng`. Crick is the complement.
  - **Spin**: `φ(t) = φ₀ − ω·t` over the whole scene, with `ω = 2π·v_f/10.5 = 1.7952 rad/s`. That is the unwinding a fork at `v_f = 3 bp/s` needs, so the apex angle stays fixed. The unreplicated duplex spins as if its far end were free; in vivo it cannot rotate, and topoisomerases instead relax the positive supercoils that build ahead of the fork.
  - **Fork**: the apex is at `f(τ) = v_f·τ` bp, where τ is the time since `unwind` began. It keeps advancing through `hold` and is frozen during `reset`. There is no fork during `helix`. `v_f` is ≈ 6–17× slower than human forks (1–3 kb/min) and ≈ 300× slower than *E. coli* (≈ 1 kb/s).
  - **Arms**: behind the fork (`s = f − i > 0`) the parental strands separate into two arms, displaced by `±Y(s) = ±1.7R·smoothstep(s/6)` in world y.
    - The upper arm (+y) is the Watson template, which is the lagging-strand template. A DnaB-style helicase ring encircles it at the apex, translocating 5′→3′.
    - The lower arm is the Crick template (the leading strand).
    - Each new strand takes its partner's angular slot (Watson + Δ, or Crick − Δ), so each daughter is a B duplex like the parent. Keeping B geometry in the arms is artistic licence; real single-stranded DNA at the fork is unwound.
  - **Synthesis** is 5′→3′ only:
    - Leading: new base `j` is made at `f(τ) − 2 = j`, i.e. `τ = (j + 2)/3` (the polymerase trails the helicase by 2 bp).
    - Lagging: Okazaki fragments of `L_ok = 8` bp (scaled down from 100–200 nt in eukaryotes and 1–2 kb in bacteria) cover `[8k, 8k + 8)`. Fragment `k` is primed at its high-x end when `f ≥ 8k + 9` (`τ_k = (8k + 9)/3`) and extended toward decreasing x at `v_pol = 8 bp/s`, so base `j` is made at `τ_k + (8k + 7 − j)/8`. The fragment is complete 7/8 s after priming, and ligase seals the nick 0.5 s after completion.
    - Template bases that are not yet paired show as half-rungs.
  - Every quantity is a closed-form function of scene time; nothing is integrated.
- **Motion**: a `Timeline` of `helix` 4 s → `unwind` 14 s → `hold` 4 s → `reset` 2 s, looping every 24 s.
  - **Spin**: from the first frame the spin reads as a barber-pole drift along +x at 10.2 Å/s. That equals the fork speed, so the fork travels with the pattern. It is ≈ 7 columns/s at 80×24 (≈ 11 while zoomed) and ≈ 16 at 200×50.

### electron_orbitals  (Chemistry, M10)
- **Intent**: the hydrogen atom as its quantum states actually look. A Monte-Carlo cloud of electron positions is drawn from |ψ_nlm|² and turns slowly in 3-D. Lobes are coloured by the sign of the wavefunction, and the nucleus glints through the nodes. Between states the atom passes through a real superposition, and the charge cloud sloshes from lobe to lobe at a display-compressed Bohr frequency. That oscillating dipole is how the atom emits or absorbs Lyman-α, Hα or Paschen-α light. The cloud is centred. The r₉₀ surface of 4f spans 0.75 of the height, and smaller n is drawn smaller but never as a dot. Point density is matched to each orbital's on-screen size, so 1s is a speckled ball, not a solid disc, and 4f's lobes are not sparse. The eye reads lobe shape first, then the colour of the sign, then the corner label. Nothing else is on screen.
- **Model**: atomic units, a₀ = 1. `ψ = R_nl(r)·Y(r̂)` with `R_nl = √((2/n)³(n−l−1)!/(2n(n+l)!)) e^{−ρ/2} ρ^l L^{(2l+1)}_{n−l−1}(ρ)`, ρ = 2r/n (generalised Laguerre by its three-term recurrence). Real harmonics on the unit direction (x, y, z): `s = 1/√(4π)`, `p_z = √(3/4π) z`, `p_x = √(3/4π) x`, `d_z² = √(5/16π)(3z² − 1)`, `d_xz = √(15/4π) xz`, `d_xy = √(15/4π) xy`, `f_z³ = √(7/16π)(5z³ − 3z)`, `f_xyz = √(105/4π) xyz`. The gallery is a closed dipole-allowed loop (Δl = ±1): 1s → 2p_z → 3d_z² → 4f_z³ → 3d_xz → 4f_xyz → 3d_xy → 2p_x → 1s. Energies are `E_n = −R_H/n²` with `R_H = R∞hc·μ/mₑ = 13.5983 eV` (reduced mass). Lines are `λ = hc/ΔE` with hc = 1239.842 eV·nm: Lyα 121.57 nm, Hα 656.47 nm, Pα 1875.6 nm (vacuum). Sampling is exact and separable, because every real harmonic here is a product Θ(θ)·Φ(φ): `|ψ|² d³r = [r²R² dr]·[Θ² d cosθ]·[Φ² dφ]`. Each factor is tabulated once as a 1024-knot inverse CDF (r ∈ [0, 1.5·r₉₉.₉], cos θ ∈ [−1, 1], φ ∈ [0, 2π)). One sample is three linear-interpolated lookups driven by `ctx.rng` uniforms. Each point also stores fixed uniforms `u_i` (visibility) and `v_i` (mixture), plus ψ of its own orbital and of its partner (f32 storage). Superposition a → b: `Ψ = c_a ψ_a e^{−iE_a t} + c_b ψ_b e^{−iE_b t}` with `c_a = cos χ`, `c_b = sin χ`, so `|Ψ|² = c_a²ψ_a² + c_b²ψ_b² + 2c_a c_b ψ_a ψ_b cos ωt`. Display sampler (exact intensity, per-orbital density): orbital k uses a prefix of `M_k = clamp(round(0.8·π(4ρ_k)²), 256, 16384)` iid points, where ρ_k is its on-screen r₉₀ radius in rows (View), which gives ≈ 0.4 visible points per dot inside r₉₀. For a → b: `M(χ) = (c_a²/M_a + c_b²/M_b)⁻¹`, `p_a = M(χ)c_a²/M_a` and `p_b = M(χ)c_b²/M_b`, so p_a + p_b = 1. Point i < M_a of slot S_a is proposed iff `v_i < p_a`, and point i < M_b of S_b iff `v_i < p_b`, so proposals have intensity exactly `M(χ)·q` with `q = c_a²ψ_a² + c_b²ψ_b²`. A proposed point is visible iff `u_i < w/2`, where `w = |Ψ|²/q = 1 + 2c_a c_b ψ_a ψ_b cos ωt / q ∈ [0, 2]` (AM–GM), evaluated in f64 (q ≤ 0 or non-finite ⇒ hidden). The visible points therefore have expected density exactly `(M(χ)/2)·|Ψ(t)|²` at every instant, and their count glides from M_a/2 to M_b/2. Because u and v are fixed per point, the cloud changes only where thresholds are crossed: no shimmer, no pops. At the endpoints c² (and therefore p) is set to exactly 0 or 1. The centroid follows `⟨r⟩(t) = sin 2χ·d_ab cos ωt` with `d_ab = ⟨a|r|b⟩`: 0.7449, 2.452, 5.188, 2.995, 3.867, 3.867, 2.123 and 0.7449 a₀ around the loop. Real Bohr frequencies (Lyα is 2.47 PHz) are compressed monotonically to `f_disp = 2.0 Hz·√(ΔE/ΔE_Lyα)`: 2.0 / 0.86 / 0.51 Hz for Lyα / Hα / Pα, with ω = 2π f_disp and `t` counted from the rise start. Timeline per orbital: `hold` 6 s (pure; this is χ = π/2 of the (prev, a) pair and χ = 0 of the (a, next) pair, which give the same visible set), `rise` 2 s (χ 0 → π/4, smoothstep), `beat` 3 s (χ = π/4, full sloshing), `fall` 2 s (χ π/4 → π/2). That is 13 s per orbital and a 104 s loop, starting at a seeded orbital's hold. Two-slot ring: during a's hold the free slot is filled with S_next, and S_a's partner ψ is rewritten to ψ_next (≤ 256 points per 1/60 s of dt; any remainder completes at the rise boundary). View: orbital (x, y, z) maps to world (x, z, −y), so orbital z is screen-up. One world unit is the orbital's r₉₀ times `g_k = (r₉₀,k/r₉₀,4f)^{0.3}` (r₉₀ = 2.66 / 7.99 / 15.80 / 25.99 a₀ for 1s / 2p / 3d / 4f), so `ρ_k = R·g_k` with `R = fit_radius(size, 0.75)`, which is 0.50 R, 0.70 R, 0.86 R and R. During transitions log g is lerped with weight c_b². Camera: `geom::Camera` with distance 6, yaw `2πt/24 s`, and pitch `0.15 + 0.10 sin(2πt/17 s)`. The pitch stays low so the nodal planes of m = 0 states stay open; at pitch 0.32 the 2p_z gap fills in. References: Bethe & Salpeter (1957) for ⟨r⟩, ⟨r²⟩ and d(1s–2p).
- **Motion**: the camera turns 15°/s. Even a 1s or m = 0 state, whose shape is invariant under yaw, visibly turns because the finite sample rotates, so the frame changes within the first second (GEN-R9). Otherwise a stationary state stays still, as it does in nature; there is no resampling shimmer. Every 13 s there is a transition. The second lobe grows in, the cloud rocks between lobes at f_disp (1s–2p_z swings up and down twice a second, the 3d–4f pairs roll slowly), and then the new orbital settles and the framing eases to its size. Every 15 s visit sees at least one transition.
- **Resize**: samples live in atomic units and don't depend on the terminal size. They are drawn in the first resize (current and next slot) and then during holds. A resize rescales the view, resizes `Dots` and recomputes every M_k. When an M_k grows past the sampled count, the resize tops up that live slot (allocation is allowed there). Timeline, samples and uniforms are kept. Zero-area sizes make plotting a no-op. ADR-0012 needs no lattice.
- **Inks**: the cloud uses `Role::Div`. The sign is that of the dominant component at the point, `sign(c_kψ_k)` with `k = argmax_k |c_kψ_k|`; in holds this is exactly the orbital's phase sign, and it never blinks at ω. The level is `ink::div(±(0.44 + 0.56·near))`, i.e. `128 ± (56 + 71·near)`, so both signs stay clear of the dark zero and nearer dots are more saturated: cyan negative, orange positive. Each cell must keep its most intense dot by `|level − 128|` (the proposed CORE-R41 amendment for `Div`). Under the current by-level rule, a negative-lobe cell shows its farthest dot and every mixed cell turns orange. The nucleus is `Accent` 180 (`Glyph::new('•', 'o')`), drawn before the cloud so it shows only through nodes (l ≥ 1). The label is `Text` 150.
- **ASCII**: `Dots` fallback by dot count (`.`, `:`, `+`, `#`). 1s is a speckled round ball, `+#` at the core fading through `:` to `.`. 2p_z is two stacked `+#` lobes with a sparse `.` equatorial band and the `o` nucleus between them. 3d_z² is two lobes plus a `.:` equatorial ring. 4f_z³ is two polar lobes and two conical `+` rings. 4f_xyz shows its eight cube-diagonal lobes as four `+#` pairs at most yaws. Labels use their ASCII variants.
- **Label**: bottom-left `(1, h−1)`, static strings only (GEN-R11). Hold: `3d_z²` (ASCII `3d_z2`). Transition: `2p_z → 3d_z²  Hα 656.5 nm` (ASCII `2p_z -> 3d_z2  H-a 656.5 nm`), and likewise `Lyα 121.6 nm` / `Ly-a` and `Pα 1876 nm` / `P-a`. The line part is dropped when `w < len + 2`, and the whole label when `w < 14` or `h < 8`.
- **Perf**: estimated at 200×50 with M_k ≤ 16384. The v-test runs over ≤ 2 × 16384 f32 SoA points, weights are computed for about half of them, and ≤ 8 k visible points are projected and plotted: ≈ 0.15 ms. The camera rotation is hoisted out of the loop (see the proposed `Camera::basis`). `Dots` clear and blit of 10 k cells ≈ 0.05 ms. Slot preparation of ≤ 256 samples plus ψ pairs per 1/60 s ≈ 0.08 ms. Total ≈ 0.3 ms (aim < 1 ms). The worst frame is a resize late in a hold, which forces synchronous completion of a 16384-point slot (≈ 2 ms in release, still < 5 ms). - **Acceptance**: `ORB-A1` every gallery orbital satisfies ∫R²r² dr = 1 (trapezoid, h = 0.01, r ≤ 90; 1e-6) and ∫Y² dΩ = 1 (48-point Gauss–Legendre × 96 φ, 1e-9), and distinct gallery orbitals are orthogonal (|⟨i|j⟩| < 1e-9); ### globular_cluster  (Astronomy, M10)
- **Intent**: an ancient globular cluster seen through a slowly turning telescope. A blazing, unresolved core thins into a sparse halo of single stars, with a handful of orange giants glinting among them. Every visible halo star is a live gravitating body: the swarm breathes, and the camera's slow turn gives it depth through parallax. It is calm, dense and three-dimensional, the stellar counterpart to the flat `starfield` and the disk-shaped `galaxy_collision`.
- **Model**:
  - **Units and softening**: direct N-body in Hénon units (`G = M = 1`, virial radius 1, `E ≈ −1/4`), equal masses `m = 1/N`, Plummer softening `Φ_ij = −G m²/√(r² + ε²)` with `ε = 0.05` (0.085 of the Plummer scale radius `a = 3π/16`).
  - **Forces and integration**: an O(N²/2) Newton-3 symmetric pair loop, so `Σ m a = 0` to rounding. `ode::verlet_integrate` (KDK leapfrog, CORE-R44) runs with `h_max = 1/128` N-body units at a rate of `0.4` N-body units per simulated second: one force evaluation per frame at `dt = 1/60`, 13 at `dt = 0.25`. The crossing time `t_cr = 2√2` is 7.1 s.
  - **ICs**: Plummer sphere `ρ ∝ (1 + r²/a²)^{−5/2}`, sampled per Aarseth, Hénon & Wielen (1974) in `G = M = a = 1`:
    - Radius `r = (X₁^{−2/3} − 1)^{−1/2}` with mass cut `X₁ < 0.999` (so `r ≤ 38.7 a`).
    - Isotropic directions (`z = 1 − 2u`, `φ = 2πu`).
    - Speed `v = q·√2(1 + r²)^{−1/4}`, with `q` from `g(q) = q²(1 − q²)^{7/2}` by rejection under the bound 0.1 (the maximum is 0.0923).
    - COM position and velocity subtracted.
    - Positions scaled so the unsoftened `W = −1/2` (then `a ≈ 3π/16` and `r_h ≈ 0.769`).
    - Velocities scaled so `2K = |W_ε|` (virial equilibrium in the softened potential), giving `E ≈ −0.248`.
  - **N**: the largest bucket in `{64, 96, 128, 192, 256, 384, 500}` that is `≤ max(area/5, 64)`. 80×24 gives 384, and 200×50 and anything larger gives 500 (the ADR-0012 cap).
  - **Unresolved light**: each body stands for ~10³ real stars, so 500 bodies cannot fill a braille core (at 200×50 they light only ≈ 1.5 of 8 dots per core cell). The core glow is drawn from the Plummer surface brightness `g(R) = (1 + R²/a_g²)^{−2}`, where `R` is the projected distance from the centre and `a_g = r_h/1.3048`. Here `r_h` is the body system's 3-D half-mass radius about the origin, smoothed with `τ = 4 s` and reset at every reseed, so the glow follows the real core contraction.
  - **Luminosity tags** (visual only): the first `g_n = clamp(N/50, 3, 10)` bodies (sample order is random) are red giants. The dynamics stay equal-mass, so there is no mass segregation.
- **Motion**:
  - Bodies orbit in the self-consistent potential: the core crosses in about 2 s and halo stars drift slowly.
  - `geom::Camera` yaws at `2π/90 s`, with pitch `0.30 + 0.15·sin(2πt/67 s)` and perspective `distance = 10` units, so near-side stars slide against far-side ones. The camera alone moves an `r = 1.5` star ≈ 4 dots/s at 200×50.
  - A looping `Timeline`: `evolve` 120 s (48 N-body units ≈ 17 t_cr). Spitzer's `t_rh ≈ 0.138 N r_h^{3/2}/ln(0.4N)` gives 8.8 units (22 s) at N = 500; softening at ε = 0.05 cuts ln Λ to ≈ ln(r_h/ε) ≈ 2.7, so a cycle spans ≈ 3–5 t_rh. Then a 1.5 s `fade`, a fresh Plummer sample from `ctx.rng`, and a 1.5 s `rise`: 123 s per loop.
  - Within a cycle the core contracts slightly and the odd star escapes. Core collapse needs ≈ 15 t_rh and is never reached. The reseed keeps the halo populated and bounds the accumulated energy error.
- **Resize** (ADR-0012 analogue, with N as the cap):
  - Within the same N bucket a resize only changes the view: `scale = draw::fit_radius(size, 0.92)/2.2` rows per unit, so `R = 2.2` units (≈ 93 % of the projected mass) fills the frame, and `Dots` is resized. Pos/vel are untouched.
  - On a bucket change, shrinking keeps the first N′ bodies (an unbiased subsample). Growing appends fresh AHW samples, scaled by the same length and velocity factors as the build sample. Then `m = 1/N′`, COM and momentum are re-zeroed, and velocities are re-virialised to `2K = |W_ε|` (one O(N²) pass). The cluster never visibly resets while you drag the window.
- **Inks**:
  - Everything except the giants is one `Dots` layer in `Role::Star`.
  - Glow: a dot at projected radius `R` is lit iff `p(R) > u(x, y)`, where `p = clamp(1.7·(g − 0.08)/0.92, 0, 1)` and `u` is a fixed per-dot hash in `[0, 1)` salted once from `ctx.rng`. So the core is solid for `R ≤ 0.52 a_g`, thins through a speckled ring, and ends at `R = 1.59 a_g`. Glow level is `90 + 165·g`.
  - Bodies: level `100 + 140·√g(R) + 20·clamp(z/2, −1, 1)` (nearer is brighter), clamped to `[0, 255]`.
  - Giants: `Glyph::new('✦', '*')` in `Role::Hot` 230, drawn over the blit.
  - The label is `Text` 140.
  - `Seq` was deliberately not used: a false-colour cluster reads as a heatmap, not a starfield.
- **ASCII**: the `Dots` count fallback gives the classic look. At 80×24 there is a `#` core block (≈ 3 × 6 cells), a `+`/`:` ring out to ≈ 5 rows, `.` halo stars, and giants as `*`. The label is pure ASCII.
- **Label**: top-left (row 0, column 1), e.g. `Plummer N=500  3.1 t_cr`, where the time is since the last reseed in crossing times. It is written into a stack `TextBuf` without allocating. The time part is dropped when `w < 36`, and the whole label when `w < 24`.

### ising_model  (Physics & Chemistry, M10)
- **Intent**: the ferromagnetic phase transition as a living mosaic. A quench turns static into two colours of domain that swell, round off and straighten. Reheating melts them into a dim boil, and a slow cool through the critical point leaves clusters of every size breathing (critical opalescence). Order lights up: aligned regions glow and disorder sinks toward the background, so the eye goes to the shrinking bubbles and the straightening walls.
- **Model**: 2-D Ising `H = −J Σ⟨ij⟩ sᵢsⱼ`, `sᵢ = ±1`, `J = k_B = 1`, zero field, periodic (torus) boundaries. The lattice is capped: `L = grid::lattice_for(Size(w, 2h), 16 384, 1.25)` in square pixel units, so 200×50 → 178×91 (≈ 1.1 px per spin) and 80×24 → 80×48 (below the cap the lattice is the pixel grid itself). Kinetics are single-spin Glauber heat-bath (Glauber 1963), random-sequential, with one `u64` per attempt from a scene-local SplitMix64 seeded once from `ctx.rng` at the first resize (GEN-R7). Bits 32–47 and 48–63 pick `x = (r₁₆·Lw)>>16` and `y = (r₁₆·Lh)>>16` (≤ 0.3 % non-uniform, harmless because every heat-bath move leaves the Boltzmann distribution invariant). The local field is `h = Σ_nn sⱼ ∈ {−4,−2,0,2,4}`; set `sᵢ = +1` iff the low 32 bits are `< thr[h]`, where `thr = round(p₊·2³²)` as u64 (so `p = 0, 1` are exact) and `p₊(h) = 1/(1 + e^{−2h/T})`. The 5-entry table is rebuilt once per update with T clamped to `[0.05, 100]` (NaN ⇒ T_c). This is the same model-A equilibrium, universality and coarsening class as Metropolis; it replaces checkerboard Metropolis because deterministic sublattice sweeps flip every ΔE = 0 spin at high T (a period-2 strobe) and because random-sequential updates give smooth fractional sweeps per frame at any lattice size. The rate is `max(60·N/16 384, 1)` Monte Carlo sweeps (MCS) per simulated second (N = lattice sites; one MCS per 1/60 s step at the cap), realised as `⌊acc⌋` attempts from an accumulator gaining `rate·N·dt`. Domain size relative to the lattice scales as `√(MCS/N)`, so coarsening looks the same at every terminal size. Temperature is in units of J/k_B with `T_c = 2/ln(1+√2) = 2.269 185 314` (Onsager 1944). References: `m₀(T) = (1 − sinh(2/T)⁻⁴)^{1/8}` below T_c and 0 above (Yang 1952); exact energy per spin `e(T) = −coth 2β·[1 + (2/π)·k′·K(k)]` with `k′ = 2 tanh² 2β − 1` (signed complementary modulus, computed directly to avoid cancellation), `K = π/(2·AGM(1, |k′|))`, and the product `k′·K` taken as its limit 0 when `k′ = 0`, so `e(T_c) = −√2`. T/T_c follows a looping `Timeline` of 58 s: `coarsen` 20 s at 0.60 → `reheat` 8 s, smoothstep 0.60→1.25 → `hot` 5 s at 1.25 (ξ ≈ 2–3, a boil rather than a per-frame fizz) → `cool` 15 s, `1 + 0.25(1−p)²` (eases into T_c so most of the phase sits near criticality) → `critical` 8 s at 1.00 → `quench` 2 s, smoothstep 1.00→0.60; it is continuous at every boundary. The first resize seeds a T = ∞ state (i.i.d. ±1), so t = 0 is the textbook quench. Magnetisation `M = Σs` is tracked incrementally and recomputed on resize.

### liesegang_rings  (Chemistry, M10)
- **Intent**: the rhythm of periodic precipitation. A rack of gel columns stands side by side, each with its outer electrolyte poured on top. In each gel a faint supersaturation haze sinks and, every few seconds, snaps into a bright precipitate band. Bands grow wider, brighter and farther apart with depth (Jablczynski's spacing law). Each tube keeps its own tempo: the concentrated one packs bands tight and quick, the dilute one wide and slow, so the Matalon–Packter law reads as a visual polyrhythm. Then a redissolution front wipes the bands top-down and the rack regrows. ("Liesegang rings" names the phenomenon; in tubes it forms bands.)
- **Model**: per tube, a 1-D reaction–diffusion system with nucleation-and-growth precipitation. The ingredients are Ostwald's (1897) supersaturation theory, the nucleation threshold of Keller & Rubinow (1981) and the aggregation threshold of Chopard, Luthi & Droz (1994). The reactions are A + B → C (soluble product, idealised 1:1) and C → D (immobile precipitate), as in Liesegang's 1896 experiment of AgNO₃ diffusing into K₂Cr₂O₇-loaded gelatin to precipitate silver dichromate. The equations are `∂a/∂t = a'' − k·a·b`, `∂b/∂t = b'' − k·a·b`, `∂c/∂t = D_c·c'' + k·a·b − P`, `∂d/∂t = P`, `P = λ·max(c − c_s, 0)·[nᵢ]`. After each Euler step the per-node nucleation flag nᵢ is set irreversibly when `cᵢ ≥ c*` (homogeneous nucleation at supersaturation) or when `cᵢ ≥ c_g` and a neighbour was nucleated before the step (growth on adjacent precipitate; a Jacobi update, so the result is sweep-order-free). The step time is stored in `t_nuc[i]`. Parameters (dimensionless) are `D_a = D_b = 1`, `D_c = 0.3`, `k = 1`, `λ = 0.05`, `c_s = 0.01`, `c_g = 0.037`, `c* = 0.04`, `b₀ = 0.05`, and five presets `a₀ ∈ {0.7, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0}` (a₀/b₀ = 14–60). One unit ≈ 0.2 mm and one time unit ≈ ℓ²/D_A ≈ 27 s (D_A ≈ 1.5·10⁻⁹ m²/s), so the displayed 100 units are 2 cm grown in about 9 h, which are real Liesegang scales. The discretisation is part of the model: band positions shift about 10 % with τ while p changes by ≤ 0.02 between τ = 0.15 and 0.075. It uses a 3-point Laplacian and explicit Euler with `τ = 0.15`; positivity holds exactly since `τ(2 + k·a₀,max) = 0.75 ≤ 1` and `τ(2D_c + λ) ≤ 1`. Fields are f64 on 160-node columns, and node i sits at depth `x = i + 1` (the reservoir ghost is x = 0). The top ghost is `a = a₀` (reservoir) with no flux for b and c; the bottom ghost is `a = 0, b = b₀,c, c = 0` (semi-infinite gel; a closed bottom starves the front of b). All five tubes are always simulated, whatever is shown. Sim time is a warp of scene time s: `t_sim = T_END·(s/36 s)^{1.5}` with `T_END = 1200` through `grow`, then continuing at its final rate of 50 units/s, so `t_sim = 1500` when `hold` ends. Euler steps are taken whole from an accumulator, so the scheme always uses exactly τ. `scan_bands(d)` runs every 0.25 s: nodes with `d ≥ 5 %` of the column max form contiguous runs, each run is a band with d-weighted centroid x, width = run length and birth time `t_n = min t_nuc` over its nodes, and the first run is the junction zone, excluded from every law. Reference behaviour (a₀ = 2, t = 1500): bands at x = 8.9 (junction zone, 17 wide), 21.3, 28.3, 36.9, 48.6, 63.9, 83.6 and 106.8, ratios 1.33, 1.31, 1.32, 1.32, 1.31, 1.28 (last five mean 1.304), widths 5 → 12 (width law), and x_n/√t_n rising to 2.9 (last four mean 2.84; time law, Morse & Pierce 1903). Across the presets at t = 1400, p = 0.507, 0.406, 0.341, 0.299, 0.264, which fits `p = F + G·b₀/a₀` with F = 0.190, G = 4.41, R² = 0.999 (Matalon & Packter 1955; Antal et al. 1998). The looping `Timeline` (50 s) is `grow` 36 s → `hold` 6 s (bands thicken and the fronts leave the view) → `dissolve` 8 s. Dissolve is a scripted redissolution with the reaction frozen, like an ammonia front complexing the silver salt to [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ (or Co(OH)₂ in excess NH₃). The front descends as `y_e = 120·√progress` nodes (it passes the view bottom at 69 % of the phase), and above it `c, d ← ·e^{−3·dt}`; a, b and the flags do not change. At the loop, state resets to `(a, b, c, d, n, t_nuc) = (0, b₀,c, 0, 0, false, ∞)` with `b₀,c = b₀·(1 + 0.03ξ)`, where ξ ∈ [−1, 1] is drawn from `ctx.rng` at the loop (ξ = 0 in the first cycle). `regrow` is simply the next cycle, with slightly different bands.
- **Motion**: in the first second (at s ≈ 0.7 s) each junction zone precipitates as a dim, cloudy cap under the meniscus, and a dim-red haze starts to sink. Bands are then born 2–5 s apart, staggered across tubes (a₀ = 2: s ≈ 6, 8, 11, 15, 20, 28 s). At each birth a thin faint line appears just ahead of the haze, then brightens and thickens over about 1 s as it drains the haze, leaving a darker depletion gap behind. During `dissolve` a clear front sweeps down and erases the bands top-down; the tubes rest clear for about 2.5 s before the next cycle.

- **Label**: the header is on row 0. - **Perf**: update is ≤ 5 tubes × 160 nodes × ≤ 6 Euler sub-steps per 1/60 s (the peak comes in `hold`; 1–2 early) ≈ 4.8 k node updates ≈ 25 µs, plus a band scan of 5 × 160 nodes every 0.25 s. `dt = 0.25` costs ≤ 84 sub-steps ≈ 0.35 ms. ### molecule_viewer  (Chemistry, M10)
- **Intent**: a slowly tumbling ball-and-stick model of a real molecule, lit like a physical model on a dark bench. Near atoms are bright and far atoms recede.   - water: r(OH) 0.9572 Å, ∠HOH 104.52° (Benedict, Gailar & Plyler 1956).
  - methane: T_d, r(CH) 1.087 Å.

### physarum  (Biology, M10)
- **Intent**: a slime mould solving a transport problem in time-lapse. A glowing inoculum spills outward in a lace of veins, finds the scattered oat flakes, and thickens into a few pulsing tubes that link them like a rail map (Nakagaki et al. 2000; Tero et al. 2010). The network then starves and dissolves tube by tube into darkness. Fresh flakes drop onto the empty plate, and a new colony starts somewhere else.
- **Model**: the agent-based approximation of Jones (2010). The chemoattractant `T` is a `Grid<f32>` on a capped square lattice `L = grid::lattice_for(Size(w, 2h), 20_000, 1.25)` (CORE-R48). That gives 197×100 at 200×50 and 80×48 at 80×24; below the cap the lattice equals the pixel display, so the view is exact. There are `n = max(1, round(p·lw·lh))` agents with `p = 5 %` (985 and 192), within the population range Jones explored. Each agent has a continuous position and a heading in `Z₁₆` (22.5° units) with a 16-entry `(cos, sin)` table, so no trig runs per step. Its site is its rounded position. Each Jones step is two passes over a fresh Fisher–Yates shuffle of the live agents. **Motor pass**: step `SS = 1` px along the heading. If the target site is on the lattice and unoccupied (particle exclusion, one agent per site), the agent moves and deposits `depT = 5` at its new site. Otherwise it stays, deposits nothing and takes a uniformly random new heading. **Sensory pass**: sample `T` at three sensors at distance `SO = clamp(round(0.09·min(lw, lh)), 3, 9)` px (4 at 80×24, 9 at 200×50) and angles `h − SA, h, h + SA` with `SA = 22.5°`. Off-lattice sensors read −1, so walls repel. The agent then rotates by `RA = 45°` according to Jones's rule: F strictly greatest ⇒ keep the heading; F strictly least ⇒ ±RA by coin; otherwise turn toward the strictly stronger side (FL < FR ⇒ toward R, FR < FL ⇒ toward L); any remaining tie ⇒ keep. **Chemoattractant**: after both passes, `T ← (1 − δ)·mean₃ₓ₃(T)` as a separable 3-tap box filter with replicated (Neumann) edges and `δ = 0.1`. **Food**: `n_f = clamp(round(lw·lh/800), 4, 9)` flakes (5 at 80×24, 9 at 200×50) at seeded positions in the central 80 %. They are at least `0.25·min(lw, lh)` apart and outside the inoculum disc plus SO. Placement uses at most 200 rejection tries and accepts fewer flakes on a crowded plate, including 0. Food discs of radius 1.5 px are pinned to `T = max(T, 20)` (4·depT) during explore and consolidate, which anchors trunk lines between sources as in Tero et al. (2010). **Inoculum**: a disc of radius `√(2n/π)` px (50 % occupancy) centred at a seeded point in the central 40 %, with random headings. Each agent deposits once at seeding, so the colony is visible at t = 0. **Rate**: 60 Jones steps per simulated second (an accumulator, at most 15 per update). This is a time-lapse: the network in Tero et al. took about a day to grow. **Timeline** (48 s, loops): explore 10 s → consolidate 30 s (the physics is unchanged; the network coarsens under its own dynamics while the food holds the trunk lines) → fade 6 s (food unpinned; each agent dies at a seeded uniform time in the phase, and a dead agent stops moving and depositing and frees its site, so tubes thin and snap one by one) → rest 2 s (the trail decays to black, `0.9^120 ≈ 3·10⁻⁶`). At rest start, the next cycle's inoculum centre and food are drawn from `ctx.rng`, and the new flakes fade in. The inoculum is seeded at the cycle boundary. Every cycle differs, and each seed is deterministic.
- **Motion**: agents move 60 px/s and the trail changes every step. Within 1 s the inoculum grows filaments that reach visibly past its rim. At 80×24 the first flake is reached in about 3–6 s, and by 10 s the sources are joined by a web with loops. During consolidation, brightness flickers along the stable tubes as agents stream through them. The fade reads as a slow disintegration, followed by a dark plate where new flakes appear, and then a new bloom.
- **Resize** (ADR-0012): the lattice comes from `lattice_for`. If it is unchanged (same aspect bucket, with both sizes at or above the cap in both axes), only the display grid and `Pixels` are rebuilt, no `ctx.rng` is consumed, and the simulation stays bit-identical. If it changes, `T` is resampled with `grid::resample_bilinear`. Agent positions are scaled by `(lw'/lw, lh'/lh)` and re-inserted into the occupancy grid in index order, and any collisions are dropped. Outside fade and rest, the population is topped up to `round(p·lw'·lh')` with agents placed on free sites where `T` is above the mean, using at most 16 `ctx.rng` tries per agent before skipping it. Food is scaled, SO is recomputed, and the timeline is untouched. Agent, order and occupancy buffers have capacity 1000 (= p·20 000) from `build`.
- **Inks**: the trail uses `Role::Seq` via `ink::seq(0.2 + 0.8·u)`, with `u = min(1, √(T / (2.5·T_ref)))`. `T_ref` starts at depT and is an EMA (τ = 1 s) of the mean `T` over sites above the global mean. It is frozen during fade and rest, so the dying network dims instead of being re-exposed. Starting the ramp at 0.2 skips the colormap's violet floor. In astro the organism shows crimson halos, amber tubes and pale-yellow junctions (Physarum is yellow), which reads differently from the full-range Seq fields of chemical_reaction, belousov_zhabotinsky and liesegang_rings. Pixels with `u < 0.3` stay `Ink::BLANK`, so the plate is the terminal's own dark. Food is `Role::Structure` 255, pale cool oat flakes against the warm organism; new flakes ramp from 60 to 255 over rest. Label `Role::Text` 110. Agents are not drawn: the trail is the organism.
- **ASCII**: half-block `Pixels` (CORE-R42) at `w × 2h`, filled from `T` by `grid::resample_bilinear` into a display-sized `Grid<f32>` and then tone-mapped. The `blit` closure works per cell: either pixel is food ⇒ `@` (Structure); both blank ⇒ untouched; otherwise `m = max(u_top, u_bottom)` maps to `.` (< 0.45), `:` (< 0.65), `+` (< 0.85) or `#`, using the brighter pixel's Seq ink. At 80×24 the snapshots show a `:`/`+` blob with five `@` at t = 0, filaments at t = 1 s, and a `.`/`:` lace with `+`/`#` trunks reaching the `@`s at t = 5 s.
- **Label**: `SA 22.5 RA 45 SO 9` (ASCII, rebuilt in `resize` when SO changes) at the bottom-left, drawn only when `w ≥ 40` and `h ≥ 12`.
- **Perf**: at 200×50 (lattice 197×100, 985 agents, 1 Jones step per update): shuffle ≈ 5 µs; motor and sensory passes ≈ 20–30 µs; separable diffuse-decay with the fused mean and T_ref statistics ≈ 30–40 µs; food pinning < 1 µs; bilinear resample to 200×100 plus tone map ≈ 60–80 µs, all in `update`; `Pixels::blit` of 10 k cells ≈ 40 µs. The total is ≈ 0.15–0.2 ms, against the < 1 ms aim. At dt = 0.25 (15 steps) it is ≈ 1.5 ms. The cost is independent of terminal size above the cap, and steady state is allocation-free.
- **Acceptance**: `PHY-A1` `steer(f, fl, fr, coin)` returns exactly 0 / ±2 (by coin) / +2 / −2 / 0 in Z₁₆ units (+ = toward FR) for the five cases of Jones's rule, exhaustively over all orderings of three values with ties, every entry of the 16-direction table has unit length to 1e-6, and sixteen +1 rotations return to the starting heading; `PHY-A2` `diffuse_decay`: a constant field `c` maps to `(1 − δ)c` (relative error 1e-6); with δ = 0 an interior impulse spreads to exactly its 3×3 block at 1/9 each and conserves the total to 1e-6; for any seeded non-negative field, `max(out) ≤ (1 − δ)·max(in)`, `min(out) ≥ (1 − δ)·min(in)` and no output is negative; `PHY-A3` over a seeded 600-step run on an 80×48 lattice followed by a full fade, after every step the occupancy count equals the live agent count, no two agents share a site and every agent is on the lattice; the live count is constant outside fade, non-increasing within it and 0 at its end; `PHY-A4` network forms (thresholds from a Jones-model prototype): from a seeded inoculum on a 96×48 lattice with 5 food sources, at step 0 no food source touches the inoculum's 8-connected component of {T > 0}; after 600 steps (10 s) the trail's coefficient of variation is ≥ 1.2, the fraction of sites above the mean is in [0.2, 0.45], and the largest 8-connected component of {T > mean} holds ≥ 85 % of those sites and ≥ 4 of the 5 food sources (~0.5 s in debug); `PHY-A5` on a 40×24 display (lattice 40×48) the beats are explore [0, 10) / consolidate [10, 40) / fade [40, 46) / rest [46, 48) s and repeat at 48 s, the total trail at 47.9 s is < 1e-3 of its mean over consolidate, the second cycle's food layout and inoculum centre differ from the first cycle's, and both are identical across two runs with the same seed (< 1.5 s in debug); `PHY-A6` resizing 220×60 → 240×64 (both lattice 197×101) leaves the lattice and `T` bit-identical, and after 60 more steps `T` is bit-identical to a run that never resized; resizing 200×50 → 80×24 (197×100 → 80×48) gives a new max ≤ the old max, a new mean within 20 % of the old, every agent on the lattice with exclusion intact, an unchanged food count and an unchanged beat; resizing 80×24 → 200×50 during consolidate tops the population up to ≥ 90 % of `round(p·197·100)`.

### solar_surface  (Astronomy, M10)
- **Intent**: the Sun up close as a slowly turning ball. The granulated photosphere darkens and reddens toward the limb. A few sunspot groups with dark umbrae and striated penumbrae ride differential rotation from east (left) to west (right). Cool loops, with knots of coronal rain sliding along them, stand bright above the limb as their active regions rotate into view, and cross the disc as dark filaments. The disc fills the height, and the eye goes to the loops at the limb and to the spots. The black sky around it stays empty. At 200×50 the disc is 92 cols × 46 rows with 54 empty cols on each side; at 80×24 it is 44 × 22 with 18 on each side.
- **Model**: the view is a composite: the photosphere in white light, loops and rim as in H-α. The disc is centred with radius `R = min(0.46·P, 0.36·W)` px on `Pixels` (P = 2h), with solar north up and east left. The viewer is fixed in inertial space, so rates are sidereal (from Earth the synodic rate is 0.986 °/day slower). B0, the heliographic latitude of the disc centre, is seeded in [−7.25°, 7.25°]. Each disc pixel gets an observer-frame unit normal n = (dx/R, −dy/R, √(1 − ·)) with μ = n_z, tabulated per resize. *Limb darkening*: `I(μ) = 1 − u(1 − μ)` with u = 0.6 (linear law, visible continuum). *Differential rotation*: `ω(φ) = A + B sin²φ + C sin⁴φ` with A = 14.713, B = −2.396, C = −1.787 °/day sidereal (Snodgrass & Ulrich 1990). Time runs at 0.3 day per simulated second, so the equator turns in 81.6 s and latitude 60° in 100.8 s. Every surface feature advances its longitude by exactly `ω(φ)·0.3·dt` (closed form). *Granulation*: multiplicatively weighted Worley cells from `N_g = clamp(round(4πR²/s_g²), 64, 2400)` feature points on the sphere. The spacing at disc centre is s_g = 3.6 px, ≈ 40× the real ≈ 1.3 Mm spacing, which would be sub-pixel. Each point carries (φ, λ), a lifetime of 6–10 s and a weight w that fades in and out over 1.5 s by smoothstep; a dead point respawns uniformly on the sphere. Per frame, points with μ > 0 are projected and counting-sorted into a screen bucket grid of cell size s_g. Each disc pixel takes F1 and F2 from its 3×3 buckets, with squared distances scaled by m² where `m = 1 + 1.5(1 − w)`. Newborn cells therefore grow from a point and dying ones shrink, with no popping. Each region is a bipolar pair tilted by Joy's law, γ = 0.5|φ|, with the leading spot equatorward and the spots 2δ = 6–10° apart. The region rotates rigidly at ω(φ_AR). *Spots*: angular distance from a spot centre is β ≈ √(2(1 − n·s)) (chord, no trig), with q = β/ρ_p. The umbra is displaced toward disc centre by 0.25 ρ_p sin θ in the sky plane: the Wilson depression, exaggerated ≈ 15× so it reads. Faculae brighten outside the spots as `I *= 1 + 0.35(1 − μ)·exp(−(β/3ρ_p)²)`, which shows only near the limb, as observed. The only per-pixel trig is ψ inside penumbrae. *Loops*: each region has 5 field lines of a horizontal point dipole buried at depth `d_b = 2δR` below its midpoint, so B_z peaks under the spots at ±d_b/2. The dipole axis â runs along the region: `p(θ') = c_d + L sin²θ'(cos θ' â + sin θ' v̂_ψ)`, with fan angle ψ ∈ {0, ±12°, ±24°} about â and `L = d_b + h` for apex heights h = 0.04–0.20 R (≈ 28–140 Mm). Footpoints (|p| = R) are found by bisection at spawn. The 56 samples between them are stored in region-local coordinates and rotated each frame through an orthographic `geom::Camera`. Each loop carries 3 knots of coronal rain or siphon flow that slide from footpoint to footpoint: `σ += dt/T` with T = 5–9 s on display time (like the granules), a seeded direction and Gaussian width 0.05 in σ. A sample is *Emission* if its projected radius is ≥ R, and *Filament* if it lies inside the disc with z ≥ 0; a Filament sample darkens its pixel idempotently to `min(level, seq(0.12 + 0.78·0.8·I_disc))`. Otherwise the sample is *Hidden*. Cool H-α loops are thus bright against the sky and absorb against the disc. A region that has reached +150° and is fully hidden (all spots μ < 0, no Emission or Filament sample) respawns 0–60° further on with new parameters, so nothing pops on the visible disc. *Chromosphere*: a 1 px rim at R < r ≤ R + 0.8, plus spicules of height `1.6·max(0, ½sin(n₁ϑ + 1.3t + φ₁) + ½sin(n₂ϑ − 0.9t + φ₂))` px. Here n₁ = round(2πR/3) and n₂ = round(2πR/2) are whole numbers of lobes (so the pattern closes around the limb), and ϑ is the limb angle.

### strange_attractors  (Physics & Chemistry, M10)
- **Intent**: a slowly turning gallery of five classic chaotic flows: Lorenz's butterfly, Aizawa's shell, Thomas's labyrinth, Halvorsen's propeller and Rössler's band. Each is drawn by a swarm of particles leaving short glowing threads on the braille lattice, so the flow itself builds the shape instead of a plotted curve. Every 12 s the swarm flows into the next attractor, with no cut and no fade to black. Each attractor is framed to its own projected extent, so it fills about 0.88 of the height (or of the width, if that is tighter). Each is also seen from its own best elevation: Halvorsen from above its 3-fold axis, so the three blades read. Threads are continuous at every size and the particle count grows only linearly with the lattice, so 200×50 gains resolution but not clutter. The eye goes first to the bright dense cores (the Lorenz wing eyes, the Aizawa tube), then follows the threads outward. A single corner label is the only text.
- **Model**: model time in *tu*. Gallery: Lorenz (1963) `ẋ = σ(y−x), ẏ = x(ρ−z) − y, ż = xy − βz`, σ = 10, ρ = 28, β = 8/3. Aizawa (1982, Langford-type torus flow) `ẋ = (z−b)x − dy, ẏ = dx + (z−b)y, ż = c + az − z³/3 − (x²+y²)(1+ez) + fzx³`, (a,b,c,d,e,f) = (0.95, 0.7, 0.6, 3.5, 0.25, 0.1). Thomas (1999) `ẋ = sin y − bx` (cyclic), b = 0.18 (λ₁ ≈ 0.038). The popular b = 0.19 is a limit cycle, and at the often-quoted b = 0.1998 and 0.208186 orbits stay on one of several coexisting off-centre attractors. Halvorsen (Sprott 2003) `ẋ = −ax − 4y − 4z − y²` (cyclic), a = 1.27 (λ₁ ≈ 0.82); the popular a = 1.89 is periodic (λ₁ ≈ 0). Rössler (1976) `ẋ = −y − z, ẏ = x + ay, ż = b + z(x − c)`, a = b = 0.2, c = 5.7 (λ₁ ≈ 0.071). Starts are fixed in-basin constants, never random: Lorenz (1, 1, 1), Aizawa (0.1, 0, 0), Thomas (0.1, 0, 0), Halvorsen (−1, 0, 0), Rössler (1, 1, 0). Aizawa has a coexisting non-chaotic attractor: from (0.2, 0.1, 0.5), λ₁ ≈ 0 and the z extent is only 82 % of the chaotic one. Each particle is advanced in f64 with `ode::integrate` (RK4) at `h_max` = 0.01 / 0.02 / 0.1 / 0.005 / 0.02 tu, at a model rate `r_k` = 0.33 / 0.69 / 6.5 / 0.31 / 1.6 tu per simulated second. These rates are measured so the mean normalised speed is 1.2 box half-widths/s for every attractor (the peak is ≈ 9/s in Rössler's fold). That is ≤ 2 substeps per particle per 1/60 s. Samples are stored at bit-reversed indices, so every power-of-two prefix is spread along the scout. Two slots hold the current and next ensemble. The next slot is filled during the first 2 s of each hold (≤ 400 scout steps per update, with any remainder finished at the morph boundary). Morph: at morph start both prefixes are ordered by azimuth about the vertical axis, in place with `sort_unstable_by` and ties broken by index, then paired i↔i. Camera: `geom::Camera` with yaw `2πt/36 s`, pitch `p_k + 0.10 sin(2πt/29 s)` with `p_k` = 0.20 / 0.35 / 0.55 / 0.95 / 0.50, and distance 4.5. The fit is `scale_k = min(0.44·h/E_y,k, 0.46·w/(2·E_x,k))` rows per world unit. Here `E_k` is the ensemble's projected half-extent over the whole camera loop, perspective included: Lorenz (1.03, 0.80), Aizawa (1.06, 0.96), Thomas (0.93, 1.04), Halvorsen (1.00, 1.05), Rössler (0.68, 1.06). ### three_body  (Astronomy, M10)
- **Intent**: three equal suns dancing some of the rare *periodic* solutions of the three-body problem: the figure-8, where all three chase each other along one curve; Lagrange's equilateral triangle spinning and breathing; Broucke's A1 and R4; and Šuvakov & Dmitrašinović's moth I. Each glowing head draws its own comet trail, exactly one period long, so the orbit is sketched once and then keeps refreshing. The loop is a small gallery of 250 years of celestial mechanics, and it is honest about chaos: each orbit restarts from its published initial conditions long before any instability can show.
- **Model**: planar Newtonian three-body problem, `G = m₁ = m₂ = m₃ = 1`, `r̈ᵢ = Σ_{j≠i} (rⱼ − rᵢ)/|rⱼ − rᵢ|³`, unsoftened. The symmetric (Newton 3) pair loop conserves `P` and `L` to rounding. KDK leapfrog (`ode::verlet_step`, CORE-R44) runs on a fixed per-orbit substep lattice `hᵢ = Tᵢ/(480·qᵢ)`, `qᵢ = ⌈4096·Tᵢ/480⌉` (q = 54, 48, 54, 47, 128, so `hᵢ ≤ 1/4096`), and one trail sample is pushed every `qᵢ` substeps. Each update takes `⌊τ·kᵢ/hᵢ⌋ − n_done` whole substeps, where `τ` is the time since the current `run` started, so the state is a pure function of the substep count and does not depend on the frame rate. `verlet_integrate` is not used because it chooses its own equal substeps. State is `pos, vel, acc: [f64; 6]`. The orbits (ICs COM-centred with `P = 0`) are below; `k` is model time units per simulated second and `n` is the number of periods shown:
  - **figure-8** (Moore 1993; Chenciner & Montgomery 2000; ICs by Simó): `r₁ = −r₂ = (0.97000436, −0.24308753)`, `r₃ = 0`, `v₃ = (−0.93240737, −0.86473146)`, `v₁ = v₂ = −v₃/2`, `T = 6.32591398`, `E = −1.287142`, `L = 0`; KAM-stable (Simó 2002); `k = 1`, `n = 3`.
  - **Lagrange** (1772), homographic elliptic variant with `e = 0.3`: the bodies start at apocentre at `90° + 120°·i` on the unit circle, with perpendicular velocity `v_a = √(μ(1−e))` and `μ = Gm/√3` (the net pull on each body is `μ/r²` toward the COM). Each body therefore follows a Kepler ellipse about the COM with `a = 1/1.3`, `r_p = 0.538462`, `T = 2π√(a³√3) = 5.578856`, `E = −1.125833`, `L = 1.907172`. Routh's stability condition `27(m₁m₂ + m₂m₃ + m₃m₁) < (Σm)²` fails for equal masses (81 > 9), so the configuration is linearly unstable (Gascheau 1843, Routh 1875); in the circular case the growth rate is `ω/√2`, ×85 per period. `k = 1`, `n = 2`.
  - **Broucke A1** (Broucke 1975; Hénon 1976; ICs from the Šuvakov–Dmitrašinović gallery): `x = (−0.9892620043, 2.2096177241, −1.2203557197)`, `y = 0`, `v_x = 0`, `v_y = (1.9169244185, 0.1910268738, −2.1079512924)`, `T = 6.283213`, `E = −0.854131`, `L = 1.098206`; `k = 1`, `n = 2`.
  - **Broucke R4**: `x = (0.8733047091, −0.6254030288, −0.2479016803)`, `v_y = (1.0107764436, −1.6833533458, 0.6725769022)`, `T = 5.395826`, `E = −2.054282`, `L = 1.768757`; `k = 1`, `n = 2`.
  - **moth I** (Šuvakov & Dmitrašinović 2013, PRL 110 114301, I.B.1): `r₁ = (−1, 0)`, `r₂ = (1, 0)`, `r₃ = 0`, `v₁ = v₂ = (0.464445, 0.396060)`, `v₃ = −2v₁`, `T = 14.893826`, `E = −1.382282`, `L = 0`, closest approach 0.079; `k = 1.5`, `n = 1`.
  - **Excluded**: butterfly I, yin-yang and goggles pass within 0.01–0.02 of a collision and would need a regularised integrator (at `h = 1/4096`, butterfly I loses 2 % of its energy per period).
- **Motion**:
  - A looping `Timeline` of five `run` phases (`nᵢTᵢ/kᵢ` s), each followed by a 1.2 s `fade`, totalling `Σ nᵢTᵢ/kᵢ + 6.0 ≈ 69.42 s`: fig-8 18.98 s, Lagrange 11.16 s, A1 12.57 s, R4 10.79 s, moth 9.93 s.

### vortex_street  (Physics & Chemistry, M10)
- **Intent**: flow past a cylinder at Re ≈ 150 sheds a von Kármán vortex street. That is the regime of real 2-D laminar shedding: onset is at Re ≈ 47, and 3-D mode A appears above Re ≈ 190. A calm inflow comes in from the left and meets a solid round cylinder a fifth of the way across. Two thin shear layers peel off its top and bottom, flap, and roll up into alternating eddies, clockwise (cyan) in the upper row and counter-clockwise (orange) in the lower row, drifting right at ≈ 0.85 U. The eye lands on the cylinder and then follows the wake. Only the vorticity field, the cylinder and one corner label are drawn; the irrotational flow is the terminal's own background. At 80×24 the cylinder is ≈ 9 columns × 4½ rows centred near column 15 and row 11.5, with 3–4 eddies in view in a band across the middle ten rows. At 200×50 the framing is the same, and the eddies have soft graded cores and the shear layers read as fine filaments.
- **Model**: D2Q9 lattice Boltzmann (Qian, d'Humières & Lallemand 1992) in lattice units (Δx = Δt = 1). Streaming is `f_i(x + c_i, t + 1) = f_i*(x, t)`, with equilibrium `f_i^eq = w_i ρ (1 + 3 c_i·u + 4.5 (c_i·u)² − 1.5 u²)`, weights `w = 4/9, 1/9 (×4), 1/36 (×4)` and `c_s² = 1/3`. **Collision**: two-relaxation-time (TRT; Ginzburg, Verhaeghe & d'Humières 2008), `f_i* = f_i − ω⁺(f_i⁺ − f_i^{eq+}) − ω⁻(f_i⁻ − f_i^{eq−})`, where `f_i^± = (f_i ± f_ī)/2` pairs opposite directions, `ω⁺ = 1/τ`, `ν = (τ − ½)/3`, and `ω⁻ = 1/τ⁻` with magic parameter `Λ = (τ − ½)(τ⁻ − ½) = 1/4`. BGK (Bhatnagar, Gross & Krook 1954) is the special case `τ⁻ = τ`, but at τ = 0.53 BGK leaves the odd (ghost) modes almost undamped and blows up in under-resolved shear layers. TRT has the same ν(τ) and a similar cost. The kernel is a fused pull-scheme stream+collide in f32 over SoA planes. Populations are stored as `f_i − w_i` (Lehmann et al. 2022) so the O(Ma²) deviations keep their precision, and ρ, uₓ, u_y are written out as a by-product. **Lattice**: the lattice does not depend on terminal size. The display's square-pixel aspect `A = w/(2h)` is clamped to [1, 4] and widened by the hidden sponge (`A/0.9`). It is passed to `grid::lattice_for` as a virtual display large enough to reach the cap (`MAX_NODES = 10 240`, `BUCKET = 1.12`), so every terminal in one aspect bucket runs identical physics: 150×68 at 200×50 and 134×76 at 80×24. The view shows lattice columns 2 … W_vis − 1 with `W_vis = W − ⌈0.1 W⌉`, which hides the inlet columns and the sponge. For display aspects in [1, 4] its aspect is within 7 % of the display's, so the cylinder stays round; outside that range the view is stretched. **Geometry**: `D = clamp(round(0.19 H), 6, 16)` (blockage ≤ 0.2), centre `(x_c, y_c) = (round(0.2 W_vis), H/2)`, staircase mask. y is periodic, so the domain is a column of cylinders H ≈ 5.3 D apart. The inlet (column 0) is set to `f^eq(1, (U, 0))` every step, and the outlet (column W − 1) copies column W − 2. Over the hidden last ⌈0.1 W⌉ columns a sponge (Israeli & Orszag 1981) adds relaxation toward `f^eq(1, (U, 0))`, with strength ramping quadratically from 0 to 0.25. The cylinder wall uses halfway bounce-back (Ladd 1994). **Parameters**: `τ = 0.53` (ν = 0.01) and `U = min(0.125, 1.5/D)`, so `Re = U D/ν = min(150, 12.5 D)` and `Ma = U√3 ≤ 0.217`. Re = 150 whenever D ≥ 12, which covers both reference lattices (D = 13 and 14) and every display aspect up to ≈ 2.3. At the widest bucket (D = 9) Re is 112.5. **Start**: the flow starts from an analytic street, so a street exists at t = 0. The background is potential flow past the cylinder, `u = U(1 − R²(x² − y²)/r⁴, −2R² x y/r⁴)` with R = D/2, which is rational and irrotational and avoids an impulsive jolt at the wall. On top of it sit two rows of Kaufmann–Scully vortices, `v_θ = Γ r/(2π(r² + r_c²))` (rational, so no libm), with `Γ = −1.8 U D` in the upper row (clockwise) and `+1.8 U D` in the lower row, and `r_c = 0.35 D`. The streamwise spacing is `a = 4.3 D` (≈ 0.85 U/f at St ≈ 0.2) and the row gap is `b = 0.281 a` (von Kármán's stable ratio). Each vortex is summed with its periodic images at y ± H. The rows run from `x_c + 1.5 D` to the outlet, with a seeded phase in `[0, a)` and a seeded leading row. Initially ρ = 1, u = 0 in the solid, and `f = f^eq(ρ, u)`. **Watchdog**: once per simulated second, any non-finite value or ρ outside [0.7, 1.3] re-initialises the flow from the analytic street. It never fires in normal operation (VOR-A5, VOR-A6).
- **Motion**: `STEPS_PER_S = 270` lattice steps per simulated second: 4.5 per 60 Hz frame through a fractional accumulator, and at most 68 per `update` at dt = 0.25. The shedding period `P ≈ D/(St U)` is ≈ 560–650 steps, or ≈ 2.1–2.4 s. Eddies advect ≈ 25 lattice nodes/s, which is ≈ 39 columns/s at 200×50 and ≈ 16 columns/s at 80×24, so motion is obvious within 1 s. The flow is a statistically stationary limit cycle, so it loops forever without a Timeline, and each seed opens on a different street phase.
- **Resize**: `lattice_size(display)` decides everything. If the lattice is unchanged (same aspect bucket), only the view mapping and `Pixels` are rebuilt and the populations stay bit-identical. On a bucket change, ρ, uₓ and u_y are resampled onto the new lattice with `grid::resample_bilinear` (ADR-0012). Velocities are then scaled by `U'/U` and `ρ − 1` by `(U'/U)²`, so the flow stays in the new inlet's units. Solid nodes of the new cylinder get `u = 0`, `FlowParams` are recomputed, `f = f^eq(ρ, u)` is rebuilt and the probe history is cleared. Non-equilibrium populations are never resampled. The first resize initialises the flow from the analytic street. Every buffer (lattice planes, vorticity grid, pixel-size view grid, `Pixels`) is sized here. The view is refreshed at the end of both `resize` and `update`, so `render` only blits and writes the label.
- **Inks**: vorticity `ω = ∂ₓu_y − ∂_y uₓ` (y up on screen) is taken by central differences on the visible lattice and normalised as `x = ω D/(2.5 U)`. It is soft-clipped with the [3/2] Padé tanh `s = clamp(x(27 + x²)/(27 + 9x²), −1, 1)`, which is monotone and exactly 1 at |x| = 3, so no transcendental function runs per pixel. The vorticity grid is resampled bilinearly to pixel size, and each pixel gets `ink::div(s)` (`Role::Div`): CW (upper row, s < 0) is cyan and CCW (lower row) is orange. Pixels with |s| < 0.06 stay `Ink::BLANK`. The Scully core peak `ω_max = Γ/(π r_c²) ≈ 4.7 U/D` maps to |s| ≈ 0.95, so eddy cores are graded while the thin shear layers saturate. Cylinder pixels are those whose centre, mapped into lattice coordinates, lies within D/2 of (x_c, y_c), so the outline is round rather than the staircase. They are `Role::Structure` level 200. The label is `Role::Text` level 150.
- **ASCII**: the `Pixels::blit` closure decides per cell. Any `Structure` half gives `@` (the cylinder). Otherwise `s` is the mean of the non-empty `Div` halves: |s| < 0.25 gives `.`, s < 0 gives `x` (|s| < 0.6) or `X`, and s > 0 gives `o` or `O`, each with the averaged `Div` ink. A cell with both halves empty is left untouched. Snapshots show an `@` disc, an `x/X` upper row and an `o/O` lower row alternating downstream on a blank field.
- **Label**: `Re≈150` (ASCII `Re~150`, Re rounded to the nearest 5) at column 1 of row 0, above the calm inflow. At width ≥ 40, `  St≈0.21` (ASCII `St~0.21`) is appended once ≥ 3 upward zero-crossings of u_y, with ±0.02 U hysteresis, have been recorded at the probe `(x_c + 2.5 D, y_c)`. `St = D/(U·P̄)`, where P̄ is the mean of the last ≤ 4 crossing intervals, kept in a fixed ring. There is no label below 16 columns or 6 rows, and it is never clipped. It is written through a stack buffer.
- **Perf**: 10 200 nodes × 4.5 steps ≈ 46 k lattice updates per frame. The interior uses a branch-free row-sliced kernel as in `gs_step`; only the cylinder's bounding rows and the inlet, outlet and sponge columns take the special paths. At 60–100 MLUPS single-threaded that is 0.45–0.75 ms. Vorticity takes ≈ 0.02 ms, resampling to 20 k pixels ≈ 0.08 ms, ink mapping ≈ 0.04 ms and the blit ≈ 0.05 ms, for ≈ 0.65–0.95 ms at 200×50. 80×24 costs the same because the lattice does not depend on display size (GEN-R8 is 5 ms). `render` and steady `update` do not allocate. Flag for the maintainer: every `update(1/60)` costs ≈ 46 k lattice updates at any size, including 1×1, so the debug contract, props and snapshot suites spend ≈ 50–60 M lattice updates on this scene. If `rs-test` slows noticeably, set `[profile.dev.package.sciart-generators] opt-level = 1`.
