### binary_inspiral  (Deep Cosmos, M21)
- **Intent**: two black holes spiral together; the gravitational waves they radiate stream out as a two-armed spiral that tightens and brightens until the merger, then a ringdown fades to calm.
- **Model**: quadrupole (Peters) inspiral of an equal-mass binary in model units: separation `a(t)` with `a⁴ = a₀⁴ − 4βt`, orbital phase `Φ` integrated from Kepler `ω = √(M/a³)`; GW phase `2Φ` and amplitude `∝ ω^{2/3}`. The plus-polarisation strain seen face-on at screen radius `r`, angle `θ` is `h₊(r, θ, t) ∝ A(t − r/c) · cos(2θ − 2Φ(t − r/c)) / max(r, r₀)`, read from a ring buffer of `(Φ, A)` per step (retarded time), so the arms carry the past chirp outward. At contact (`a ≤ a_min`): merger flash, then ringdown `A ∝ e^{−t/τ}` at fixed frequency for ~2 s, a quiet pause, and the loop restarts (~20 s per loop).
- **Motion**: the arms rotate at the GW frequency, ever faster and tighter; the two holes close in.
- **Resize**: the retarded-time buffer is in model time (unchanged); screen scale and per-dot fixed thresholds rebuilt.
- **Look** *(GEN-R26)*: the strain field as fixed-threshold stipple (`raster::stipple_keep`), density `0.45 · |h₊| / h_ref` capped at 0.45, `Hot` where `h₊ > 0`, `Cold` where `h₊ < 0`, beyond an inner radius only; the two holes are small solid `Star` 255 stipple atoms (`Dots::stipple_puff`) with short `Accent` orbit threads (afterglow ≈ ½ orbit). The merger is one brief `Sun` 255 puff. A small chirp trace (the strain at a fixed observer over the last few seconds, a 1-px `Wave` thread) sits right-aligned just above row `h − 1`, dropped when narrow.
- **Title**: `Black hole merger · rising chirp`; captions `inspiral`, `merger`, `ringdown`.
- **Stylisation**: Newtonian orbit with quadrupole radiation reaction, no spin, no higher post-Newtonian terms; the ringdown is a single damped mode; the wave speed is slowed to fit the screen; merger and ringdown frequencies are illustrative; the merger flash marks the peak of gravitational-wave emission (merging black holes emit no light).
- **Acceptance**: `BBH-A1` during `inspiral` the separation falls and the GW frequency rises monotonically (a chirp), and `a⁴` falls linearly in time within 1 %; `BBH-A2` the drawn field has quadrupole symmetry: `h₊(r, θ + π) = h₊(r, θ)` and `h₊(r, θ + π/2) = −h₊(r, θ)` at equal retarded time; `BBH-A3` the strain at radius `r` equals the source value delayed by `r/c` (retarded-time buffer); `BBH-A4` `caption()` runs `inspiral` → `merger` → `ringdown` and loops; `BBH-A5` dots are `Hot` only where `h₊ > 0` and `Cold` only where `h₊ < 0`, and stipple density never exceeds 0.45.
- **As built**: contact at `a_min = 0.4 a₀` (≈ 4× frequency sweep); loop 14 s inspiral + 0.8 s merger + 5.2 s ringdown (τ = 0.9 s, mode frequency 1.5× contact); the scene opens 3 s into the inspiral with the arms already out. Wave speed 0.5 field radii/s; field from `r₀ = 0.2` to 1 field radius (0.45 of the shorter side), easing out over the outer 20 %. Density `0.45 · min(A·r₀/r / (0.85 h_ref), 1) · |cos(2θ − 2Φ)|³` (`h_ref = 0.35`; the cube sharpens crests into arms), ≤ 0.45, signed by `h₊`; a cell never mixes `Hot` and `Cold`; where the envelope is weak the arms keep their density and step down in level (230/180/130/90). Holes σ 1.3 (remnant 1.5) with a solid core; each hole's afterglow covers 0.25 orbit. Within GEN-R26's 10 % changed cells (no exception).

### black_hole  (Deep Cosmos, M5, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: an Interstellar-style black hole: a dark shadow, a thin bright photon ring, and a tilted accretion disk whose far side is lensed up over the shadow; the approaching side glows brighter.
- **Model**: shadow radius `rs = R(0.14)`; ISCO `r_in = 2.2 rs`; disk out to `R(0.95)`; inclination 78° (disk ellipse flattening `cos i`); particles on circular orbits `ω(r) = kepler_omega(gm, r)` with the ISCO period fixed at 2.5 s; relativistic beaming for shading only: level ∝ `(1 + 0.55·β_los)³` with `β_los` the line-of-sight velocity fraction. Particles slowly drift inward (`dr/dt = −0.02 r`); inside `r_in` they plunge and are respawned at the outer edge. The far half of the disk is drawn at its weak-field lensed image positions (`θE² = 2·R_s·depth`): the bright primary is lifted into an arc over the shadow, a faint secondary appears below; there is no separate unlensed copy. Pool `clamp(area/6, 24, 1600)`, initially uniform in `ln r` (the steady state of the drift).
- **Inks**: disk `Hot` (level from radius and beaming), photon ring `Accent`, lensed arc `Hot` dimmer, shadow = blank.
- **Acceptance**: `BH-A1` ω strictly decreases with r and the ISCO period is 2.5 s; `BH-A2` no disk particle is drawn inside the shadow when it is behind the hole (far side, projected distance < rs); `BH-A3` the approaching half is brighter on average than the receding half; `BH-A4` the pool size stays constant after warm-up (respawn, no growth).

### comet_breakup  (Sun & Planets, M13, M15, M16)
- **Intent**: Shoemaker–Levy 9: a comet grazes Jupiter inside the Roche limit and is torn into a string of pearls; two years later (compressed) the fragments strike one after another, raising fireballs on the limb and leaving dark scars.

Act 2: each fragment's impact at 60 km/s spawns a `plume` (shared) fireball on the limb and a persistent dark scar that Jupiter's rotation carries round.
- **Motion**: `Timeline`: approach (2 s) → breakup (12 s) → string of pearls (12 s, the view follows the chain) → impacts (16 s) → scars (6 s), with orbit time advancing 0.7, 0.9 and 1.1 τ per second in the first three acts from τ = −2.6 (the approach is short so the tide visibly stretches the pile by t ≈ 5 s). Roche distance 2.5 R_J in the model's units, perijove 1.33 R_J. On the run-in the chain's spacing is shrunk if needed so the last fragment strikes by 13 s into the impacts act.

### comet_tails  (Sun & Planets, M13, M15)
- **Intent** *(M15, ADR-0018: flat-stepped tails on black, the white head the accent)*: a bright comet near the Sun (the Sun itself off frame, not drawn): a curved, fanned yellow dust tail and a straight, narrow blue ion tail of fine rippling streamers pointing straight away from the Sun, which occasionally snaps off and regrows (a disconnection event).
- **Model**: dust grains released from the nucleus with small velocity spreads; each moves on an analytic two-body orbit about the Sun under reduced gravity `μ(1 − β)`, β log-uniform in [0.04, 0.9] (< 1 keeps gravity attractive; β > r/2a ≈ 0.015–0.03 everywhere on the shown arc, so every grain is on a hyperbolic orbit), propagated in closed form with the universal-variable Kepler solution (Newton, ≤ 3 iterations warm-started from the previous frame; the scene's own `Orbit`, since core has no Kepler-equation solver). Grains of one β form a syndyne, grains of one release time a synchrone. Dust pool 8000, grains dropped after 20 s; activity ∝ r^{−3/2}. ### cosmic_microwave_background  (Deep Cosmos, M4, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: the Planck-style oval sky map: smooth hot/cold anisotropies that slowly shimmer (not per-frame noise).
- **Model**: an isotropic Gaussian random field by spectral synthesis, `f = (1/N) Σ sin(kᵢ·(x/ASPECT, y) + φᵢ + ωᵢ t)` with N = 96 equal-power modes; `|kᵢ|` stratified-sampled so their density in `ln ℓ` follows `D_ℓ·B_ℓ²` — `D_ℓ` an approximate Planck 2018 ΛCDM TT spectrum (Sachs–Wolfe plateau, first acoustic peak at ℓ = 220 ≈ 5× plateau, trough, 2nd/3rd peaks, damping), `B_ℓ² = exp(−(ℓ/400)²)` a "cell beam", ℓ ∈ [10, 760] — mapped to the screen as `|k| = (2π/6)·ℓ/220` rad/row (first peak = 6-row wavelength); directions from a jittered golden-ratio sequence over [0, π) along the ℓ-sorted modes; phases uniform; `|ωᵢ| ∈ [0.08, 0.35]` rad/s with random sign (slow shimmer, no drift); display saturates at `|f| = 2.7σ`, `σ = 1/√(2N)` (Planck colour bar ±300 μK ≈ ±2.7 σ_CMB); drawn only inside an ellipse covering 94 % of the screen (Mollweide-like); caption `CMB  T=2.725K  dT~1e-5` on the last row when `h > 6`.
- **Inks**: `f > 0` ⇒ `Hot`, else `Cold`, level ∝ `min(|f|/2.7σ, 1)`.
- **Acceptance**: `CMB-A1` `|f| ≤ 1`; `CMB-A2` smooth — horizontally adjacent cells differ by at most `Σ|kₓᵢ| / (ASPECT·N)`, and adjacent-cell correlation is ≥ 0.9 horizontally and ≥ 0.7 vertically; `CMB-A3` a 200×50 frame contains both hot and cold cells; `CMB-A4` isotropic, not stripy — the direction tensor `Σ k̂k̂ᵀ` has anisotropy ≤ 0.05 and the two-point correlation at equal aspect-corrected lags agrees across directions within 0.1 (mode ensemble at 0°/90°/±45°, 1–6 rows; and sampled on a 200×50 screen, horizontal vs vertical, 1–3 rows); `CMB-A5` CMB-like spectrum — `D_ℓ` peaks at ℓ ∈ [200, 240] with `D_220/D_10 ∈ [4.5, 6]`, a trough then a second peak, and damping; the most populated Δln ℓ = 0.5 band of modes is centred at ℓ ∈ [140, 300].

### cosmic_web  (Deep Cosmos, M13, M16)
- **Intent** *(M14: a thin web on black; M15, ADR-0018: in M11's stepped `Seq`)*: a faint mottling in the dark gathers under gravity into a connected web of faint links, thin filaments and small bright knots: the cosmic web as seen in the Millennium simulation, then gently dissolves and replays.
- **Model**: Zel'dovich approximation `x = q + D(t)·s(q)`, `s = −∇ψ`, with ψ a sum of 48 plane waves (spectrum `P_δ(k) ∝ k^{-2}` over wavelengths of 1/22 to 1/1.6 of the box, periodic, seeded) built on resize as separable sin/cos tables along each axis (the CMB trick, no FFT). One particle per node of the display's pixel lattice capped at 16k (ADR-0012); the next cycle's displacement table is filled a chunk per step. Adhesion-like freeze: each particle stops at its own shell crossing, `x = q + min(D, D_c(q))·s(q)` with `D_c = −1/λ_min(∂s/∂q)`, so pancakes and filaments stay sharp instead of streaming through and smearing back to uniform; `D_max` is 2.5× the first shell crossing (`CROSSING_AT` = 0.4). Periodic wrap and a slow pan across the box. Particles are deposited cloud-in-cell into the lattice, blurred (three `[1 2 1]` passes) where sparse and kept sharp where matter has gathered, sampled to the pixels and tone-mapped with an asinh `Tone` over the density minus a floor, then a gamma 2.5, so the voids end in black on a smooth ramp (no dither). *(M14)* The floor and the scale adapt to each frame: the floor is the density above which 22 % of the lattice lies (at least the mean, 1), the tone's white the density above which 0.3 % lies, and the densest 0.4 % are the knots (white at the densest 0.15 %); so the web shows at every stage (the early fog as faint mottling, the collapsed web as links, filaments and knots). The quantiles come from one fine histogram (2048 bins over densities 0–32) interpolated inside the bin, so they are smooth in time (no pumping from frame to frame) and independent of the render cadence.
- **Motion**: `D(t)` rises over 20 s (fast at first: a faint web, then sheets → filaments → knots), creeps on for 5 s while the view pans, then eases back over 5 s to `D = 0`, where the next cycle's phase-shifted modes take over invisibly. The scene starts at `D ≈ 0.3 D_max`.
- **Resize**: modes stay in box units; particle grid, sin/cos tables and buffers rebuilt for the size.
- **Inks** *(M15, ADR-0018: M11's stepped field; was a smooth `Ramp(Plasma)`)*: a brightness `b` (0–255) as before: faint links at about 20, easing in from black by `smoothstep(t/0.12)` of the toned value `t`, rising as `t^2.5` to 110 in the filaments' cores; the knots continue from 110 at the knot threshold (the densest 0.4 %) to 250 at the densest 0.15 % of the frame. It is drawn as a `Seq` field in four flat steps, like M11's reaction fields: `b` ≥ 15 → `Seq` 60 (violet links), ≥ 50 → 115 (red filaments), ≥ 112 → 180 (orange halos), ≥ 200 → 235 (the densest knots, yellow); below 15 black. ASCII by level.
- **Look** *(M16, GEN-R25; supersedes the toned `Seq` field above)*: the particles themselves are drawn, one braille dot each (the same Zel'dovich positions, frozen at shell crossing, panned and wrapped), on black; no fill. The density lattice is still deposited and smoothed, but only to ink each particle by the density where it sits, in four classes ranked so a brighter one wins a shared braille cell: knot halos (the densest 0.6 % of the lattice, and at least 3× the mean) `Sun` 245; filaments (the densest 5 %, at least 2.2×) `Accent` 200; sheets (≥ 1.4×) `Accent` 110; the rest is fog (or a void's last stragglers), of which a fixed 40 % (a per-particle hash) is drawn `Accent` 40. So the early fog is a faint stipple that collapses into magenta sheets and filaments with yellow knots, and the voids empty to black. Each particle is drawn with a fixed sub-cell jitter (≤ 0.45 lattice cells, drawing only), a glass-like start that breaks the start lattice's rows. No trails: the web's motion is a slow drift. ASCII: by braille count. *(M16 review)*: while the web is diffuse the sheets are thinned too: a sheet particle is drawn only if its fixed hash is below `0.5 + 0.5·smoothstep((d − 0.35)/0.35)` of the growth `d` (in `D_max`), so the early fog is stipple, not a blanket.
- **Title**: `Cosmic web · gravity gathers`.
- **Stylisation**: Zel'dovich (first-order Lagrangian) flow with particles frozen at shell crossing (adhesion-like, not the adhesion model's viscous limit); a 2-D flow, not a slice of 3-D; the loop runs time backwards to restart (stated).

### galaxy_collision  (Deep Cosmos, M5, M12, M15, M19)
- **Intent**: two spiral galaxies passing each other and pulling out tidal tails and bridges (Toomre & Toomre 1972).

### globular_cluster  (Deep Cosmos, M10, M12, M15, M16)
- **Intent**: an ancient globular cluster seen through a slowly turning telescope. A blazing, unresolved core thins into a sparse halo of single stars, with a handful of orange giants glinting among them. Every visible halo star is a live gravitating body: the swarm breathes, and the camera's slow turn gives it depth through parallax. It is calm, dense and three-dimensional, the stellar counterpart to the flat `starfield` and the disk-shaped `galaxy_collision`.
- **Model**:
  - **Units and softening**: direct N-body in Hénon units (`G = M = 1`, virial radius 1, `E ≈ −1/4`), equal masses `m = 1/N`, Plummer softening `Φ_ij = −G m²/√(r² + ε²)` with `ε = 0.05` (0.085 of the Plummer scale radius `a = 3π/16`).

  - **ICs**: Plummer sphere `ρ ∝ (1 + r²/a²)^{−5/2}`, sampled per Aarseth, Hénon & Wielen (1974) in `G = M = a = 1`:
    - Radius `r = (X₁^{−2/3} − 1)^{−1/2}` with mass cut `X₁ < 0.999` (so `r ≤ 38.7 a`).
    - Isotropic directions (`z = 1 − 2u`, `φ = 2πu`).

    - COM position and velocity subtracted.
    - Positions scaled so the unsoftened `W = −1/2` (then `a ≈ 3π/16` and `r_h ≈ 0.769`).
    - The density centre moved to the origin: 4 passes that each subtract the mean position of the bodies inside the half-mass radius. The heavy tail (`r_rms ≈ 3.0 a` with the 0.999 cut) puts the COM ≈ `r_rms/√N` off the body core (0.08 units at N = 500, 0.22 at N = 64). The view and the glow are centred on the origin, so without this the core would sit off the glow and swing around it as the camera turns. It is a translation, so `W` and the dynamics are unchanged, and the momentum stays zero, so the cluster does not drift.
    - Velocities scaled so `2K = |W_ε|` (virial equilibrium in the softened potential), giving `E ≈ −0.248`.
  - **N**: the largest bucket in `{64, 96, 128, 192, 256, 384, 500}` that is `≤ max(area/5, 64)`. 80×24 gives 384, and 200×50 and anything larger gives 500 (the ADR-0012 cap).

Here `r_h` is the body system's 3-D half-mass radius about the origin, smoothed with `τ = 4 s` and reset at every reseed, so the glow follows the real core contraction.

  - Bodies orbit in the self-consistent potential: the core crosses in about 2 s and halo stars drift slowly.

### relativistic_jet  (Deep Cosmos, M13, M16)
- **Intent**: the jet of M87 streaming from a bright galaxy core into a radio lobe: bright knots race outward at what looks like several times the speed of light and pulse, a hotspot and bow shock glow where the jet stops, and the counter-jet is invisible while its (unbeamed) lobe still shows faintly.

### solar_eruption  (Sun & Planets, M13, M15, M16, M17)
- **Intent** *(M15, ADR-0018: thin lines on black in M11's complementary colours)*: the Sun's limb: a quiet arcade of thin glowing loops over a black disc edged by a thin red chromosphere, then a twisted flux rope rises, field lines snap and reconnect beneath it, two flare ribbons flash at the footpoints, a coronal mass ejection balloons out, and bright coronal rain drains back down the new loops.

### solar_surface  (Sun & Planets, M10, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: the Sun up close as a slowly turning ball. The granulated photosphere darkens and reddens toward the limb. A few sunspot groups with dark umbrae and striated penumbrae ride differential rotation from east (left) to west (right). Cool loops, with knots of coronal rain sliding along them, stand bright above the limb as their active regions rotate into view, and cross the disc as dark filaments. The disc fills the height, and the eye goes to the loops at the limb and to the spots. The black sky around it stays empty. At 200×50 the disc is 92 cols × 46 rows with 54 empty cols on each side; at 80×24 it is 44 × 22 with 18 on each side.
- **Model**: the view is a composite: the photosphere in white light, loops and rim as in H-α. The disc is centred with radius `R = min(0.46·P, 0.36·W)` px on `Pixels` (P = 2h), with solar north up and east left. The viewer is fixed in inertial space, so rates are sidereal (from Earth the synodic rate is 0.986 °/day slower). B0, the heliographic latitude of the disc centre, is seeded in [−7.25°, 7.25°]. Each disc pixel gets an observer-frame unit normal n = (dx/R, −dy/R, √(1 − ·)) with μ = n_z, tabulated per resize. *Limb darkening*: `I(μ) = 1 − u(1 − μ)` with u = 0.6 (linear law, visible continuum). *Differential rotation*: `ω(φ) = A + B sin²φ + C sin⁴φ` with A = 14.713, B = −2.396, C = −1.787 °/day sidereal (Snodgrass & Ulrich 1990). Time runs at 0.3 day per simulated second, so the equator turns in 81.6 s and latitude 60° in 100.7 s. Every surface feature advances its longitude by exactly `ω(φ)·0.3·dt` (closed form). *Granulation*: multiplicatively weighted Worley cells from `N_g = clamp(round(4πR²/s_g²), 64, 2400)` feature points on the sphere. The spacing at disc centre is `s_g = clamp(0.085 R, 3.6, 5)` px (3.91 px at R = 46, ≈ 45× the real ≈ 1.3 Mm spacing, which would be sub-pixel), so a cell spans 2–3 terminal cells and lanes read as polygons rather than a blocky maze. Each point carries (φ, λ), a lifetime of 6–10 s and a weight w that fades in and out over 1.5 s by smoothstep; a dead point respawns uniformly on the sphere. Per frame, points with μ > 0 are projected and counting-sorted into a screen bucket grid of cell size s_g. Each disc pixel takes F1 and F2 from the sites within s_g (its 3×3 buckets), widening the query to 1.5 s_g for the few pixels whose F2 reaches s_g, with squared distances scaled by m² where `m = 1 + 1.5(1 − w)`. Newborn cells therefore grow from a point and dying ones shrink, with no popping. The pattern is `g = 0.55·smoothstep(0, 0.3 s_g, F2 − F1) + 0.45·w₁·(1 − min(F1/0.9 s_g, 1))²`, giving bright granule cores and dark intergranular lanes. It is applied as `I *= 1 + (G − 1)·gate` with the granulation factor `G = 1 + C(μ)(2g − 1)`, `C = 0.12·√μ·smoothstep(0.1, 0.2, μ)`, and `gate` the product of the spots' photosphere weights (0 inside a spot, 1 outside, see *Spots*): convection is suppressed in umbrae and penumbrae, so a spot is a clean dark ring on the mottle rather than a dot on tiles. The real rms contrast is ≈ 0.13 at 500 nm and falls toward the limb; 0.12 keeps granule cores below the ramp's clip, so the mottle is a fine texture under the spots and loops, and the smoothstep avoids a hard ring. Worley is skipped for μ < 0.1. Granules evolve on display time, because real 5–10 min lifetimes would flicker at 0.3 d/s, but they rotate with the surface, so the texture itself shows the sphere turning and shearing. *Active regions* (3): mean latitude seeded in 10–25° (Spörer), ±5° per region, either hemisphere, ~120° apart in longitude. The first region starts at −78° (just inside the east limb) with its tallest loop at h = 0.32 R, so loops show at t = 0. Each region is a bipolar pair tilted by Joy's law, γ = 0.5|φ|, with the leading spot equatorward and the spots 2δ = 13–15° apart, so the pair's blended penumbrae (out to 1.07 ρ_p each) never touch: no δ-spots, which are a few % of real groups. Penumbral radius ρ_p is 4–6° for the leading spot and 2.5–4° for the following one, which is absent 40 % of the time. The region rotates rigidly at ω(φ_AR). *Spots*: angular distance from a spot centre is β ≈ √(2(1 − n·s)) (chord, no trig), with q = β/ρ_p. The umbra is 0.22 for q ≤ 0.375 and blends by smoothstep to the penumbra over q ∈ [0.375, 0.525]. Its radius of 0.45 ρ_p gives U/(U+P) ≈ 0.20 and U/P ≈ 0.25, as observed. The penumbra is `0.70 + 0.08 cos(nψ + ψ₀)` (a little darker than the observed ≈ 0.75–0.85, so that with the granulation gated out the ring stands about one ramp step below the intergranular lanes, 0.88, at 80×24) with `n = clamp(round(2π ρ_px / 3), 6, 24)` radial filaments (one per 3 px of penumbral circumference, so they never alias below a pixel; real penumbrae hold hundreds) and blends to 1 over q ∈ [0.93, 1.07]. The umbra is displaced toward disc centre by 0.10 ρ_p sin θ in the sky plane: the Wilson depression, ≈ 2.5× the real d/ρ_p ≈ 0.04 (≈ 10× in absolute depth, since spots are drawn 2–3× oversize). Lifted onto the sphere it grows as 0.10 tan θ, so the centre-side penumbra narrows toward the limb and is covered below μ ≈ 0.2 (≈ 0.08 at the real depth). Faculae brighten outside the spots as `I *= 1 + 0.35(1 − μ)·exp(−(β/3ρ_p)²)`, which shows only near the limb, as observed. Each spot acts only within its own facular cap `cos β ≥ cos 6.5 ρ_p` (where the Gaussian is < 1 %), and faculae never brighten a spot pixel, whatever the spot order. The only per-pixel trig is ψ inside penumbrae. Small discs floor the drawn spot size: each spot is drawn at `ρ_eff = max(ρ_p, min(2.0 px/R, sep/2.2))`, with sep the pair's angular separation, so at 80×24 (R ≈ 22 px, where a 2.5° following spot is ≈ 1 px in penumbral radius and its umbra under half a pixel) every spot is ≥ 2 px with an umbra ≥ 0.9 px and reads as umbra and penumbra. At 200×50 the floor (2.49°) is under every ρ_p, so no spot is enlarged. A field line is `p(θ') = c_d + L sin²θ'(cos θ' â + sin θ' ẑ)` with `L = d_b + h`; the apex heights are `h_max·{1, 0.72, 0.48}` (floored at 0.04 R) with `h_max` = 0.20–0.32 R (first region 0.32 R): 139–223 Mm, ≈ 1–1.5× the tallest observed cool H-α and coronal-rain loops, a stated display exaggeration like the granules and the Wilson shift, so the arcade is the focal point at 80×24 (≈ 7 px) and still fits the empty columns beside the disc. Loops with an apex below 2.5 px are not drawn, and on discs with R < 30 px (80×24 is 22) the middle arch is dropped so the outer and inner arches keep a black gap. ### spacetime_grid  (Deep Cosmos, M6, M12, M15, M16, M17)

### stellar_nursery  (Deep Cosmos, M5, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: a glowing emission nebula with dark dust pillars where gas streams onto protostars that ignite, flare bipolar jets, then shine.

### supernova_remnant  (Deep Cosmos, M13, M15, M16, M17)
- **Intent** *(M15, ADR-0018: M11's idiom, on black)*: a line-colour portrait of a young remnant like Cassiopeia A: a thin, dim blast-wave line racing outward, and inside it a filamentary shell of ejecta (silicon, iron and oxygen patches) whose Rayleigh–Taylor fingers reach out, with knots lighting up as the reverse shock reaches them.
- **Model**: forward-shock radius `R_f(t)` from the ejecta-dominated `t^{4/7}` (Chevalier 1982, n = 7) to Sedov–Taylor `t^{2/5}`, a smooth blend (Truelove–McKee style), drawn as a one-pixel line (antialiased inward; *M14*: the outward falloff band is gone); reverse shock `R_r` easing from 0.62 to 0.45 `R_f` over the expansion. The whole remnant is out of round at low angular order where the surrounding gas is lumpy: a shape factor `1 + 0.09·w(θ, t)`, `w` a sum of modes `m` = 2, 3, 4 (relative amplitudes 1, 0.6, 0.35; `m` = 2 dominant, a lopsided shell) normalised to `[−1, 1]`, their phases drifting slowly (≤ 0.05 rad/s), scales `R_f`, `R_c`, `R_r` and the knots alike, so their ordering holds at every angle. - **Motion**: the blast wave expands over a 36 s loop (fast at first; model time starts at 0.3, so the radius grows ~1.7× between the scene's 1 s and 5 s) from a flash to 0.9 of the half-height, then the remnant fades over 4 s and a new one starts from a flash; the scene opens ~5.4 s after a flash (15 % into the expansion, so the first frame is a grown remnant); fingers grow; knots light and fade continuously.
- **Resize**: modes and knots in units of `R_f`; tables and buffers rebuilt.
- **Inks** *(M15, ADR-0018: M11's roles in flat steps; were M14's `Starlight`/`Sulfur`/`HAlpha`/`Ice` ramps)*: the blast-wave line one flat dim blue, `Cold` 70; the ejecta's toned value `u` (×1.8 exposure) at `250·u²` through a 256-entry table, black below level 12, its bodies (≤ 110) two flat dim orange steps of shocked gas (`Hot` 0 below level 55, 45 above) and the brighter knots and fingertips one flat colour per element: sulfur/silicon `Sun` 210, iron `Hot` 220, oxygen `Wave` 200; knots splat to single pixels, the ejecta smoothed by one `[1 2 1]` blur; the young remnant's exposure (shock and ejecta) is lifted ×3.5 at the start, easing to ×1 over its first 6 model seconds, so it reads from the first second; background stars (one per 200 cells) `Star` 40 or 120; the central compact object a single `Star` 200 dot; the flash flat rings, `Star` 230, `Sun` 220, `Hot` 150, with a `Star` 255 heart. ASCII by level.
- **Look** *(M16, GEN-R25; supersedes the M15 Inks and the element colours)*: a filament shell drawn in braille threads on black, like the filaments of the Crab and Cas A. The shocked ejecta are radial Rayleigh–Taylor filaments, each a one-dot thread from the inner edge of the emitting shell, max(`R_r`, 0.5 `R_c0`) (in practice the reverse shock), out to its fingertip at the contact `R_c(θ)` (long where the R–T fingers reach, short stubs between them), wiggling sideways by a smooth per-filament curve of at most ~2 dots; their angles follow the golden-angle sequence, and the drawn prefix holds one filament per `FIL_SPACING` = 2 dots of the inner edge's circumference (at most 400), so the shell keeps its spacing as it grows and new filaments sprout between old ones. Colour along a filament is the ejecta's temperature, which falls with the time since the reverse shock heated it: the inner 35 % (freshly shocked, next to the reverse shock) `Sun` 220, the rest `Hot` 190 in the fingers and `Hot` 150 on the stubs between them. Knots the reverse shock has just lit are sparks coasting outward on their homologous paths, `r ∝ t`: a head dot (`Star` 255 while its brightness is ≥ 0.75, else `Sun` 220) and a radial trail back along the path it took in the last `SPARK_TRAIL` = 1.2 s (`Sun` 220), drawn while the brightness is ≥ 0.45; afterwards a knot is part of the filament shell. The forward shock is a faint, thin `Cold` 110 circle one dot thick at `R_f(θ)` (dotted: every other dot), outside the filaments. Background stars single `Star` 60/120 dots (one per 200 cells); the compact object one `Star` 200 dot; the flash a braille starburst (a `Sun` 220 ring and `Star` 255 arms that shrink as it fades) round a `Star` 255 heart. In the 4 s fade the drawn filament prefix shrinks with the envelope, so the remnant dissolves thread by thread. No half blocks.
- **Title**: `Supernova remnant · blast wave`.
- **Stylisation**: element → hue on the brightest ejecta only (*M15*: false colour in M11's roles, sulfur yellow, iron red, oxygen cyan; the early exposure lift; the oxygen patches stand for O emission seen mostly in optical light and Chandra's O VIII lines); a 2-D projection of shells; the time scale is ~300 yr in 36 s, and the blast wave reaches the Sedov slope sooner than Cas A does (its expansion index is m ≈ 0.65 today); R–T growth is exponential then clamped, not simulated hydrodynamics.
- **Acceptance**: `SNR-A1` `R_f` is increasing and its log-slope moves from ≥ 0.55 early to ≤ 0.45 late; `SNR-A2` `R_r < R_c < R_f` at every angle; `SNR-A3` a knot is lit only after crossing `R_r` and its brightness is non-increasing afterwards; `SNR-A4` *(changed, M16)* every `Cold` shock dot lies within 1.5 dots of `R_f(θ)`, and the shock is a thin circle (≥ 40 % of its circumference drawn); `SNR-A5` *(changed, M16)* the knot pool never exceeds its capacity and the loop restarts with a flash (the centre cell is the `Star` 255 heart); `SNR-A7` *(changed, M16)* at 200×50 more than 60 cells are lit one second in, and at every time every lit cell is braille in one of the M16 inks (`Cold` 110, `Sun` 220, `Hot` 190/150, `Star`); `SNR-A6` the blast wave is visibly out of round (max/min radius 1.08–1.2 for any seed), at low order (≤ 8 sign changes of the shape round the rim), and reshapes only slowly (< 0.01 per frame); ~~SNR-A8~~ *(superseded by the M17 look)* *(new, M16)* the ejecta are thin radial threads: ≥ 85 % of filament dots have at most 3 lit dots across the radial direction within ±2 dots, the filament count follows the shell circumference (more at 30 s than at 3 s), and every filament dot lies between `R_r` and the blast wave; ~~SNR-A9~~ *(superseded by the M17 look)* *(new, M16)* colour by temperature: `Sun` filament dots sit on average inside `Hot` ones (smaller `r/R_f`); `SNR-A10` *(new, M16)* a spark's trail is radial and behind its head (the head is the farthest point from the centre), its length the distance coasted in `SPARK_TRAIL`.
- **Look** *(M17, GEN-R26, Doppler-shell Cas A; supersedes the M16 radial filament combs)*: the ejecta are a few hundred knots on a slowly turning 3D shell, each knot a small stipple puff (fixed samples, 6–12 dots) coloured by its line-of-sight velocity, as the two signs of an orbital lobe: `Cold` where it approaches us (blueshift), `Hot` where it recedes, `Sun` near the limb where `|v_z|` is small; Rayleigh–Taylor fingers are fine threads trailing inward behind knots; the forward shock is a thin faint `Cold` synchrotron thread with a sparse stipple; the neutron star is a tiny `Star` dot kicked off-centre and drifting; the shell expands with knot afterglow trails. *(Model, M17: knots coast freely until the reverse shock lights them, then decelerate into the shocked shell at 0.92 × the R–T-rippled contact radius, so they gather between the reverse shock and the contact; face-on knots show fewer, dimmer dots than limb knots (limb brightening), so the shell reads as a ring; small screens draw a prefix of the knots.)*
- **Acceptance** *(M17)*: `SNR-A11` a knot is `Cold` iff its line-of-sight velocity points toward the viewer by more than the limb band, `Hot` iff away, `Sun` otherwise; `SNR-A12` every knot lies inside the forward shock; `SNR-A13` the neutron star is off-centre and moves; `SNR-A14` the forward-shock rim is at most two dots thick and sparse (at most half its dots lit).
