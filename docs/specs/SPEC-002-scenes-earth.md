### aurora  (Earth & Sky, M10, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: a subauroral night, looking north over a still lake. A quiet arc low in the north brightens suddenly and breaks into folding, rayed curtains that climb and fill the sky, green below and capped with crimson above, with a pink hem along the lower border at the substorm's peak. It then fades into softly pulsating patches. Between the curtains the sky stays black: light is drawn as coherent arcs, rayed curtains and patches, never as scattered speckle. The focal point is the lower border of the brightest curtain, which is the sharpest and brightest edge in the frame. A black spruce ridge and its rippling reflection anchor the bottom, and the sparse stars never compete. At 200×50 the shore is row 40. At scene start the arch's crest stands ≈ 18° up (≈ 12 rows above the shore) with both ends sagging into the horizon, and at the expansion peak the curtains fill rows ≈ 2–38. At 80×24 the shore is row 19 and the crest starts ≈ 6 rows above it.
- **Model**: *View*: every object lives in sky angles (azimuth α, east positive; elevation e) or in world km, projected equirectangularly onto a full-screen `Pixels` lattice (P = 2h pixel rows). The horizon row is `y_h = round(0.80·P)` with a lake below it, or `round(0.92·P)` with no lake when h < 14. The scale is `f = max(y_h/62°, W/π)` px/rad, with `x = W/2 + f·α` and `y = y_h − f·e`, so the frame is ≈ 155° × 62° at 200×50 and ≈ 130° × 62° at 80×24. *Curtains*: 1–3 thin vertical sheets aligned east–west. Sheet c lies at northward ground distance `D_c(s,t) = D0_c(t) + ξ_c(s,t)` of the along-arc coordinate s (east positive). It is sampled at M = 480 fixed points `s_j = 300 km·tan α_j`, with α_j uniform in ±85°. The displacement is `ξ = Σ_{k=1..4} w_kj·a_k sin(κ_k s + θ_k)` with λ = 900/240/70/18 km (bend/surge/fold/curl), `a_1 = 40 km` and `a_k = F(t)·σ_k/κ_k` for slope amplitudes σ = 0.5/1.3/0.8. The weight `w_kj = clamp(λ_k/(2Δs_j) − 1, 0, 1)` fades a mode out wherever it has fewer than 4 samples per wavelength, so flank folds never alias. Phases integrate as `θ_k −= κ_k c_k dt`, so mode k drifts at velocity c_k, with c = +0.3, −v_s(t), +1.2, −0.8 km/s (negative is westward; v_s is the westward-travelling surge). Speed changes therefore never make the pattern jump. Each sample lies at ground distance `d = hypot(s, D)` and azimuth `α = atan2(s, D)`. An altitude z maps to `e = atan((z − d²/2R⊕)/d)` (flat earth plus curvature drop, R⊕ = 6371 km). A straight east–west arc is therefore an arch that sags into both horizons. The sheet doubles back on screen wherever `ξ' > cot|α|`; for the fold band's slope of 1.3 that happens only beyond |α| ≈ 38°, which is how real folds read in the oblique sky. *Precipitation*: `Φ = A_c(t)·B·((1 − r) + r·ρ/ρ̄)`. B is a westward-drifting bulge `1 + b(t)·exp(−((α − α_b)/w_b)²)`. The ray field is `ρ = ⅓Σ_m max(0, cos(κ_m(s − v_m t) + φ_m))⁶` with λ_m = 23/37/61 km and `v_m = v_r(t)·{1, −0.6, 0.8}`. Its mean is ρ̄ = 5/32, so switching rays on moves precipitation into the rays without changing its average; the ray-averaged flux is `Φ̄ = A·B`. The mean energy E0(t) sets the lower border `z_b = 131 − 25·log10(E0/1 keV)` km, clamped to [85, 150]. This is a fit to the peak energy-deposition altitudes of Rees (1963). *Emission* at altitude z has three components (rays also raise the green column by up to 1.8×, since H grows inside a ray and the profile is not renormalised). Green OI 557.7 nm is `G = Φ·S·g(z − z_b)` with `g(u) = (1 − e^{−(u+2)/2})·e^{−u/H}` for u ≥ −2 km (else 0). It peaks ≈ 3 km above z_b, and `H = 22 km·(1 + 0.8 r ρ)` so that rays reach higher. Green is prompt (the O(¹S) lifetime is 0.74 s). Red OI 630.0 nm is `R = R_j·min(S, 2)·0.4·exp(1 − y − e^{−y})/(1 + e^{−(z−190)/12})` with y = (z − 240)/50 km: a Chapman production layer times collisional quenching of O(¹D) below ~200 km. O(¹D) atoms diffuse tens of km during their 110 s lifetime, so the red layer is thick: its slant gain is capped at 2, and the red is smoothed along the arc by a ≈ 4° Gaussian (three running-sum box passes per pixel row) before it is thresholded, so where folds double back the red ramps instead of standing as a wall. It is displayed at 0.27× (the photopic ratio V(630)/V(557.7)), so the diffuse fringe stays a fringe. The red source relaxes exactly as `R_j ← Φ + (R_j − Φ)·e^{−dt/τ_R}` with τ_R = 3 s. That is the 110 s radiative lifetime compressed only enough to keep the lag visible (on the 144× substorm clock it would be 0.76 s), so the red is smooth, rayless and lingers. The pink N₂ 1PG hem marks type-B aurora, where fast electrons reach heights at which O(¹S) is quenched. It is `P = Φ·S·clamp((E0 − 8)/12, 0, 1)` for z_b − 4 ≤ z ≤ z_b + 1 km, splatted for the brightest curtain only, and it always includes the layer's lowest lit pixel, so it never vanishes at small sizes. A pixel is pink only where P ≥ 0.25 G and the pink's own tone `1 − e^{−P/E_sat}` (after extinction) is ≥ 0.6; isolated hem pixels with no hem neighbour in the adjacent columns are demoted to green (keeping their level), so the hem reads as a run. The slant factor `S = min(1/|n̂·l̂|, 6)` uses the sheet normal n ∝ (−ξ', 1, 0) (east, north, up) and the line of sight l̂ to the sample's lower border. It is the path length through a thin sheet, so folds seen edge-on become bright caustic "rays". *Simplifications*: sheets and rays are vertical (field-aligned; valid for the northern sky seen from a subauroral site) and arcs are strictly east–west. The red source ignores E0 (real 630 nm weakens as precipitation hardens), and its 0.4 normalisation makes the red column ≈ 2.2× the green one, 10–45× the photometric I630/I557.7 of keV aurora, for a vivid fringe; the photopic weight 0.27 brings the displayed ratio to ≈ 0.6. *Raster*: consecutive samples are splatted as column spans into per-column layers {d, z_b, H, G/R/P amplitudes}, at most 6 per column (one crossing per curtain plus room for three more where folds double back; a seventh merges into the nearest d). A fold that doubles back gives a column several layers, and they add. Pixel row y samples each layer at `z = d·tan e_y + d²/2R⊕`. This uses a per-resize tan e row table, a 0.5-km green LUT in u with H quantised to 16 steps, and a 1-km red LUT in z. The sum is multiplied by the extinction `exp(−0.12(1/sin max(e, 2°) − 1))` and tone-mapped as `1 − e^{−E/E_sat}` (E_sat = 0.9) through a LUT. Emission fades in under the top edge over 20 % of the pixel rows (at least 3 px) by smoothstep, so a crown overhead thins into dark sky and the label stays in clear air. *Thresholds, not dither*: the theme floors lit roles at 30 % brightness, so faint light cannot be shown by level; it is left dark rather than scattered as a dither. The pulsating-patch green Q (see *Recovery*) adds to the tone but is kept apart from the curtains' green G. Where the curtains' own green tone is < 0.03 the red is dropped (below notice), so patch light never opens the crown's gate. A pixel whose red share R/(G+Q+R) is ≤ ½ is green if its tone is ≥ 0.14, at level `12 + 243·(tone − 0.14)/0.86`. A red-dominant pixel is the crown if its tone is ≥ 0.2 *and* the curtains' green tone there is ≥ 0.03, at level `12 + 98·(tone − 0.2)/0.8` in `Accent`, so every lit level starts from the core floor `ink::MIN_VISIBLE_LEVEL` = 12 (CORE-R66): the crown caps each ray and fold with crimson and so follows the curtains' envelope; diffuse red far above the green would be cut flat by the top fade (a lid) and stand as a slab over the sky. A hem pixel is `Accent` at `190 + 65·(tone − 0.14)/0.86`. Every lit run thus starts from the theme's dimmest shade of its role, each column lights contiguous runs where its emission profile crosses the threshold, and rays that reach higher end higher, so a curtain's top edge is combed into vertical streaks. Finally specks are dropped: a lit sky pixel with no lit neighbour (of 8), and a pair whose two pixels neighbour only each other, so a fading arc end or a distant arc on the horizon never crumbles into grit. *Setting*: a far-shore ridge (3-octave value noise in α, 0.8–4° high) is topped by seeded spruce spires (1.5–4° apart, 1.2–3° tall, half-width 0.28× height); above f = 60 px/rad a second, nearer forest of fewer, taller spires (2.5–6° apart, 2.5–4° tall, half-width 0.35×) replaces them so each shows triangular flanks. Below it the far spires are stretched upward (widths unchanged, so they get spikier) until a 3° spire (the height cap) stands 3 px: at 80×24 a 3° spruce is under 2 px and the comb would read as a ruled line. Silhouette pixels are never lit. A `Seq` skyglow lights every sky pixel below the glow height `max(f·8°, tallest silhouette + 1.5 px, 3 px)`, from 0.2 at the horizon falling quadratically to 0.02 at its top, solid throughout: the ramp's black end is a dark navy, so the glow stops just above it rather than being carried into it, and the ridge and spires cut a crisp black edge into it. The lake mirrors the sky about y_h at 0.45× level, with their roles: the skyglow, and each column's lit sky runs whole wherever a run's brightest pixel has level ≥ 40 (a fainter run's copy could not be dimmer than the theme's floor, while cutting the dim ends off brighter runs would leave shards), emission copies never below level 12; the ridge and spires mirror as a black band cut out of the reflected glow. Its ripple is `δx = round(0.04 d·sin(0.9d − 1.7t + 0.13x))`, where d is the number of pixel rows below the shore, so the wavefronts travel toward the viewer while the water stays still. Whole rows drop out where `sin(0.9d − 1.7t) > 0.9`, so mirrored rays read as vertical streaks cut by horizontal ripple gaps. Stars come from a seeded 1200-star catalogue in (α, e) with magnitudes. They are shown brightest-first until 2 % of sky cells hold one, so the limiting magnitude adapts to resolution. They twinkle more toward the horizon and are drawn only in cells whose two pixels are empty.
- **Motion**: a `Timeline` runs growth 12 s → onset 3 s → expansion 15 s → recovery 20 s. The 50 s loop compresses a ~2 h substorm ≈ 144× (Akasofu 1964), while folds and rays move at real km/s speeds. Keyframes are smoothstep-interpolated, except that every segment starting at the onset uses `ease::out_quad` (the sudden breakup), and every one returns to its growth-start value by the end of recovery. The scene clock starts at t = 8 s, so breakup lands 4 s into a default 15 s visit. *Growth*: two faint homogeneous arcs drift equatorward, toward the viewer (D0₁ 520 → 260 km, A₁ 0.35 → 0.4, A₂ 0.3, E0 2 → 4 keV, F 0.15, r 0.04, v_r 1 km/s), each a clear band above its threshold rather than a broken line hovering at it. *Onset*: the equatorward arc brightens 2.5× within 1.5 s (`ease::out_quad`). E0 rises to 20 keV, so the lower border drops ≈ 18 km and the pink hem appears. Rays switch on (r → 0.8, v_r → 12 km/s). *Expansion*: the bulge swells both ways. Arc 1 moves toward the zenith (D0₁ → 170 km, base ≈ 31°) while arcs 2–3 light up poleward, arc 2 at D0₁ + 150 km closing to + 110 km within 9 s, arc 3 at D0₁ + 330 km. F rises to 1.0, so folds double back in the oblique sky. The bulge surges west (α_b +10° → −40°, v_s → 6 km/s), and E0 settles to 10 keV. *Recovery*: the arcs dim and retreat poleward (D0 → 520 km; E0, F, r, v_r back to their growth values), and the red fringe outlives the green by τ_R. Six pulsating patches fade in and out: seeded one per 15° stratum across α ±45° (±3° jitter, so neighbours drifting together keep apart instead of fusing into one blob) and at e 25–45° (mid-sky, clear of the top fade), soft Gaussian 6–12° × 3–6° ellipses cut at 1.6 half-extents, with half-extents floored at min(5 px, 0.07 W) × min(3 px, P/12) (at 80×24 a 6° patch is only 4 columns, a smudge; the floor is 5 × 3 px there and at 200×50 and 2.8 × 2 px at 40×12, so a small frame's patches never swell into clouds half the sky wide), period 4–12 s, on-fraction 0.4 with 0.4 s edges, drifting east 0.5°/s, green at 0.45× the arc peak, so a patch at full pulse stays dimmer than the recovery arc. Pulsating aurora has no red cap: patch light is kept out of the crown's gate (*Thresholds*), so a patch in the lingering red dome raises no crimson hood. Activity a(t) runs 0.02 → 0.15 (end of growth) → 0.55 (end of onset) → 1.0 (t = 22 s) → 0.75 (end of expansion) → 0.02 and sets `AL = −(30 + 800a)` nT. In any 1 s window the lake ripple alone changes pixel occupancy (GEN-R9 compares glyphs), and rays and folds add more.
- **Resize**: there is no simulation lattice, so ADR-0012 bucketing does not apply. All state lives in world or sky coordinates and survives any resize untouched: fold and ray phases, red sources, clock, star catalogue, trees and patches. Resize rebuilds only the view: f, y_h, the tan e and extinction row tables, the per-column silhouette, the visible-star list, the layer scratch and `Pixels`. A resize re-frames the same sky. At 1×1 the view is sky-only and nothing panics.
- **Inks**: green `Particle` 12–255; red crown `Accent` 12–110 (a deep magenta-crimson: dark `Hot` is a rust brown and bright `Hot` an orange, while the 630 nm crown reads crimson over green in photographs); pink hem `Accent` ≥ 190, well clear of the crown; skyglow `Seq` 0.02–0.2; stars `Star`; ASCII ridge `Dim` 70; label `Text`. Reflections reuse the source role at 0.45× level (emission copies at least 12). Silhouettes stay empty (black).
- **ASCII**: every cell takes the brighter emission pixel of its pair (a hem pixel wins, so the hem stays a line); skyglow returns `None`. Sky: green is `|` (≥ 200), `!` (≥ 130), `:` (≥ 70), else `,` (so a quiet arc never reads as the dim stars `.`); the crown is `"` (≥ 60), else `'`; the hem is `=`. Lake cells are `~` (≥ 60), else `-`. After the blit, in ASCII mode only, the ridge top is drawn as one continuous `_` line (the cell holding each column's top ridge pixel), with a spire tip `^` in the cell just above it wherever a spire rises above the line (one comb however tall the stretched spires), in still-blank cells, `Dim` 70. Stars are `.`, with `*` for the brightest 10 %, and also need a still-blank cell. At 80×24 this reads as `,:!|` columns capped with `'"`, arching over a `^^_` shore and `~` water.
- **Label**: top-left `AL -620 nT  expansion` (`Text` 150). AL is rounded to 10 nT and written digit by digit into a stack buffer, and the phase word is the `Timeline` beat label. The phase word is dropped when w < 60, and the whole label when w < 24 or h < 10.
- **Perf** at 200×50: update ≈ 0.35 ms. Sampling costs ≈ 0.09 ms (3 × 480 samples × 4 fold sines, 3 ray cos⁶ and a red relaxation, with one exp per call), independent of terminal size; the rest is the rasterisation, which runs in `update`: projection and spans ≈ 0.08 ms; sky shading ≈ 16k px × ≤ 2 live layers of LUT reads ≈ 0.15 ms, skipping rows above the emission top; lake via per-row ripple tables ≈ 0.02 ms; silhouette. Render (blit, stars, the ASCII ridge and the label) ≈ 0.05 ms. Total ≈ 0.45 ms (measured 0.47 ms in criterion at load ≈ 14, 2026-09-27, after the patch and whole-run lake rework), with no allocation after resize. Thresholding and the speck pass cost ≈ 0.02 ms, the lake's per-column run-peak scan ≈ 0.01 ms; nothing is precomputed per process.
- **Acceptance**: `AUR-A1` `lower_border_km(e0)` is strictly decreasing on [1, 60] keV, stays within [85, 150] km for any input, and gives 113.5, 106.0, 98.5 and 88.5 km (±0.1) at 5, 10, 20 and 50 keV; `AUR-A2` with H = 22 km (ρ = 0) and E0 ∈ {3, 10, 20} keV, the green profile peaks within [z_b, z_b + 6] km, is 0 below z_b − 2 km and is < 5 % of its peak above z_b + 80 km; the red profile peaks in [220, 260] km and is < 5 % of its peak below 170 km; with equal sources, red > green for all z ∈ [200, 450] km and red < 0.05·green for all z ∈ [z_b, 150] km; the pink profile is 0 outside [z_b − 4, z_b + 1] km and 0 for E0 ≤ 8 keV; `AUR-A3` from a unit step, `red_relax(r, target, dt)` reaches 1 − e⁻¹ (±1e-12) after τ_R = 3 s, composes exactly (0.1 s then 0.2 s equals 0.3 s within 1e-12) and never overshoots; along the onset keyframes A(t) at least doubles within 1.5 s of the onset start, while a red source relaxed toward A(t) in 1/60 s steps covers < 45 % of the jump at that moment; the mean of ρ over s ∈ [0, 10⁴] km at fixed t is 5/32 ± 0.005; `AUR-A4` `elevation_deg(105, 600)` is 7.29 ± 0.05°, `elevation_deg(105, 180)` is 29.65 ± 0.05°, and `altitude_km(elevation_deg(z, d), d)` returns z within 1e-9 km; with F = 0 and a_1 = 0, a curtain at D0 = 300 km projects to an arch whose lower-border elevation is maximal at α = 0 and strictly decreasing in |α|; `AUR-A5` (200×50 view, curtain at D0 = 300 km): with F = 0 and a_1 = 0, every column where the lower border is above the horizon gets exactly one layer; with only the fold band (σ = 1.3, F = 1), no column with |α| < 37° gets 2 layers from that curtain and at least one column with |α| > 38° does; S is ≥ 1, never exceeds 6, and equals 1/cos e_b (1e-9) for an unfolded arc at α = 0; `AUR-A6` the beats are growth 12 s → onset 3 s → expansion 15 s → recovery 20 s in order, the loop is 50 s, and a fresh scene starts at t = 8 s; every keyframed parameter is continuous across the wrap (|Δ| < 1e-9); `al_nt(t)` stays in [−150, −30] nT throughout growth and ≤ −450 nT throughout expansion; `patch_pulse(t, P)` is P-periodic (1e-12) with on-fraction (value > 0.5) 0.40 ± 0.02, and patches are 0 outside recovery; `AUR-A7` at 200×50, after 56 × `update(0.25)` (scene t = 22 s) and one render (< 1 s in debug): no silhouette pixel is lit, lit pixels are 15–70 % of non-silhouette sky pixels, every lit lake pixel has level ≤ 0.5× the brightest sky level, every star cell has both pixels empty, and at least one hem pixel (`Accent` ≥ 190) exists, while after 4 updates (t = 9 s) there is none; ### constellation_map  (Earth & Sky, M4, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: a star chart that traces one constellation at a time, then names it, over a twinkling sky.
- **Model**: 6 constellations (Orion, Ursa Major, Cassiopeia, Cygnus, Crux, Scorpius) as normalized star coordinates + edges, fitted to 80 % of the screen preserving shape; each is shown for 12 s: lines trace in over the first 4 s, the name appears after tracing; background stars `0.02·area` twinkle.
- **Data**: star positions from real J2000 RA/Dec and visual magnitudes, gnomonic projection about the figure centre (east left, north up); only stars on the conventional stick figure are included (e.g. ε Cru omitted).
- **Inks**: constellation stars `Star`, lines `Structure`, tracing pen tip `Accent`, name `Text`, background `Dim`.
- **Acceptance**: `CON-A1` every edge references valid stars and every star is on at least one edge; `CON-A2` tracing progress 0 draws no segment, 1 draws all, and drawn length is monotone in progress; `CON-A3` the constellation index advances every 12 s and wraps.

### gravity_assist  (Voyages, M21)
- **Intent**: how a slingshot works: in Jupiter's frame the probe swings by on a hyperbola and leaves as fast as it came; in the Sun's frame the same pass bends its orbit outward and it leaves much faster.
- **Model**: patched conics in model units. Left panel (planet frame): a hyperbola with incoming excess speed `v∞`, periapsis `r_p`, turn angle `δ` from `sin(δ/2) = 1 / (1 + r_p v∞² / μ)`. Right panel (Sun frame): the probe's heliocentric ellipse before the encounter and the new conic after it, from `v_out = v_J + R(δ) · (v_in − v_J)`; Jupiter on a circular orbit. One looping `Timeline` (~20 s): `approach` (the probe climbs its pre-flyby orbit; the left panel shows it inbound), `flyby` (it swings round Jupiter in both panels at once), `faster away` (it departs on the new orbit). The encounter geometry (a trailing-side pass) varies slightly per loop from `ctx.rng`, always gaining speed.
- **Motion**: the probe moves along its conics; a thread trails it.
- **Resize**: panel split recomputed; below 60×14 cells only the right panel is drawn.
- **Look** *(GEN-R25)*: two panels separated by a thin `Dim` dotted divider. The probe's path as a 1-px braille thread: the pre-flyby orbit `Cold`, the post-flyby orbit `Hot`; the moving probe a small `Accent` atom with a short afterglow; Jupiter a small solid `Planet` atom at the centre of the left panel and a small `Planet` atom on a `Dim` dotted orbit in the right; the Sun a small `Sun` atom. Readout right-aligned on row `h − 1`: `v 10 km/s` until the flyby, then `v 10 → 20 km/s` (heliocentric speeds scaled to a Voyager-2-like flyby; the new orbit exceeds solar escape), shortened to `20 km/s` when room is short; the left panel carries `|v - vJ| in = out` as a small `Text` label at its top.
- **Title**: `Gravity assist · borrowed speed`; captions `approach`, `flyby`, `faster away`.
- **Stylisation**: patched conics, no n-body integration; the planet's sphere of influence shrinks to a point in the Sun's frame; speeds are scaled to a Voyager-2-at-Jupiter-like gain, not computed from real ephemerides.
- **Acceptance**: `ASST-A1` in the planet frame `|v_out − v_J| = |v_in − v_J|` within 1e-9; `ASST-A2` the turn angle obeys `sin(δ/2) = 1/(1 + r_p v∞²/μ)`; `ASST-A3` a trailing-side pass raises the heliocentric speed and orbital energy, every loop and seed; `ASST-A4` the drawn post-flyby conic passes through the encounter point with velocity `v_out`; `ASST-A5` below 60×14 only the Sun-frame panel is drawn; `ASST-A6` `caption()` follows the acts and loops.
- **As built**: GM_sun = 1, Jupiter at r = 1 with speed 1 (13.07 km/s scales the readout); pre-flyby ellipse perihelion 0.192, aphelion 1.15–1.3; Jupiter-frame periapsis 5.5–10·10⁻⁴ (≈ 6–11 R_J, μ_J = 9.55·10⁻⁴), δ ≈ 70–95°, so about 10 → 20–21 km/s. Acts 4.5 + 4.5 + 8 s; one Sun-frame clock (0.33 model time/s) for approach and departure, stopped during the flyby (the sphere of influence is a point); the probe launches from perihelion, reaches Jupiter at the end of `approach` and runs the new conic to the panel's edge (2.6 Jupiter-orbit radii) in ≈ 5–6 s. The left panel runs the probe leg to leg on the Kepler-timed hyperbola during `flyby`, Jupiter centred, both legs ending ≈ 2 cells inside. Only perihelion → encounter of the old orbit is drawn. Probe σ 0.7 with ≈ 0.7 s afterglow. The Sun panel's drawing stops 3 cells short of the divider, and the escaping probe stops there. All braille.

### hurricane  (Earth & Sky, M13, M15, M16)
- **Intent** *(M15, ADR-0018)*: a mature hurricane seen from above on a black sea: a small clear eye inside a solid white central dense overcast, broad ragged rain bands winding counter-clockwise into it and faint cirrus outflow fanning clockwise at the edge, and an infrared act in the classic enhancement curve, the coldest cloud tops rising through blue, cyan, green and yellow to orange.
- **Model**: `vortex_advect` (shared with jupiter_storms): semi-Lagrangian cloud-water field on a capped lattice advected by a Holland-profile vortex (tangential wind peaking at the radius of maximum wind) plus radial inflow near the surface that tightens bands into logarithmic spirals; convective sources in the eyewall ring (0.75–1.5 radii of maximum wind), a solid central dense overcast out to 2.2 radii (fading over 0.8 more) and 3 broad spiral bands (noise-modulated cells) that switch on over one radius past 2.4 radii so they merge into the overcast; the visible picture adds a subtle fixed convective-tower texture (overshooting tops, turning slowly with the storm) with its own sunlit and shadowed sides where cloud is thick; the eye (r < 0.6 radii of maximum wind) is kept clear by a sink (subsidence). Display only (not advected), refreshed every 6 steps: faint cirrus outflow streaks spiralling out clockwise (anticyclonic aloft) at 0.7–1.5 storm radii. Brightness is blurred once (1-2-1) for soft 2–4 px cloud edges. On a coarse lattice (height ≤ 64, i.e. 80×24 and smaller) the secondary band and the tower texture drop out, the bands start at 3.2 radii, the eyewall cells are weaker and the picture is blurred twice, so a small frame shows a few broad arms rather than thin stripes. Northern hemisphere: cyclonic = counter-clockwise (Coriolis).
- **Motion**: clouds rotate and spiral inward; bands pulse; `Timeline` switches visible → IR act every 25 s; the whole storm drifts slowly.
- **Resize**: lattice regridded (ADR-0012); vortex in storm radii. *(M14, GEN-R19)* The first resize spins the storm up (180 steps) on a coarse lattice of 1/24 of the cap and then regrids it to the full lattice; the convective-tower tile is size-independent and built once.
- **Inks** *(M15, ADR-0018: M11's roles in flat steps; were M14's `Starlight` and muted `Infrared` ramps)*: visible: the open sea and the eye are black (no ocean colour or sunglint). Cloud lightness is `0.16 + 0.84·√b` of the cloud-top brightness `b`, with sun-facing relief and the tower texture where the cloud has body, times an opacity `smoothstep((b − 0.012)/0.128)`, scaled by 0.8, and drawn in four flat grey-to-white steps: black below level 14, the thin veil `Dim` 0 below 0.28, the deck `Structure` 120 below 0.5, `Star` 110 below 0.66, sunlit tops `Star` 220. Rows of the cubic lattice sample are cached while shading. IR act (25 s visible, 12 s infrared, 1.5 s cross-fades: a dip through black, the visible picture fading out while the IR weight rises to ½ and the IR one fading in after, uniform in space): coldness `c` (the enhancement curve's position, from cloud-top brightness standing in for height, hence coldness, times a height factor, 1 inside the overcast (2.2 radii of maximum wind) falling to 0.62 at 0.9 storm radii, so the overcast and eyewall are the coldest), times the thin-cloud opacity and the cross-fade, in six flat steps of the classic enhancement curve: warm low cloud `Dim` 0 below 0.18, `Cold` 90 below 0.36, `Wave` 150 below 0.52, `Particle` 130 below 0.66, `Sun` 200 below 0.8, the coldest tops `Hot` 230; black where the opacity × cross-fade is below level 14. ASCII by level, the sea left blank; in Unicode a cell whose two pixels match is a space on that background.
- **Look** *(M16, GEN-R25; spiral band threads, superseding the half-block cloud picture, its relief and tower texture, and the inks above)*: the same storm model, drawn as braille threads on black. The blurred cloud-top brightness on the lattice is contoured by marching squares at `b = 0.3, 0.5, 0.7` (below 0.3 the band cells' noise, near the lattice scale, only frays into specks), each crossed lattice cell refined into `k × k` bilinear sub-cells (`Dots::contour_cell_refined`, CORE-R106) (`k = clamp(⌈max(sx, sy)/2.2⌉, 1, 5)`, `sx, sy` dots per node), so the threads are smooth. *(M16 round 2)* Inside 1.8 radii of maximum wind only the 0.5 and 0.7 contours are drawn: two eyewall rings round the clear eye. Outside it each rain band is one thread along its centre line: the zero of the brightness's radial derivative (central differences per node) where it falls outward, in cloud of at least 0.4, so the bands are single logarithmic-spiral threads winding counter-clockwise into the eye. **Visible act**: colour by radius, outer bands (beyond 3.2 radii of maximum wind) `Cold`, the inner bands and overcast edge (1.8–3.2) `Wave`, the eyewall (inside 1.8) `Star`, each stepped 150, 200, 245 by contour level. **Inflow tracers**: 90 dots carried by the low-level wind (the model's tangential wind plus inflow, storm-relative), each with a trail of its last 0.4 s (`Star` 255 head, `Cold` 150/110 trail), respawned at 0.5–1 storm radii when they reach the eye or after 12–20 s, so they spiral in counter-clockwise. **Cirrus**: the faint outflow stays in the brightness (below the lowest contour) and is not drawn; as threads it only hatched the storm's flanks. **IR act**: the threads recolour by the coldness of their cloud tops (the enhancement curve of the contour level, or of a band ridge's own brightness, times the height factor, so the inner bands recolour too): warm `Cold` 170 below 0.4, `Wave` 210 below 0.7, the coldest tops `Accent` 245; the cross-fade dips through black (no threads while its weight is below 0.25, one step dimmer below 0.6). ASCII by dot count. Rasterised in `prepare` (GEN-R20).
- **Title**: `Hurricane · Coriolis spirals`; captions `visible`, `infrared`.
- **Stylisation**: kinematic vortex, not a moist-convection model; time-lapse; brightness stands in for cloud-top temperature in the IR act, and the enhancement curve is drawn in six flat semantic steps; the sea is drawn black; seen from above (Earth's weather seen from above is Earth & Sky: ADR-0013 addendum, M13).
- **Acceptance**: `HUR-A1` the tangential wind profile peaks at the radius of maximum wind and turns counter-clockwise; `HUR-A2` a tracer released at 0.8 R spirals inward (radius decreasing, angle increasing CCW); `HUR-A3` the eye (r < 0.5 radii of maximum wind) holds < 10 % of the eyewall's cloud; `HUR-A4` *(changed, M16)* in the IR act every thread ink is one of the three IR steps (`Cold`, `Wave`, `Accent`, the tracers aside), the coldest tops reach `Accent`, and the steps run `Cold`, `Wave`, `Accent` with coldness; `HUR-A5` the lattice stays within its cap at 500×150; `HUR-A6`/`HUR-A7` *(changed, M16)* at 120×40 and 80×24 the visible act is braille threads in `Cold`, `Wave` and `Star` on black: lit cells hold on average ≤ 4.5 of their 8 dots, and more than half of the cells are blank; `HUR-A8` *(changed, M16)* at 80×24, the mean cloud-top brightness of the lattice nodes in 36 angular bins round annuli at 2.5, 3.5 and 4.5 radii of maximum wind shows at most 4 arms (peaks with 0.08 hysteresis); `HUR-A9` *(changed, M16)* at 120×40 (t = 2 s and 9 s) the central dense overcast is unbroken: all 36 angular bins of the 1.2–2 radii annulus of the lattice brightness average at least the top contour level (0.7) and ≥ 0.75 of the brightest, and `Star` eyewall threads ring the eye: every one of 12 directions has a `Star` cell within 0.5–2 radii of maximum wind; `HUR-A10` *(changed, M16)* in the IR act at 120×40 there are `Accent` threads inside 3 radii of maximum wind (the overcast's top contour; inside the eye the lower contours are thin, warmer cloud), and fewer than a quarter of the band thread cells at 4.5–6 radii are `Accent` (coldest tops in the overcast, not the bands); `HUR-A11` *(new, M16)* the inflow tracers spiral in: over 1 s the tracers that were not respawned turn counter-clockwise and move inward on average, and each trail point is a position its tracer held in the last 0.4 s.

### jwst_unfolding  (Voyages, M13, M15, M16)
- **Intent**: the James Webb Space Telescope unfolding after launch: the fairing opens on the folded observatory, the sunshield pallets drop, the mid-booms stretch the shield and its five layers tension one by one, the secondary mirror swings out, the gold mirror wings fold into place, and the observatory cruises into its halo orbit around L2, the Earth and Moon small behind.
- **Model**: a diorama driven by a `Timeline` of deployment beats (the real sequence, compressed). Parts are convex polygons in a 3-D body frame (sunshield layers as two halves of an elongated hexagon each, the primary as an explicit 18-hex honeycomb: three panels (the centre's 12 segments round the open central hole, and the two 3-segment wing columns) rasterised per `Pixels` pixel by mapping the pixel back into the mirror plane (flat-top axial hex lookup), each segment a flat pale-gold fill with a one-pixel dark backplane gap measured in pixel space, the hole dark grey, an edge-on wing a flat gold strip; bus, solar array and fairing halves) projected with `geom::Camera` (then rolled 0.08 rad about the screen centre), painted far to near with per-polygon gradients, a specular band sweeping across the gold (each segment lit as a whole, keeping its flat fill) and a sheen across the Kapton; the secondary a gold dot in a dark baffle on a three-strut tripod of dark struts; edges and struts in braille dots, depth-tested against the painted surfaces. Each beat is an eased angle or extension: pallets fold down, the tower lifts the telescope, the mid-booms extend, then the layers tension from the Sun-facing layer 1 up to layer 5. Final act: a halo orbit drawn as a Lissajous-like loop around L2 in the rotating frame, with the telescope moving along it and the Earth–Moon in the distance.
- **Motion**: beats: fairing (3.5 s), pallets (5 s), tower (3 s), mid-booms and layers tension (10 s), secondary (4 s), wings (7 s), orbit (16 s); the camera sways.
- **Resize**: camera fit rebuilt; state in the Timeline.
- **Layout**: the deployed shield spans ~60–65 % of the width, seen as a thin tilted diamond (camera pitch 0.4 rad, roll 0.08 rad, yaw 0.14 ± 0.12 rad so the mirror faces the viewer) with the five layers' near edges visible under the top sheet (layers drawn lowest-first so the top sheet is never notched); stowed, the camera sees the stack in profile (yaw 1.1 rad, swinging round as the pallets drop): the telescope with its wings folded, the pallets two grey-silver slabs folded up beside it, the bus a box right under their hinge line and the solar array a thin panel hinged on the bus, one connected body.
- **Inks** *(M15, ADR-0018: M11's roles, every shaded surface in four flat steps (`step`: 30 below level 60, 90, 160, 230 from 185); were M14's `Ember`/`Starlight`/`Plasma`/`Cobalt` ramps)*: the mirror gold `Sun` (level 200 ± 9 per segment, the specular glint sweeping every 14 s lifting it a step); the sunshield silver Kapton `Structure` (top layer and layers beneath by their level), the lower layers' shadowed edges `Accent` braille line work only (no magenta fills); the honeycomb's gaps `Star` 24, its central hole 34; stowed pallets, structure, fairing and solar array `Structure` steps; the secondary and trail `Sun`; the Earth `Cold` steps, the Moon `Star`, the distant Sun `Sun` steps; stars `Star` points. ASCII: blocks by level, lines by braille count.
- **Look** *(M16, GEN-R25, blueprint line art; supersedes the M15 Inks and the M14 shading above; geometry, camera and beats unchanged)*: nothing is filled. Every part is still a convex polygon in the body frame, but polygons only write a per-pixel depth buffer (the `w × 2h` lattice of the old half blocks), which hides the line work behind them; what is drawn is braille: the bus and solar array outlines, the tower and the folded pallets in `Structure` 170, the secondary's tripod struts `Structure` 130, the fairing halves' outlines `Wave` 150. Each sunshield layer's outline is coloured by its temperature: layer 5 (facing the telescope, ≈ 40 K) `Cold` 200, then `Wave` 160, `Accent` 150, `Hot` 160, and layer 1 (facing the Sun, ≈ 380 K) `Hot` 230, so the stacked near edges read as the shield's cold-to-hot gradient; folded (before the booms), plain `Structure` lines. The gold mirror is the one solid subject, drawn in dots so it stays braille (GEN-R25's 3 % half-block budget would not hold a half-block mirror against thin line work): every dot inside one of the 18 segments, off the one-pixel seams and the central hole and not behind a nearer surface, lit in the segment's `Sun` step (level 200 ± 9, the glint lifting it), so the honeycomb is a field of gold dots with black seams. The secondary is a small `Sun` 240 dot disc. Stars are single `Star` dots on open sky. The orbit act is braille too: the Earth a small dotted disc (`Cold` 230 day side to the Sun, `Cold` 120 night limb), the Moon a `Star` dot, the halo loop dashed, JWST a `Cold` shield stroke with a gold dot and a dashed gold trail, the Sun a thin `Sun` limb arc off the left edge. ASCII by dot count. *Round 2 (M16)*: frame lines `Structure` 200 (was 170); the tower is one continuous line, not hidden by the shield it passes through; a mirror dot is hidden only by a nearer surface (compared at its pixel's centre, so a steeply seen panel never hides its own dots); the seams are drawn only while a segment's edge spans ≥ 6 dots on screen along its shorter axis, otherwise the honeycomb is solid gold with its hole.
- **Title**: `JWST · unfolding in space`; captions `fairing opens`, `sunshield opens`, `layers tension`, `mirror unfolds`, `halo orbit at L2`.
- **Stylisation**: two weeks in ~48 s (the fairing opened on launch day, the rest over the following two weeks); sizes, layer spacing and orbit not to scale; the halo orbit is an illustrative loop, not a computed CR3BP solution.
- **M14 geometry, M15 inks**: the sunshield a faint silver fill (top layer 48–62, the layers under it 30–42, stowed 70, so its lowest step) whose 1-px braille layer edges carry the shape; the glint the bright accent on the gold.
- **Acceptance**: `JWST-A1` beats happen in the documented order (fairing, pallets, tower, mid-booms, layers, secondary, wings); `JWST-A2` the five layers tension one at a time, Sun-facing layer 1 first; `JWST-A3` the mirror is fully deployed (all 18 hexes visible) at the end of the wings beat, each wing's three hexes showing once it has swung out (folded, a wing hides behind the centre panel); `JWST-A4` captions follow the Timeline; `JWST-A5` after the loop the diorama resets to stowed; `JWST-A6` *(changed, M16)* fully deployed, ≥ 90 % of the mirror's gold (`Sun`) cells are on its upper steps (≥ 110) and the shield's layer outlines span 55–72 % of the width at 80×24, 120×40 and 200×50; `JWST-A7` *(changed, M16)* the primary reads as JWST's honeycomb: walking between ≥ 30 neighbouring segments' centres crosses unlit dots between gilded ones, the centre is an unlit hole and the secondary shows as gold in front of it; stowed (5 s), the camera looks at the stack in profile and the bus joins the stack (the depth buffer is solid from the bus to the mirror); `JWST-A8` *(changed, M16)* deployed, at 80×24, 120×40 and 200×50 every lit cell is a braille cell on black (no fills), and both the cold top layer's `Cold` outline and the Sun-facing layer's `Hot` outline show; `JWST-A9` *(new, M16)* at 120×40 in the fairing beat (1.5 and 2.5 s) ≥ 85 % of the sampled points inside the visible segments (within half a segment radius of their centres) are lit gold dots.

### magnetosphere  (Earth & Sky, M21)
- **Intent**: Earth's magnetic field as a shield: the solar wind streams in from the left, is turned aside at a bow shock, squeezes the dayside field and drags the night side into a long tail; a gust compresses it, the tail snaps (reconnection) and auroral ovals flare at both poles.
- **Model**: field = Earth dipole + a Chapman–Ferraro image dipole that compresses the dayside + a tail current-sheet term that stretches the night side, all in Earth radii (R_E). Magnetopause standoff from pressure balance, `r_mp = r₀ · (p / p₀)^{−1/6}`; bow shock a paraboloid at `≈ 1.3 r_mp`. Field lines traced (RK4 along `B̂`) from fixed footpoint latitudes; a line is *closed* when both ends return to Earth, *open* otherwise. Solar-wind particles move left to right and are deflected along the shock/magnetopause (a drawn stand-in, not solved). Acts on a looping `Timeline` (~30 s): `quiet` → `gust` (dynamic pressure ×4, the field compresses smoothly) → `reconnection` (an X-line in the tail; stretched lines snap, a plasmoid loop slides tailward) → `substorm` (auroral ovals flare) → back to quiet.
- **Motion**: wind dust streams; the field breathes with the pressure; reconnection and the plasmoid; aurora flashes.
- **Resize**: field-line geometry in R_E is unchanged; projected polylines and the wind pool rebuilt. Field lines are retraced only when the model parameters change, amortised over frames (never a whole retrace in one frame).
- **Look** *(GEN-R25)*: 1-px braille field-line threads, closed lines `Cold`, open lines `Hot`, in two or three flat levels by footpoint latitude; solar wind as sparse `Particle` dust with short trails; the bow shock a dotted `Dim` thread; Earth a small half-block `Planet` disc, day side lit. Aurora ovals: short `Particle` (oxygen green) arcs at the field-line feet near both poles during `substorm`, brightest at onset, fading.
- **Title**: `Magnetosphere · wind meets field`; captions `quiet`, `gust`, `reconnection`, `substorm`.
- **Stylisation**: a cartoon field model (dipole + image dipole + sheet), not MHD; the tail-sheet term is a drawn stretch, not divergence-free; a meridian-plane cut; scales compressed (the tail is shortened to fit); the wind's path is drawn, not solved; the X-line and plasmoid are drawn, not traced; the trigger is simplified to a pressure gust (real substorms are loaded by a southward interplanetary field); the aurora is drawn 1.6–2.3 R_E out (real curtains are 100–300 km up).
- **Acceptance**: `MAG-A1` the standoff scales as `p^{−1/6}`: ×4 pressure moves it to `0.794 ×` within 1 %; `MAG-A2` near Earth (r ≤ 2 R_E) the field is a dipole within 5 %; `MAG-A3` closed lines are drawn `Cold` and open lines `Hot`, and both footpoints of every closed line lie on Earth; `MAG-A4` aurora dots appear only during `substorm` and only within 3 cells of a polar footpoint; `MAG-A5` `caption()` follows the acts and loops; `MAG-A6` no step retraces more than a bounded number of field lines (amortised); `MAG-A7` during `reconnection` at least one of the innermost night-side lobe lines flips open (`Hot`) → closed (`Cold`).
- **As built**: Earth's moment points south (−z); lines are traced outward from northern feet against `B̂`. View x ∈ [−18, 42] R_E, |z| ≤ 15.5 R_E, X-line at 16 R_E; r₀ = 10 R_E, image dipole at −2 r_mp; tail sheet `8·10⁻⁴ √p · tanh(z/1.5)` ramping in over 3–11 R_E; reconnection removes up to 80 % of it earthward of the X-line over 4 s, closing the 73.5° lobe line. Feet 45–74° on the day side and 45–78° on the night side (every other night line below 100 columns); at most 3 lines retraced per step. At substorm onset the plasmoid (`Accent`) leaves the X-line and the aurora flares: per polar cap one 1-px `Particle` 255 arc ±35° about the pole at 1.6 R_E, with up to 15 curtains (fixed thresholds) over its middle ±25° reaching 1.95–2.3 R_E, the night side brighter, while the high lines give way within 2.6 R_E. Wind dust `Particle` 150 on jittered sheath lanes.

### mars_rover  (Voyages, M7, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: a Perseverance-style rover crossing rust-red dunes under a butterscotch sky: parallax ridges, dust devils, a stop to drill a sample and beam data to an orbiter, as the sol passes from day to dusk (blue Martian sunset).
- **Model**: world-x terrain from seeded fractal value noise (≥ 3 octaves) that is a pure function of world x (deterministic, continuous); 3 parallax layers; rocker–bogie suspension: 6 wheels each placed on the terrain surface, body height/pitch from the rocker and bogie pivots; a `Timeline` sol: drive (18 s) → stop & deploy arm (3 s) → drill with dust (4 s) → stow (2 s) → transmit to an orbiter crossing the sky (4 s) → drive … ; dust particles from wheels and drill (`ParticlePool`); 0–2 dust devils drifting; Phobos crossing fast; sky/sun tint changes over a slower 90 s sol cycle (solar elevation at Jezero's latitude; Phobos on its real synodic period, scaled). Pose is solved by Newton iteration on the three wheel-contact residuals (rigid links, body pitch = rocker angle); far-side wheels coincide with the near ones in the side view. Dust over solid fills lightens rather than replaces the cell (a "veil" compositor), since cells have no background colour.
- **Inks**: terrain `Hot` (near) → `Dim` (far layers), day haze `Sun` (low levels), blue sunset glow `Cold`, dusk band/orbiter `Accent`, dust devils `Hot`, rover body `Text`, wheels/arm `Structure`, dust `Particle`, signal `Wave`.
- **Acceptance**: `MR-A1` every wheel contact point lies on the terrain surface (within 0.5 row); `MR-A2` terrain height is a pure deterministic function of world x — identical across scrolls, resizes and chunk boundaries, and continuous (adjacent columns differ by ≤ a bound); `MR-A3` the sol timeline visits its phases in order with their durations and loops.

### meteor_shower  (Earth & Sky, M13, M15)
- **Intent**: a dark summer sky at the Perseids' peak: meteors flash in every part of the sky, yet every streak points back to one radiant; bright ones leave green glowing trains that linger and twist in the upper-atmosphere wind.
- **Model**: meteoroids share one velocity vector (the stream); each meteor is a straight path segment through the ablation layer (Perseids: begins 105–125 km, brighter ones higher; ends 80–95 km, brighter ones deeper), seen through a gnomonic (rectilinear) lens facing north, which keeps paths straight and sends them all back to one vanishing point, the radiant; paths seen near the radiant are foreshortened. The radiant is placed in frame (upper third, off to one side) and marked by a faint ring of 16 braille dots (`Ramp(Starlight)` 80) with a dim `radiant` label; 30 % of meteors start within 22° of it, short foreshortened streaks fanning out of it. Pool of 48 meteors; arrivals are a Poisson process (seeded `ctx.rng`, 2.4/s, slowly swelling and ebbing); magnitudes from a power law (population index 2.2, faintest drawn 3.5, clamped at −4); light curve `√(27/4 u²(1 − u))` along the path. Each streak is drawn in braille: a white-hot head (a 2 × 2 dot blaze) and a tail behind it over 40–75 % of the path (longer for bright meteors), two dots wide just behind the head, then one, thinning toward its end (hashed dots dropped, ever more of them), white-hot at the head (its last 12 %), green (`Ramp(Aurora)` 175–235, magnesium and oxygen) back to 45 % of the tail, then yellow (`Ramp(Sulfur)` 120–190, sodium and iron) to its end; a faint green glow a pixel or two wide is stroked along the bright half of the tail into the trains' layer (a thin anti-aliased thread: bilinear splats every half pixel). Finished tracks join a faint braille composite that fades in 3.5 s (as in a stacked photograph of a shower; ≤ 32 kept), so at any moment several recent tracks show, all pointing back to the radiant. Nothing is drawn over the land: streaks, heads, glare, glow and trains stop at the treeline (only the sky's anti-aliased edge shows below the land's top). Trains (pool 12) from meteors of magnitude ≤ 0.6: 24 points along the brightest part of the track that drift with a sheared wind (two sinusoidal layers, 3.5 and 2.2 km/s time-lapsed, of vertical wavelength 7 and 12.5 km), stroked each step into a second `Glow` as a thin anti-aliased thread one to two pixels wide, dimming as it spreads (time constant 0.8 s) and fading over 1.8–3.2 s (the O I 557.7 nm afterglow, time-lapsed), drawn at half their toned level so the streaks stay the hero. Background: fixed stars (`Blackbody`), a mottled Milky Way (peak 50: a broad glow plus a brighter spine 0.38 widths wide, knotted with star clouds by two octaves of value noise along and across it) with a dark rift and dust lanes (two octaves of value noise stretched along the band, darkening it by up to 80 % near its spine), horizon airglow (44 at the horizon, e-folding 9°) against which an uneven spruce ridge with clearings (`Skyline`, trees 1.4–5.2°, gaps 0.7–2.9°) stands with anti-aliased edges.
- **Motion**: several meteors a second; trains drift, twist and fade. The sky itself is fixed: the loop spans minutes of a night, and a 15°/h turn would only jitter single dots.
- **Resize**: lens, radiant and sky rebuilt; the shower restarts with a train already in the sky and a meteor due at once.
- **Inks** *(M15, ADR-0018: M11's roles in flat steps; were M13/M14's `Starlight`/`Aurora`/`Sulfur`/`Blackbody` inks)*: meteor heads `Star` 255 braille only (a 2 × 2-dot blaze; the half-block glare is gone, M15 review), tails green `Particle` then yellow `Sun` (braille), each in two steps (220 where the trail's `200·f^1.2·(0.5 + 0.5·taper)` reaches 100, 110 below, nothing under level 12), trains (when drawn) one faint `Particle` 30 step, stars `Star` points (200 for the brightest, 50 the rest), composite tracks two dim grey steps (`Structure` 0, 60 from half their peak), land black; the radiant ring (when drawn) `Accent`. ASCII: trains by level; each flying meteor's last stretch as `-/\|` in `Sun` with the head `*`.
- **M23 look** (IMP-15): the Milky Way is a dim `Structure` braille layer with clumps and a dark lane, the background stars are band-biased, and meteors have longer, brighter tails (`TRAIL_TOP` 240) with brighter recent tracks and a small `Particle` 110 halo on bright heads.
- **Title**: `Meteor shower · comet dust burns`.
- **Stylisation**: rate far above a real ZHR (~100/h); paths drawn 5× longer than the ablation layer allows and flown ×3.5 faster so a streak reads on a terminal; streak colours simplified to white, green and yellow; trains brightened and time-lapsed; the radiant marked.
- **M14 geometry** (kept at M15; supersedes the Milky Way and airglow, the wake glow and the radiant mark): a black sky with a sparse starfield (the land is black, stars stop at its top); no sky pass at resize. Each meteor is a 1-dot braille streak, every dot drawn (no dithered thinning): the head white-hot (its 2 × 2-dot blaze the brightest thing), the trail stepping down from the head (`f` = 1) to black at its end, green then yellow past `WAKE_SPLIT`; no half-block wake glow. Persistent trains are modelled but not drawn (they read as blocky smears). Stars are neutral points (one in 15 brighter), no crosses. The rate is 4 per second (was 2.4) and the entry speed ×2.2 (was ×3.5), so a streak stays on screen longer and a frame is rarely empty. The radiant's ring and label are not drawn.
- **Acceptance**: `MET-A1` every meteor's track, extended backward, passes within 1° of the radiant; `MET-A2` meteors near the radiant are shorter on screen than those 60° away (mean ratio < 0.5); `MET-A3` magnitude counts follow the population index within ±20 % over 1000 samples; `MET-A4` trains fade monotonically and drift (and bend: the wind is sheared); `MET-A5` the pools never exceed capacity; `MET-A6` every meteor begins 105–125 km up and (its drawn path divided by the stretch) ends 80–95 km up; `MET-A7` the radiant projects inside the frame above the horizon (it is not marked, M14), and rendered streak cells are only white-hot `Star`, green `Particle` and yellow `Sun` (besides the `Star` points, grey `Structure` tracks and the `Accent` ring), both tail colours appearing *(changed, M15)*; `MET-A8` trains are thin (a stroked thread lights ≤ 2 pixel rows), short-lived (life ≤ 4.5 s), faint (`Particle` ≤ 128, *changed, M15*) and never drawn over the land (M14: not drawn at all).

### moon_landing  (Voyages, M7, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: Apollo 11's powered descent: the LM pitching over, throttling its descent engine above a cratered Mare Tranquillitatis, kicking up dust, "CONTACT LIGHT", "The Eagle has landed", then the flag and an earthrise.

### star_trails  (Earth & Sky, M13, M15)
- **Intent**: a long-exposure photograph building before your eyes: concentric coloured arcs grow around the celestial pole above a treeline, blue and orange stars drawing arcs of their own colour, the pole star a tiny bright circle.

The camera is a rectilinear (gnomonic) lens centred on the pole, which stands at the seeded latitude (35–50°): a star `ρ` from the pole is `f·tan ρ` from it on screen, so every trail is an exact circle about the pole, and the horizon (a great circle) is a straight row `f·tan φ` below it. Stars below the treeline (the shared `Skyline`, `src/shared/skyline.rs`: near spruces, anti-aliased) are hidden, and no trail dot is drawn over a land pixel (the paint-time test sees one height per dot column, which can slip between two spires).
- **Motion**: arcs lengthen continuously; circumpolar stars sweep round the pole; others rise and set behind the trees.
- **Resize**: projection rebuilt; the exposure is cleared and restarts. *(M14, GEN-R19)* The opening 10 s of exposure is painted in 0.1 s strides (was 1/60 s): a bright trail is the max of the dots it crosses and a faint one gathers light per dot of arc, so the picture does not depend on the stride, and a resize at 500×150 takes ~7 ms (was 15).
- **Inks** *(M15, ADR-0018: M11's roles in flat steps; were M14's `Blackbody` trails and `Starlight` haze)*: bright trails (magnitude ≤ 4.4) in braille dots drawn straight from the exposure buffer (the brightest thicker), coloured by the star's temperature (the buffer keeps the hottest trail's blackbody level, a model quantity): orange `Hot` 210 below 4300 K, yellow `Sun` 210 below 5500 K, white `Star` 220 below 7200 K, cyan-white `Wave` 200 below 11 000 K, blue `Cold` 230 above; faint trails (magnitude > 4.4) as dim `Dim` 150 braille dots on the same dot lattice as the bright trails (each dot lit where its fixed hash falls below its pixel's toned faint light, `0.36·level` faded with the exposure, none below 12; a cell with a bright trail takes the trail's colour), so the sky stays black between the arcs; the only half-block sky is the barely-lit horizon, one flat `Dim` 0 step (base level ≥ 12, ≤ 22, e-folding 2.5°); trees black; trail colours saturated ×1.15. ASCII: braille dot counts (`.:+#`); the skyglow is not drawn.
- **Title**: `Star trails · Earth turns`.
- **Stylisation**: time-lapse; a stacked exposure; star colours saturated; bright trails carry colour, faint ones only light; faint-trail exposure is even along the arc.
- **Acceptance**: `TRL-A1` every trail point stays at a constant distance from the pole (± 0.5 px), hence a constant angular distance; `TRL-A2` arc length in angle is the same for all stars (15.04°/h × time-lapse × exposure ≈ 150°) and on screen ∝ the projected pole distance `f·tan ρ`; `TRL-A3` stars with declination > 90° − latitude never cross the horizon; `TRL-A4` the exposure buffer stays allocated once (no growth) and resets at the loop; `TRL-A5` *(changed, M15)* braille trail inks are the five temperature steps or the faint `Dim` dots (both present); `TRL-A6` the sky is black at the top (the sky's pixel layer is black above the horizon glow), barely lit just above the land (≤ 24), and no braille trail cell lies over land; `TRL-A7` *(new, M15)* at 120×40 and 20 s the sky's half-block layer is black in the upper half of the frame (no haze between the arcs).

### total_solar_eclipse  (Earth & Sky, M13, M15, M16)
- **Intent**: totality seen from the ground: on a black sky over a dim skyline, the last sunlight breaks into Baily's beads and a diamond ring, then the pearly corona blooms with long streamers and small pink prominences at the limb; then it runs in reverse.
- **Model**: geometry in solar radii about the Sun's centre. The Moon (radius 1.03 R☉) moves along a tilted chord through the Sun's centre; its offset is piecewise linear in the `Timeline` (fast in the partial phases, slow through the beads and totality). Uncovered fraction `F`: the exact circle–circle lens area of the Moon's mountain-top envelope; once the envelope covers the Sun, `F` is the light through the limb valleys. The limb is a seeded valley profile (360 samples, 14 valleys up to 0.02 R☉ deep): a valley shows a bead when its floor still dips inside the photosphere, by its penetration depth. Beads and the diamond (the brightest bead, flaring most when it is nearly alone) are glare splats (`Glow`, `halo_kernel` 2 and 3) centred 0.8 px outside the lunar limb and masked by the Moon's disc, so the diamond is a small bead beside an intact dark disc. Photosphere: `raster::disc_cover` of Sun and Moon, `stellar::limb_darkening` (its colour shift softened, √I, so the limb is warm rather than an orange ring) and `brightness_temperature`; partly covered rim pixels at either disc's edge fade as pale `Starlight` (anti-aliased by brightness, not by a redder colour, and never in the sky's blue: no cyan rim on the crescent). Corona, precomputed per pixel in `resize`: a power law `r^{−3.3}` (toned with `asinh`, knee 0.35, with no taper or cut-off: it thins into the sky's light as far as it reaches) modulated by 6–10 helmet streamers (angular Gaussians crowding the equator, longer there: the solar-minimum shape, tapering and bending toward the equator with height), polar plumes and fine radial striations; shown only as the photosphere's glare falls. Prominences: 3–6 small arches on the limb. Sky brightness grows with `F^0.8` (dimming toward deep twilight through the partial phases) plus glare round the uncovered Sun; the Moon a black silhouette closing in, its rim anti-aliased against the air, and in the partial phases the Moon's shadow darkens the side of the sky it comes from first (and leaves last); a sunset glow along the bottom, strongest in totality; low hills (`Skyline`) in silhouette; Venus, Jupiter and two bright stars come out one by one below `F` = 0.12, Venus first.
- **Motion**: `Timeline`: partial (8 s) → beads + diamond ring (4 s) → totality (14 s) → diamond ring + beads (4 s) → partial (8 s), symmetric.
- **Resize**: geometry in solar radii centred; corona and prominence buffers rebuilt; the limb profile reseeded.
- **Inks** *(M15, ADR-0018: M11's roles in flat steps, no halos; were M14's `Starlight`/`HAlpha`/`Blackbody` inks and a glare halo)*: the sky is black in every act (no glare round the uncovered Sun: M14's glare table, twilight air, sunset glow and `Ice` sky are removed). The corona, the beads' glare (their kernels radius 2 and, for the diamond, 3) and the crescent's partly covered rim pixels are summed as light and tone-mapped (linear to level 140, rolling off exponentially toward 245), then drawn in four flat steps: `Star` 230 from 170, `Star` 110 from 95, grey `Structure` 100 from 45, `Structure` 0 from 18, black below, so the corona steps down into black. Bead cores (toned ≥ 200) `Star` 255. The diamond ring: through both diamond-ring acts the last light is a white `Star` 255 bead of 3 pixels on the limb at the contact point (the Sun's limb straight away from the Moon's centre, 0.8 px out), with 8 short braille spikes (reach max(8 dots, 1.6 solar radii in dots): `Star` 230 along the axes, `Sun` 200 at 0.55 of that on the diagonals), kept off the Moon's disc; meanwhile the corona is held back one step, blooming fully in totality. Prominences `Accent` 200 (small pink arches; the accent). The photosphere is stepped like M11's exoplanet star: `Sun` 255 at the centre (limb-darkened brightness √I > 0.93), `Sun` 170 (> 0.8), an orange `Hot` 200 limb, and pixels more than 45 % lit at the crescent's edge also `Hot` 200 (no pale fringe). The hills are black under a 1-px `Dim` 60 skyline; Venus, Jupiter and two bright stars `Star` 255 or 110 on black. ASCII: the Sun, beads and corona by level (`raster::ascii_ramp_cell`). The corona and prominence tables are filled 2 pixel rows per frame while the corona is hidden (`F ≥ 0.01`), or all at once when it is needed sooner, so a resize costs only the land table (GEN-R19); the picture is the same either way.
- **Look** *(M16, GEN-R25; supersedes the M15 Inks and the per-pixel corona of Model)*: braille on black, no half blocks. The corona is long streamer threads that follow a dipole-like field (solar minimum): a dipole whose equator is the seeded `equator` angle, closed loops `r = L sin²θ` (θ the colatitude; only the cusp loop `L = L_max` is drawn, seeded 2.2–2.6 per side, so the two helmets differ) under each helmet, and per pole and side 9 open field lines from footpoint colatitudes `θ₀ = s·θ_c` (`θ_c` the last closed footpoint, `sin²θ_c = 1/L_max`; `s` evenly spread with a seeded jitter, three crowding the separatrix as the streamer stalk) that fan out along the separatrix, `θ(r) = s·θ_sep(r)`, `θ_sep = asin √(min(r, L_max)/L_max)`, and run radially beyond the cusp out to 4 R☉: polar plumes bending toward the equator, helmet streamers drawn to a point. Colour along a thread is its brightness, falling outward as `e = gain·vis·r^−2.2` (gain seeded 0.5–1.0 per line, ×1.6 on the stalk lines, ×1.3 on closed loops): `Star` 230 from 0.55, `Star` 190 from 0.25, `Star` 150 from 0.1, `Star` 120 from 0.035, not drawn below, so the corona blooms outward from the limb as the visibility `vis` rises and each thread steps down into black; in the diamond-ring acts one step darker. In totality (vis > 0.5) the Moon's limb is a thin `Star` 200 ring (0.6 dots outside it). Prominences are tiny dotted arches of `Accent` 200 dots (every other dot). The uncovered photosphere is a checkerboard of braille dots (half the dots) in the limb-darkening steps `Sun` 255 (√I > 0.93), `Sun` 170 (> 0.8) and `Hot` 200, so the Moon's black disc bites a crisp crescent out of it. Beads are small `Star` 255 dot discs (radius 0.6 + 1.4 × penetration/valley depth) 0.8 px beyond the lunar limb; the diamond ring a `Star` 255 disc of radius 2 dots at the contact point with the 8 braille spikes, reach max(16 dots, 1.6 solar radii in dots), the inner 40 % of the axis spikes `Star` 255. The hills are black under a skyline of `Dim` 60 dots (one per dot column); Venus, Jupiter and two bright stars `Star` 255 or 110 dots. Nothing is drawn on the Moon's disc.
- **M23 look** (IMP-15): only each helmet's cusp loop is drawn (over its middle arc), open streamers end at hash-seeded radii and each corona rank is stippled (45/65/85/100 %), so the corona fades into dots with varied tips instead of hard concentric bands. Removing the old loops shifted the RNG stream, so the skyline and prominences were reseeded and the re-recorded frames show it.
- **Title**: `Total eclipse · Moon hides Sun`; captions `partial`, `diamond ring`, `totality`.
- **Stylisation**: totality lengthened relative to the partial phases (real partial phases last ~25× longer than totality; here 8 s against 14 s); the Moon's apparent motion is uneven, ~180× faster in the partial phases than through totality (really uniform); limb valleys ~10× real lunar relief; streamers procedural; the corona's power law steepened to `r^{−3.3}` so it reads on a terminal; a black daytime sky.
- **Acceptance**: `ECL-A1` the uncovered fraction is 1 at the start, 0 at mid-totality and symmetric in time (the smooth crescent exactly; the bead light, which differs between second and third contact, to within 0.005); `ECL-A2` beads appear only when the uncovered fraction is in (0, 0.005) and lie on the Moon's limb (within the valley depth); `ECL-A3` *(changed, M16)* the corona is drawn only while the uncovered fraction < 0.01 (its visibility is 0 above that, and no corona thread is drawn then); `ECL-A4` *(changed, M16)* corona brightness falls outward along every thread (its step never rises with `r`); `ECL-A5` captions follow the Timeline; `ECL-A6` *(changed, M16)* in totality consecutive segments of a thread differ by at most one corona step (black counting as the step below the faintest); `ECL-A7` *(changed, M16)* the sky's corner stays black through the partial phase, and no dot is lit on the Moon's disc (1.5 dots inside its limb) through the beads and diamond ring; `ECL-A8` the timing stylisation holds (8 s partial, 14 s totality, a partial-to-totality speed ratio of 150–210); `ECL-A9` *(changed, M16)* in the partial phase every photosphere dot is `Sun` 255, `Sun` 170 or `Hot` 200 and lies on the uncovered disc; the stalk threads reach 3.5 R☉ in totality (no cut-off); open threads bend toward the equator (their colatitude grows outward) and stay outside the closed loops. `ECL-A10` *(changed, M16)* black overhead: in every act the top quarter's corner cells are blank (but for the planets and stars), the land holds only its skyline (at most one lit cell per column below the horizon's top), and in totality between 1.3 and 3 R☉ every lit cell is a corona step, a prominence or a `Star`; `ECL-A11` *(changed, M16)* mid diamond-ring act (11 s) the diamond's bead and ≥ 4 braille spike cells show and no corona segment is the corona's brightest step, and in totality (20 s) there are no spikes; `ECL-A12` *(new, M16)* the corona threads follow the field: every closed-loop point satisfies `r = L sin²θ` (to 1e-9), every thread is one dot thick (≥ 85 % of its dots have at most 3 lit dots across it within ±2), and in totality the Moon's limb is a thin `Star` 200 ring.

### voyager_journey  (Voyages, M7, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: Voyager 2's Grand Tour, 1977–1989 in 48 s: launch from Earth, gravity assists at Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune with flyby flashes and captions, then out past the heliopause to a closing "Pale Blue Dot" moment.
- **Model**: heliocentric top-down map with a monotone radial compression (e.g. `ρ = R·log(1 + r/r₀)/log(1 + r_max/r₀)`, AU→rows) so Earth…Neptune all fit; planets on circular orbits with real mean motions and J2000-ish mean longitudes; real dates: launch 1977-08-20, Jupiter 1979-07-09, Saturn 1981-08-26, Uranus 1986-01-24, Neptune 1989-08-25; the spacecraft path is a smooth curve through the planets' positions at those dates (e.g. Hermite/polar interpolation in (r, φ) between encounter points with monotone r), time compression with slow-downs near encounters (via `ease`), a date/distance readout; 48 s mission + 8 s epilogue, then loop. Planet elements from JPL/Standish "Keplerian Elements for Approximate Positions" (J2000); the path is a monotone cubic Hermite (PCHIP) curve through each planet's model position on its encounter date (a shape, not a propagated trajectory). Captions sit at the top (Uranus/Neptune are near the bottom in the real geometry). The epilogue credits the Pale Blue Dot to Voyager 1 (which took it); the sight line from Voyager 2 is artistic licence. **Stylisation**: the path is interpolated through the real encounter geometry; the gravity assists the title names ("planets fling it on") are depicted, not integrated.
- **Inks**: Sun `Sun`, orbits `Dim`, planets `Planet`, spacecraft `Accent` with `Structure` trail, captions `Text`, flyby flash `Hot`.
- **Acceptance**: `VOY-A1` at each encounter date the spacecraft coincides with the planet (same model position, and within 0.5 cell on screen); `VOY-A2` planet angular speeds follow Kepler (period ratios `(a₁/a₂)^{3/2}`) and match real periods within 1 %; `VOY-A3` mission time → calendar date is monotone and prints the exact encounter dates at encounters; `VOY-A4` the radial mapping is strictly monotone and fits Neptune's orbit on screen.
