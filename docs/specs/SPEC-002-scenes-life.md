### action_potential  (Physics of Life, M10, M12, M15, M20)
- **Intent**: a nerve impulse made visible. A stylised neuron rests in the dark: soma, branching dendrites, a long unmyelinated axon and a fan of synaptic terminals, all drawn as dim membrane outlines. A stimulus fires the soma, and a bright pulse of depolarisation runs down the axon with a cool after-hyperpolarisation wake behind it. It lights the terminals, which release a small puff of transmitter. An oscilloscope inset, wired to the recording electrode by a faint dotted lead, draws the classic spike waveform at the moment the pulse passes the electrode. A looping protocol shows four things in turn: a single spike, a train that slows as it runs into its own refractory wake, two spikes annihilating in collision, and the all-or-none threshold.
- **Model**: Hodgkin & Huxley (1952) squid giant axon, in the modern rest = −65 mV convention (Dayan & Abbott 2001).
  - **Cable equation**: `C_m ∂V/∂t = (a / 2R_i) ∂²V/∂x² − ḡ_Na m³h (V − E_Na) − ḡ_K n⁴ (V − E_K) − ḡ_L (V − E_L) + I_stim`, with `C_m = 1 μF/cm²`, `ḡ_Na = 120`, `ḡ_K = 36`, `ḡ_L = 0.3 mS/cm²`, `E_Na = +50`, `E_K = −77` and `E_L = −54.387 mV`.
  - **Rates** (ms⁻¹, V in mV): `α_m = 0.1(V + 40)/(1 − e^{−(V+40)/10})`, `β_m = 4 e^{−(V+65)/18}`, `α_h = 0.07 e^{−(V+65)/20}`, `β_h = 1/(1 + e^{−(V+35)/10})`, `α_n = 0.01(V + 55)/(1 − e^{−(V+55)/10})`, `β_n = 0.125 e^{−(V+65)/80}`. All are scaled by `φ = 3^{(T − 6.3)/10}` with `T = 6.3 °C`, so `φ = 1`. The removable singularities at −40 and −55 mV go through an `x/(1 − e^{−x})` helper with a series branch.
  - **Geometry**: axon radius `a = 238 μm` and `R_i = 35.4 Ω·cm` (HH 1952) give `D = a/(2 R_i C_m) = 0.336 cm²/ms`. The cable is `L = 10 cm` in `N = 200` compartments (`Δx = 0.5 mm`, ≈ 1/14 of the 7 mm resting length constant), with sealed (no-flux) ends.
  - **Sub-step (10 μs)**: first a Rush–Larsen (1978) exponential update of m, h and n, which is exact for frozen V and stays in `[0, 1]`. Then backward-Euler V with the ionic conductances frozen (Hines 1984), solved as a tridiagonal M-matrix by the Thomas algorithm into preallocated scratch. This is unconditionally stable; explicit Euler would need a step ≤ 3.7 μs and RK4 ≤ 5 μs. Against a converged fine-grid run (Crank–Nicolson, Δx = 0.05 mm, 0.5 μs: 12.31 and 18.72 m/s) the velocity error is 0.4 % at 6.3 °C and 0.9 % at 18.5 °C.
  - **Clock**: it counts integer sub-steps (`h = 0.01 ms`, through an accumulator with 1e-9 slack), so how frames chop time cannot change results. Slow motion is 4 ms of axon time per simulated second (250×).
  - **Measured physiology** (converged): propagated peak +37.9 mV; after-hyperpolarisation minimum −75.9 mV; V > −40 mV for 2.3 ms at a point (a ≈ 2.9 cm footprint); conduction velocity 12.3 m/s at 6.3 °C and 18.7 m/s at 18.5 °C (HH computed 18.8 m/s and measured 21.2 m/s); threshold ≈ 90 μA/cm² for a 0.3 ms pulse over the first 0.25 cm.
  - **Protocol**: a looping `Timeline` of 18 s (= 72 ms). `single` 3 s: a soma pulse at 0.2 s. `train` 8 s: pulses at 3.0, 5.5 and 8.0 s, 10 ms apart. `collision` 4.5 s: soma and terminal end stimulated together at 13.0 s, the neurophysiologist's collision test. `threshold` 2.5 s: a 0.5× pulse at 16.0 s. Strong pulses are 270 μA/cm² (3× threshold) for 0.3 ms over the first (or last) 0.25 cm, and the weak pulse is 45 μA/cm².
  - **Refractory timing**: every strong pulse starts ≥ 9.5 ms after the last spike passed its site. That is beyond the ≈ 6–8 ms refractory limit, so every strong pulse fires, but inside the relative refractory period, so each train spike runs slower than the last (measured 10.9, 10.1 and 9.8 m/s, against 12.3 isolated). The model state runs continuously across loops, and the second loop repeats the first to within 0.01 s. The first loop starts from rest; every later one starts 20 ms after the collision, in its supernormal phase (at 3–7 cm `h` is ≈ 0.01 above and `n` ≈ 0.006 below their resting values), so its single spike runs 0.8 % faster: 12.36 against 12.26 m/s. Moving the weak pulse (15.2–17.0 s) does not change this. Later loops repeat one another to 1e-6 m/s.
  - **Weak pulse**: it lands on the after-hyperpolarisation left by the collision and lifts the soma to ≈ −61 mV (≈ −57 mV from rest). It spreads passively ≈ 1 cm and fades.
  - **Stimulus markers**: each pulse lights a stimulus marker for 0.25 s, at a level proportional to its amplitude, so the weak kick visibly happens. The marker is `>` left of the soma, and `<` just before the terminals for the far-end pulse.
  - **Stylisation**: the soma, dendrites and terminal arbor are drawn, not simulated; the real giant axon is a syncytium grown from the stellate ganglion. The soma shows V of compartment 0, and the terminal arbor fans out the last 0.8 cm of the cable.
  - **Vesicles**: at each 0 mV up-crossing at the cable end, every bouton emits 4 vesicle dots (`ParticlePool`, capacity 20 = 5 boutons × 4). They drift 1–2 cells across the cleft and fade within 0.4 s.
- **Motion**: at t = 0 the neuron rests, showing only its outline. The stimulus marker and soma flash at 0.2 s. By 1 s the pulse is a third of the way along the axon (GEN-R9). It reaches the terminals at ≈ 2.3 s, with a bouton flash and a vesicle puff. The trace scrolls continuously (a 20 ms = 5 s window) and draws each spike as the pulse passes the electrode (compartment 100, 5 cm, ≈ 1.1 s after the stimulus). In the train, each spike leaves the soma as the previous one reaches the terminals, and each is visibly slower. In the collision beat, two pulses converge and vanish near mid-axon at ≈ 14.1 s. In the threshold beat the marker blinks and a faint soma glow spreads about a centimetre and fades within ≈ 1 s. Nothing travels, and the trace stays flat. The weak kick lands 12 ms after the collision spike left the soma, on its after-hyperpolarisation (≈ −69 mV), so its half-threshold amplitude and the residual refractoriness both hold it below threshold; AP-A5 checks that the same pulse also fails from rest. The loop is 18 s.
- **Resize**: the simulation does not depend on size, so no model state is touched. Geometry is rebuilt from normalised parameters:
  - the soma centre and radius;
  - the axon path, stored as a per-pixel table of arclength `s`, with V linearly interpolated between compartments;
  - the dendrite tree. Its topology is drawn from `ctx.rng` on the first resize only and stored in a fixed array of ≤ 40 segments, each ending at a node `(angle, ρ)`: polar about the soma centre, ρ soma radii beyond the rim, drawn at `r + k·ρ`. The tree is radial and planar. Three primaries leave the rim radially within a ±1.45 rad cone pointing left: one up (−1.15 rad), one down (+1.15) and one at ±0.55 on a random side, each jittered by ±0.08, so the stimulus marker straight left stays clear. Each primary owns the wedge of the cone between the midpoints to its neighbours. Every fork splits its wedge at the parent's angle and turns each child 22–38° off the parent's radial line (measured at the child's end), clamped inside its half-wedge. Since subtrees keep to disjoint convex wedges about the soma centre, no two branches ever cross. Radial reach by depth is 0.9, 0.7, 0.55 and 0.45 soma radii (±20 %). Primaries and their children always fork and about half the depth-2 twigs fork again. On resize each primary's subtree gets its own scale k, the largest that fits left of, above or below the soma (at most 1.3 r and 1.6× the tightest subtree's k: the room is mostly above and below, and terminal rows are coarse). The drawn depth follows the soma radius r: depth 1 below r = 4 cells, depth 2 below 8, depth 3 from 8 up, so a small neuron draws a few long branches instead of a scribble. A twig spanning under 2 cells (`max(|Δx|/2, |Δy|/4)` in dots) is dropped with its subtree; a short primary is stretched to 2 cells instead;
  - the terminal branches, the electrode and stimulus markers, and the trace panel rectangle.

  Tiers: the full neuron when w ≥ 24 and h ≥ 8; otherwise only the axon, as one Pixels row spanning the width. The tube holds 4 pixel rows of axoplasm (a 3-cell tube) when h ≥ 40, else 2. The far-end marker `<` sits just right of the boutons, clamped on screen. The trace panel appears only when w ≥ 56 and h ≥ 16, starting at row 1 and column 0.625 w, and ideally ending at row 1 + 0.27 h. The trace's after-hyperpolarisation dip lies on or just above the panel's bottom row, so that row ends at least two rows above the top bouton, with one blank row between, and the panel shrinks to fit (at least 4 rows). If 4 rows do not fit, the panel keeps its height and ends three columns left of the arbor's first column (the trunk end), provided it keeps at least 12 columns; otherwise there is no panel. It always ends at least two columns from the right edge (x1 ≤ w − 3), the boutons' margin. A dotted lead joins the electrode to the scope. It climbs from the cell above the electrode to the trace's resting baseline and then runs along the baseline's dot row to one blank cell before the `-65` tick. Where the electrode sits under the tick or the panel, the lead stops one row below them. Drawing happens in `update` and `resize` (no `RefCell`): the axon, soma and boutons go into a `raster::Pixels`, the dendrites, arbor and trace into one `raster::Dots` and the vesicles into another; `render` only blits them and writes markers and labels.
- **Inks**: axoplasm, soma fill and boutons use `Role::Div`. For V ≥ rest the value is `x = √((V − V_rest)/105 mV)`, peaking at ≈ +0.99; the square root keeps millivolt-scale subthreshold responses visible. Below rest it is `x = −0.55·((V_rest − V)/12 mV)^1.5` (≈ −0.48 at the −75.9 mV minimum): the shallow tail of the after-hyperpolarisation stays near the background and only the deep, recent wake reads cool, quieter than the spike. Filled regions (axoplasm, soma fill) are left blank within 6 Div levels above rest (≈ +0.2 mV) and 12 below (≈ −3.7 mV), so a resting axon reads as its two membrane lines and the half-block glyphs change as a spike passes. Rest (level 128) sits near the background, so outlines carry the form: `Structure` levels 60–110 for the membrane lines above and below the axon, the soma rim, dendrites and terminal branches. The recording electrode, stimulus markers and trace are `Accent`; the electrode's lead is `Dim` 60 and dotted (every other dot); the trace baseline and the `+40`/`-65` ticks are `Dim`, and `+40` shows only while the window holds a sample above −20 mV (it labels a spike's scale and would float alone over a flat baseline); vesicles are `Particle`; the label is `Text`.
- **ASCII**: in the `Pixels::blit` closure, Div pixels map by level: `≥ 242` → `@` (V ≳ +20 mV), `≥ 211` → `#` (V ≳ −20 mV), `≥ 145` → `+` (V ≳ −63 mV), `< 84` → `~` (V ≲ −74 mV, the deep, recent after-hyperpolarisation only), else rest. At rest the axon is `=`. Structure-only cells are `=` on the axon membrane and `o` on the boutons. The soma is drawn at cell resolution: its rim is the ellipse of radii r × r/2 cells (cells are two pixels tall), rim cells being those inside with a 4-neighbour outside, drawn `o` whatever the voltage; cells inside show the fill's voltage glyph. Dendrites, the terminal arbor and the trace are line art, one glyph per cell. Dendrites and arbor use slope glyphs (`-` `/` `\` `|`). Dendrites are drawn shallowest first, each child skipping its first cell (its parent's last), and never overwrite a drawn cell, so the parent's stroke wins every junction. An arbor cell above rest shows its voltage glyph (`+` `#` `@`), except at the junction with the trunk. Below rest an arbor cell keeps its stroke and a bouton keeps its `o`, in the `Div` ink, so the cool colour carries the after-hyperpolarisation and the fan never dissolves into `~`. The `~` of the deep wake is kept for the axon and soma fill, where the pulse pattern below depends on it. The trace uses `|` where it crosses a cell top to bottom, `/` or `\` where it rises or falls within one, and `_` or `-` for a flat run in a cell's lower or upper half; the baseline shows as `.` ticks only where the trace has left it. Vesicles use the Dots density fallback. The electrode is `v` and the stimulus markers are `>` and `<`. The lead is `:` in every row of its climb and `.` in every other column along the baseline, drawn first so that everything else overwrites it. A spike on the axon reads `==~~~~~~~~===++++#####@@@@#+++===` moving right: `~` marks only the deep, recent wake, so the resting `=` frames each pulse.
- **Label**: right-aligned on the last row with `draw::label_right` (GEN-R13), drawn only when h ≥ 3. The form depends on the room right of the title (`title::readout_room`); each threshold is the form's length with the longest beat, so the form never flickers with the beat:
  - room ≥ 62: `Hodgkin-Huxley 1952 | squid axon 6.3C | v=12.3 m/s | collision`;
  - room ≥ 38: `HH squid 6.3C | v=12.3 m/s | collision`;
  - room ≥ 32: `HH 6.3C | v=12.3 m/s | collision`;
  - room ≥ 7: `HH 6.3C`;
  - none below.

  Every form with a velocity keeps the temperature: 12.3 m/s is right for 6.3 °C, not the textbook 21 m/s at 18.5 °C. `v` is measured live from the latest orthodromic spike's 0 mV crossings at 3 cm and 7 cm. The 3 cm crossing must come first, with the 7 cm crossing 1.5–6 ms later, so antidromic and colliding spikes are ignored. The readout shows `v=--.- m/s` before the first spike, then 12.3 after the first loop's single spike (12.4 in every later loop, in the collision's supernormal wake; see Refractory timing) and 10.9 → 9.8 along the train, and holds through the collision and threshold beats. It returns to `--.-` when the loop wraps, so the slowest train spike never sits beside `single`. The last field is the current beat name. Everything is ASCII, formatted in a stack buffer.
- **Perf**: 6.67 sub-steps × 200 compartments ≈ 1.3k compartment updates per frame (9 `exp`, ~40 flops and a Thomas sweep each). Rasterising covers ≈ 800 axon pixels, ≈ 150 soma pixels, ≤ 40 dendrite segments and ≤ 400 trace columns, then the blits. Measured ≈ 0.09 ms per `update(1/60)` + `render` at 200×50 (criterion, 2026-09-27: 89.8 μs). `dt = 0.25` costs 100 sub-steps, ≈ 1.5 ms. If the bench ever shows > 0.4 ms, tabulate the six rates at build time (0.1 mV steps on `[−100, +60]` mV, with linear interpolation).
- **Acceptance**: `AP-A1` with gates at steady state, `I_ion(V) = 0` has its root at `−65.0 ± 0.1 mV` (bisection on `[−80, −50]`), an unstimulated cable stays within ±0.05 mV of rest for 50 ms, and the α helpers are finite and continuous at −40 and −55 mV (`|α(V₀ ± 1e-7) − α(V₀)| < 1e-6`); `AP-A2` the propagated spike at 5 cm (strong pulse) peaks in `[+34, +42]` mV (reference +37.9), dips to a minimum in `[−77.0, −74.5]` mV (−75.9) and is back within 0.5 mV of rest 25 ms after the peak, and over one full 18 s protocol every gate stays in `[0, 1]` and every compartment's V in `[E_K − 0.5, E_Na]` (prototype −76.2 to +42.2 mV; ≈ 1 s in a debug build); `AP-A3` the conduction velocity between the 0 mV crossings at 3 cm and 7 cm is `12.3 m/s ± 3 %` at 6.3 °C with the scene discretisation (fine-grid reference 12.32, scene scheme 12.26); `AP-A4` with `φ = 3^{(18.5 − 6.3)/10}` the same solver gives `18.8 m/s ± 3 %`, HH 1952's computed velocity (scene scheme 18.55); `AP-A5` all-or-none: the scene's weak pulse (45 μA/cm²) from rest keeps V at 3 cm below −60 mV, while strong pulses at 1.5×, 3× and 6× the 90 μA/cm² threshold give peaks at 5 cm within 0.3 mV of one another; `AP-A7` `60 × update(1/60)` and `4 × update(0.25)` leave bitwise-identical V and gate arrays (both runs execute the same 400 sub-steps), and each 18 s loop issues exactly the scripted pulses (0.2, 3.0, 5.5, 8.0 s; 13.0 s at both ends; 16.0 s weak) in order, repeating identically in the next loop; `AP-A8` over one protocol loop the live velocity readout prints exactly `12.3` after the first loop's single spike, then 10.9, 10.1 and 9.8 m/s after the three train spikes (each ± 0.3 m/s), and the collision and threshold beats leave it unchanged; the readout returns to `--.-` when the loop wraps, and the second and third loops' single spikes both print exactly `12.4`; `AP-A9` the drawn dendrites, for seeds 1–24 at 24×8, 40×12, 56×16, 80×24, 120×40 and 200×50: exactly three primaries are drawn, every drawn segment spans at least 2 cells, and no two drawn segments cross (a shared endpoint is not a crossing).
- **Look (M20)** (GEN-R25, membrane threads + ion dust; supersedes **Inks** and **ASCII** above where they disagree): model, protocol, clock, trace panel, markers, label and tiers are unchanged. Everything except the label, markers and trace axis text is drawn on one `raster::Dots` lattice in `prepare`, so there are no half-block cells. *Neuron*: the soma is a 1-px `Structure` membrane outline shaped as a rounded teardrop (a circle of the old radius whose right side tapers over ≈ 1 radius into the axon hillock, meeting the axon membranes without a gap). Inside sits a nucleus: a `Dim` stipple atom of σ ≈ 0.3 r with a 1-dot `Structure` nucleolus. The axon is two 1-px `Structure` membrane threads at the old tube edges. Dendrites keep the seeded, non-crossing radial tree but are drawn as gently curved 1-px braille threads (each segment a quadratic Bézier bowing 8–15 % of its length: a primary towards its wedge's middle, a fork's child away from its sibling, the bow halved until the control point stays inside the segment's own wedge; a primary stretched straight to reach 2 cells stays straight), tapering in level with depth (`Structure` 110 → 60), with short spines: ≈ one 1–2-dot stub per 6 dots of segment at depth ≥ 2. The terminal arbor is curved threads taking `Hot` or `Cold` with the local V, ending in boutons that are stipple atoms (σ ≈ 1.3 dots, peak 1.0) and flash `Hot` (peak 1.6) for 0.4 s when the spike arrives; vesicles stay as single dots. *Voltage as ion dust*: the axoplasm and soma interior are stippled with fixed per-dot thresholds (`Dots::stipple`, CORE-R107) at a density that follows the local V: `p = 0.03` at rest; above rest `p = 0.03 + 0.50·x` in `Hot` with x as in **Inks** (`Hot` only above rest + 0.5 mV, so the cable's sub-0.05 mV drift stays cool); below rest `p = 0.03 + 0.30·|x|` in `Cold` at level `70 + 260·|x|`. So a resting axon is sparse cool dust, the spike a dense (never solid) bright puff that travels, and the after-hyperpolarisation a light cool wake. *Ion flux*: where a compartment depolarises fast (dV/dt > 20 mV/ms), Na⁺ ions are emitted (on the trunk, full tier only, with chance 0.4 per compartment per update) as `Hot` dots at the membrane drifting inward over 0.25 s; where it repolarises fast (dV/dt < −10 mV/ms), K⁺ ions (chance 0.15) as `Cold` dots drift outward across the membrane over 0.25 s. A fixed `ParticlePool` (capacity 160) is filled in `update` from `ctx.rng`, so it is deterministic and allocation-free. The trace stays a braille thread (`Accent`), its axes `Dim`. Geometry is built in `resize` (outline, membranes, nucleus, dendrites and lead as a fixed list of dots; the soma interior by an inward fill); `prepare` replays it and adds dust, arbor, boutons, vesicles, ions and trace on one `raster::Dots`; `render` only blits it and writes markers and labels. In the small tier the axon is one cell row: membrane threads on its top and bottom dot rows and two dot rows of dust; the membranes are 9 dot rows apart when h ≥ 40, else 5. *ASCII*: the lattice falls back to dot-count glyphs (`.` `:` `+` `#`); the trace and the electrode lead keep their line art (trace `|` `/` `\` `_` `-`, baseline `.`; lead `:` up its climb and `.` every other column along the baseline); markers `>` `<`, electrode `v`. *Perf* (M20, `worst_frame`): ≈ 0.13 ms mean and 0.15 ms worst per frame at 200×50, 0.53 ms worst at 500×150.

### boids_flocking  (Living Systems, M10, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: a starling murmuration at dusk. About a thousand specks pour and fold like smoke above the roost, and the flock's texture shifts as it banks. Every 14 s a falcon, the only warm glyph on screen, stoops into it. A bright agitation wave then runs through the flock faster than any bird flies, and the murmuration bulges away from the attack and closes behind it. Birds copy a neighbour's escape manoeuvre (Hemelrijk et al. 2015), so the wave outruns any bird, as Potts (1984) observed in dunlin flocks when he found manoeuvres passing between neighbours faster than a bird's startle reaction (his chorus-line hypothesis).
- **Model**: Reynolds (1987) boids in 2-D (the sky plane) on the square braille dot lattice `W × H = 2w × 4h` dots (CORE-R41; 1 dot ≈ 0.3 m at scale σ = 1). The open sky is `W × H_s`, with `H_s = H − 4` (the label row) when the label is drawn and `H_s = H` otherwise. Population `N = clamp(round(0.1·w·h), 24, 1000)` (80×24 ⇒ 192, 200×50 ⇒ 1000). Length scale `σ = clamp(√(8wh / (80 N)), 0.5, 4)`, which is 1 whenever N is uncapped, so the flock always covers the same share of the sky. **Small sky**: `ρ = clamp((min(W, H_s) − 44)/16, 0, 1)` is 0 up to a 44-dot shorter side (a 12-row terminal with its label) and 1 from 60 dots (16 rows) up. At full size and speed a flock crosses a small sky in about a second and is pressed against its walls, so there the scene uses `σ·lerp(0.7, 1, ρ)` (clamped to [0.5, 4] again), roost stiffness `lerp(2.0, κ, ρ)` s⁻², margin fraction `lerp(0.2, 0.25, ρ)`, and roost amplitudes multiplied by ρ. At 80×24 and larger ρ = 1, and nothing changes. Every length, speed and acceleration below is multiplied by σ, except the wall margin `m`. **Neighbourhood**: the topological `k = 7` nearest within a metric horizon `R = 12` dots (3.6 m), after Ballerini et al. (2008, `n_c ≈ 6.5`) and Hemelrijk & Hildenbrandt (2011, StarDisplay). The core `SpatialHash` (CORE-R54) with cell size `R/4` is rebuilt every substep. Each query first tries the disc reaching the farthest of the bird's previous k neighbours at their current positions (its radius padded by 1e-9 relative, so the square root and the re-squaring never drop that farthest neighbour), and falls back to the full horizon R when that disc holds fewer than k birds. Once a disc holds k birds everything outside it is strictly farther, so the answer is exact for any hint. It keeps the k best in a fixed `[(d², idx); 7]` insertion array, with ties broken by index. The lists `nbr[N][7]` are built once per substep and shared by forces, contagion and rendering. **Acceleration of bird i** is the sum of five terms. (1) Separation `A_s Σ_{j∈N_i, d<r_s} (1 − d/r_s)(x_i − x_j)/d` with `A_s = 150` dots/s² and `r_s = 4` dots (1.2 m). A pair with `d < 1e-6` pushes along ±x instead, and the lower index goes −x. (2) Alignment `β(v̄_N − v_i)` with `β = 5` s⁻¹. (3) Cohesion `γ(x̄_N − x_i)` with `γ = 1` s⁻². (4) Speed relaxation `(v₀ − |v|)/τ_v · v̂` with `v₀ = 30` dots/s (9 m/s, starling cruise) and `τ_v = 0.5` s. (5) Roost attraction `κ(x_roost − x̄) + κ_i(x̄ − x_i)`, where `x̄` is the flock's centroid. The first part, with `κ = 0.45` s⁻², is the same for every bird, so it steers the whole flock around the roost (period `2π/√κ ≈ 9.4` s) without squeezing it. A polarised flock cruising at v₀ orbits the roost at radius `v₀/√κ ≈ 45` dots, which fits the 96-dot height of an 80×24 sky (κ = 0.3 would give 55). The second part keeps strays with the flock: `κ_i = κ` for N ≤ 300, falling log-linearly in N to 0.03 s⁻² at N = 1000, because a trap as stiff as κ on every bird of a large flock squeezes it into a round, evenly grainy ball (BOID-A7). The roost moves on a Lissajous path `(W/2 + a_x sin 2πt/23, H_s/2 + a_y sin 2πt/37)` with `a_x = min(0.25 W, max(0, W/2 − R_f − m))` and `a_y = min(0.2 H_s, max(0, H_s/2 − R_f − m))`, where `R_f = √(12N/π)·σ` is the flock's radius, so a flock at the roost clears the soft-bound margin. Soft bounds add `A_b·min(((m − d_edge)/m)², 4)` inward (`A_b = 200` dots/s²) within `m = 0.25·min(W, H_s)` dots of an edge of the sky; this wide, gentle cushion turns the flock back through the middle of the sky instead of piling it against the frame. The total is capped at `|a| ≤ 250` dots/s². After the velocity update, the velocity is turned by heading noise `θ ~ gaussian(0, √(2 D_θ h))` with `D_θ = 0.1` rad²/s, applied as the small-angle rotation `(v + θ v⊥)/√(1 + θ²)`, and `|v|` is clamped to `[0.5, 2]·v₀`. **Integration**: synchronous semi-implicit Euler at `h = SIM_DT = 1/60` s (all accelerations come from the old state and go into `vel_next`, then the buffers swap), with the substep count from the core `FixedStep(SIM_DT, 15)` (CORE-R9..R11). A specular wall at the edges of the sky `[0, W] × [0, H_s]` (the label row is not sky) reflects the position and negates the normal velocity. It is only a safety net; the soft bounds do the work (BOID-A4). **Falcon**: a looping 14 s `Timeline`. Patrol 7 s: slow 4 s circles of radius `r_c = 0.8 v₀ · 4 s / 2π` (≈ 15 dots) around an anchor over whichever upper corner is farther from the flock, inset `max(0.12 W, r_c + 4σ)` from the side wall and `max(0.2 H_s, r_c + 4σ)` from the top (never past the middle of the sky), at 0.8 v₀ and turning ≤ 2 rad/s. Stoop 2.5 s: speed eased from 0.8 to 3 v₀ with `ease::in_out_cubic`, steering toward the flock centroid predicted 0.5 s ahead, turning ≤ 3 rad/s. Pursue 2 s: 2 v₀, turning ≤ 1.5 rad/s, so the birds can out-turn it. Climb 2.5 s: 1.5 v₀ back onto the patrol circle, which it joins tangentially instead of overshooting the anchor. In every beat it heeds the walls: within `r_c` of an edge of the sky, `3·min(((r_c − d)/r_c)², 4)` along the inward normal (the birds' soft-bound profile with margin `r_c`) is added to its unit desired heading, and while heading into a wall its speed is divided by `1 + 2·max(0, −v̂·push)` (not below 0.8 v₀). Should it still reach an edge of the sky, it reflects specularly, so it never slides along the frame (BOID-A4). It never removes birds. **Escape wave**: Hemelrijk et al. (2015) showed that agitation waves arise only when birds repeat an escape manoeuvre, and that the dark band comes from orientation, not density. Each stoop is an attack with an id, and alarms can fire only inside that attack's stoop and pursue windows. A calm bird fires at most once per attack, in one of two ways. Directly: the falcon is within `r_flee = 12` dots, and the turn sign points away from it. By contagion: a topological neighbour fired at least `L = 2` substeps (33 ms) earlier and is still alarmed; the sign is copied from the lowest-index such neighbour. Fire steps are stored, so the outcome does not depend on update order. An alarmed bird (age < `T_a = 0.35` s = 21 substeps) adds a lateral `160` dots/s² with its sign and relaxes toward `1.3 v₀`. While the falcon is inside `r_flee` it also flees at `200(1 − d/r_flee)` dots/s². The front advances about one neighbour spacing every L substeps, ≈ 100–150 dots/s (30–45 m/s, 3–5 v₀), which is faster than any bird flies; Procaccini et al. (2011) measured wave speeds often above the flock's own speed. **Banking**: the coordinated-turn roll `φ = atan(a_⊥ / (gσ))` is signed (+ = right wing down), with `g = 32.7` dots/s² (9.81 m/s²), and is smoothed as such by an EMA (τ = 0.1 s), so the flickering pushes of separation average out and only a sustained turn banks a bird; the ink and the wings use `|φ|`. An alarmed bird's 160 dots/s² turn banks it past 75°. **Start**: a disc of radius `min(R_f, 0.3·min(W, H_s))` at the roost with uniformly random headings at v₀, so each visit opens with order emerging from chaos. All randomness comes from `ctx.rng` in index order.

### dna_helix  (Molecules of Life, M10, M12, M15)
- **Intent**: the double helix of Watson and Crick's 1953 figure: two backbones winding right-handed around a ladder of coloured base pairs and turning in space. Then a replication fork runs through it. A helicase ring unzips the ladder, and each separated strand is copied into a daughter helix that is half old (grey) and half new (magenta): the leading strand continuously, the lagging strand in Okazaki fragments.
- **Model**: B-DNA in Å, with world +y up (CORE-R45 maps it to screen up) and the axis along +x.
  - **Helix geometry**:
    - Twist is `Ω = 2π/10.5` per bp, i.e. 34.29° (Wang 1979), with a rise of 3.4 Å, so the pitch is 35.7 Å.
    - The phosphorus backbone radius is `R = 8.9 Å` (Arnott & Hukins 1972).
    - The Watson angle is `θ_W(x, t) = φ(t) + 2πx/35.7 Å`. Crick is `θ_C = θ_W + Δ`, with `Δ = 2.2 rad` from C1′–C1′ = 10.5 Å at r = 5.9 Å (Saenger 1984). That gives axial groove widths of 12.5 Å (minor) and 23.2 Å (major).
    - A backbone point is `(x, y₀ + R cos θ, R sin θ)` about its duplex axis at height `y₀`. θ increases with x, so the helix is right-handed. Watson runs 5′→3′ along +x.
  - **Layout**: base pair `i` sits at `xᵢ = x_L + (i − 4.5)·3.4 Å`, where `x_L` is the world x at column 0, so column 0 falls midway between bp 4 and bp 5. Four pairs lie beyond each screen edge, so the camera sway never exposes a blunt end.
  - **Rungs**: a chord at `xᵢ` between the two backbones (the phosphorus positions), split at its midpoint so each half shows that strand's base. Rungs are drawn only every `stride`-th pair, so at least 2 columns separate them, and a rung seen end-on at a backbone crossing (a slanted chord under 2 rows, over 4 rows, or flatter than 1 column per 2 rows) is culled rather than drawn as a diagonal along a row. *Simplifications*: joining phosphorus positions puts each chord ≈ 4 Å off the axis on the minor-groove side (real B-DNA base pairs sit on it), and the helicase is a symbolic collar, not to scale.
  - **Sequence**: `N_MAX = 512` Watson bases in a stack array (enough to span the width at any aspect ratio up to w/h ≈ 50; beyond that the height fit wins and the duplex ends short of the right edge). The GC count is a seeded uniform integer in [⌈0.35·N_MAX⌉, ⌊0.65·N_MAX⌋] (each G or C at random), placed by a Fisher–Yates shuffle from `ctx.rng`. Crick is the complement.
  - **Spin**: `φ(t) = φ₀ − ω·t` on loop-local time, which wraps at the `reset` midpoint (where the fade is 0), so the apex angle is identical in every loop, with `ω = 2π·v_f/10.5 = 1.7952 rad/s`. That is the unwinding a fork at `v_f = 3 bp/s` needs, so the apex angle stays fixed. The unreplicated duplex spins as if its far end were free; in vivo it cannot rotate, and topoisomerases instead relax the positive supercoils that build ahead of the fork.
  - **Fork**: the apex is at `f(τ) = v_f·τ` bp, where τ is the time since `unwind` began. It keeps advancing through `hold` and is frozen during `reset`. There is no fork during `helix`. `v_f` is ≈ 6–17× slower than human forks (1–3 kb/min) and ≈ 300× slower than *E. coli* (≈ 1 kb/s).
  - **Arms**: behind the fork (`s = f − i > 0`) the parental strands separate into two arms, displaced by `±Y(s) = ±1.7R·smoothstep(s/6)` in world y.
    - The upper arm (+y) is the Watson template, which is the lagging-strand template. A DnaB-style helicase ring encircles it at the apex, translocating 5′→3′.
    - The lower arm is the Crick template (the leading strand).
    - Each new strand takes its partner's angular slot (Watson + Δ, or Crick − Δ), so each daughter is a B duplex like the parent. Keeping B geometry in the arms is artistic licence; real single-stranded DNA at the fork is unwound.
  - **Synthesis** is 5′→3′ only:
    - Leading: new base `j` is made at `f(τ) − 2 = j`, i.e. `τ = (j + 2)/3` (the polymerase trails the helicase by 2 bp).
    - Lagging: Okazaki fragments of `L_ok = 8` bp (scaled down from 100–200 nt in eukaryotes and 1–2 kb in bacteria) cover `[8k, 8k + 8)`. Fragment `k` is primed at its high-x end when `f ≥ 8k + 9` (`τ_k = (8k + 9)/3`) and extended toward decreasing x at `v_pol = 8 bp/s`, so base `j` is made at `τ_k + (8k + 7 − j)/8`. The fragment is complete 7/8 s after priming, and ligase seals the nick 0.5 s after completion.
    - Template bases that are not yet paired show as half-rungs.
  - Every quantity is a closed-form function of scene time; nothing is integrated.
- **Motion**: a `Timeline` of `helix` 4 s → `unwind` 14 s → `hold` 4 s → `reset` 2 s, looping every 24 s.
  - **Spin**: from the first frame the spin reads as a barber-pole drift along +x at 10.2 Å/s. That equals the fork speed, so the fork travels with the pattern. It is ≈ 7 columns/s at 80×24 (≈ 11 while zoomed) and ≈ 16 at 200×50.
  - **Camera**:
    - Yaw is `0.2·sin(2πt/19 s)`, capped at `asin(13 Å / visible half-width)` so the edges swing ≤ 13 Å in depth; pitch is 0.18 rad, and distance is `max(40R, 6·visible half-width)` (gentle perspective).
    - A zoom `z(t)` is 2.2 during `helix`. It eases to 1 over the first 3 s of `unwind` (`in_out_cubic`) and stays 1 until the `reset` midpoint, where it steps back to 2.2 while every level is 0.

### hp_protein_folding  (Life from Matter, M13, M16)
- **Intent**: why a protein folds: a chain of oily (hydrophobic, H) and water-loving (polar, P) residues thrashes as a loose coil while hot, then, as it cools, collapses and tucks its oily residues into a core away from the water, leaving the polar ones on the surface.
- **Model**: the 2-D square-lattice HP model (Lau & Dill 1989): a self-avoiding chain of 48 residues, energy `E = −(number of H–H contacts)` between lattice neighbours that are not chain neighbours. The sequence is the 48-residue benchmark `PPHPPHHPPHHPPPPPHHHHHHHHHHPPPPPPHHPPHHPPHPPHHHHH` (best known 2-D fold: 23 contacts). Metropolis Monte Carlo (P: statistical mechanics) with end, corner, crankshaft and pull moves (Lesh et al. 2003); each attempt moves the chain, is checked for self-avoidance on an occupancy grid, and is accepted with probability `min(1, e^(−ΔE/T))`. Simulated annealing on a looping `Timeline` (C: the hydrophobic effect as a contact energy; B: a protein).
- **Motion**: 40 000 attempted moves per simulated second at `T ≤ 1.6`, fewer when hotter (`× (1.6/T)²`, ≥ 6 %), so the hot coil's beads stay distinct on screen; beads ease towards their lattice sites (50 ms), so the chain writhes smoothly; the camera follows the chain's bounding box closely and zooms in slowly (out quickly). ### physarum  (Living Systems, M10, M12, M15, M18)
- **Intent**: a slime mould solving a transport problem in time-lapse. A glowing inoculum spills outward in a lace of veins, finds the scattered oat flakes, and thickens into a few pulsing tubes that link them like a rail map (Nakagaki et al. 2000; Tero et al. 2010). The network then starves and dissolves tube by tube into darkness. Fresh flakes drop onto the empty plate, and a new colony starts somewhere else.
- **Model**: the agent-based approximation of Jones (2010). The plate is the screen above the label row: `h_p = h − 1` rows when the label is drawn and `h_p = h` otherwise, so the plate's absorbing edge keeps every tube off that row and the label sits under the organism, not inside it. The chemoattractant `T` is a `Grid<f32>` on a capped square lattice `L = grid::lattice_for(Size(w, 2h_p), 20_000, 1.25)` (CORE-R48). That gives 197×98 at 200×50 and 80×46 at 80×24; below the cap the lattice equals the pixel display, so the view is exact. There are `n = max(1, round(p·lw·lh))` agents with `p = 5 %` (965 and 184), within the population range Jones explored. Each agent has a continuous position and a heading in `Z₁₆` (22.5° units) with a 16-entry `(cos, sin)` table, so no trig runs per step; turns are addition in Z₁₆ (`rotate(h, t)`, + = clockwise on screen). Its site is its rounded position. Each Jones step is two passes over a fresh Fisher–Yates shuffle of the live agents (rand's `SliceRandom::shuffle` on a `SplitMix64` drawn from `ctx.rng`). **Motor pass**: step `SS = 1` px along the heading. If the target site is on the lattice and unoccupied (particle exclusion, one agent per site), or is the agent's own site (a sub-site move, possible after a diagonal step from a non-integer position), the agent moves and deposits `depT = 5` at its new site. Otherwise it stays, deposits nothing and takes a uniformly random new heading. **Sensory pass**: sample `T` at three sensors at distance `SO = clamp(round(0.09·min(lw, lh)), 3, 9)` px (4 at 80×24, 9 at 200×50) and angles `h − SA, h, h + SA` with `SA = 22.5°`. Off-lattice sensors read −1, so walls repel. The agent then rotates by `RA = 45°` according to Jones's rule: F strictly greatest ⇒ keep the heading; F strictly least ⇒ ±RA by coin; otherwise turn toward the strictly stronger side (FL < FR ⇒ toward R, FR < FL ⇒ toward L); any remaining tie ⇒ keep. **Chemoattractant**: after both passes, `T ← (1 − δ)·mean₃ₓ₃(T)` as a separable 3-tap box filter with `δ = 0.1`. The plate edge absorbs: sites off the lattice count as 0 in the mean, so attractant leaks out there and the network sits on the plate instead of tracing the frame. **Drawn field**: a second grid `V` receives the same deposits (at inoculation and in the motor pass) and the same diffusion and decay, but no food pinning. It is what is drawn (through the display EMA `S`, see Inks); the agents sense `T` only. **Food**: `n_f = clamp(round(lw·lh/800), 4, 9)` flakes (5 at 80×24, 9 at 200×50) at seeded positions in the central 80 %. They are at least `0.25·min(lw, lh)` apart and at least `max(2·SO, 0.15·min(lw, lh))` beyond the inoculum's rim, so the colony has to spread before it touches one. Placement uses at most 200 rejection tries and accepts fewer flakes on a crowded plate, including 0. Food discs of radius 1.5 px are pinned to `T = max(T, 20)` (4·depT) during explore and consolidate, which anchors trunk lines between sources as in Tero et al. (2010). **Inoculum**: a disc of radius `√(2n/π)` px (50 % occupancy) centred at a seeded point in the central 40 %, with random headings. Each agent deposits once at seeding, and `V` is then blurred twice by the same filter with δ = 0, so the colony shows at t = 0 as a soft drop rather than a checkerboard of single deposits. **Rate**: one Jones step per `SIM_DT` (60 per simulated second), counted by the core `FixedStep(SIM_DT, 15)` (CORE-R9..R11). This is a time-lapse: the network in Tero et al. took about a day to grow. **Timeline** (48 s, loops): explore 10 s → consolidate 30 s (the physics is unchanged; the network coarsens under its own dynamics while the food holds the trunk lines) → fade 6 s (food unpinned; each agent dies at a seeded uniform time in the phase, and a dead agent stops moving and depositing and frees its site, so tubes thin and snap one by one) → rest 2 s (the trail decays to black, `0.9^120 ≈ 3·10⁻⁶`). At rest start, the next cycle's inoculum centre and food are drawn from `ctx.rng`, and the new flakes fade in. The inoculum is seeded at the cycle boundary. A step spans `[t₀, t₁]`: starvation and the Jones step are decided at `t₀`, and these two events at `t₁`, so the state already holds them when the scene time reads `t₁` and the drawing never runs ahead of the colony (PHY-A5). Every cycle differs, and each seed is deterministic.
- **Motion**: agents move 60 px/s and the trail changes every step. Within 1 s the inoculum grows filaments that reach visibly past its rim; because the rim streams outward at 60 px/s, at 80×24 the nearest flakes are touched within about a second and tied in by `+` tubes by ≈ 3 s. By 10 s the sources are joined by a web with loops. During consolidation, brightness pulses along the stable tubes as agents stream through them, while their edges hold still (PHY-A7). The fade reads as a slow disintegration, followed by a dark plate where new flakes appear, and then a new bloom.
- **Resize** (ADR-0012): the lattice comes from `lattice_for`. If it is unchanged (same aspect bucket, with both sizes at or above the cap in both axes), only the display map and `Pixels` are rebuilt, no `ctx.rng` is consumed, and the simulation stays bit-identical. If it changes, `T` and `V` are resampled with `grid::resample_bilinear`. Agent positions are scaled by `(lw'/lw, lh'/lh)` and re-inserted into the occupancy grid in index order, and any collisions are dropped. Outside fade and rest, the population is then brought to `n' = round(p·lw'·lh')`: trimmed evenly in index order (agent i of n is kept when `⌊(i + 1)·n'/n⌋ > ⌊i·n'/n⌋`, with no randomness), or topped up with agents placed on free sites where `T` is above the mean, using at most 16 `ctx.rng` tries per agent before skipping it. Food is scaled, SO is recomputed, and the timeline is untouched. Agent, order and occupancy buffers have capacity 1000 (= p·20 000) from `build`.
- **Inks**: the drawn field `S` is `V` through a display-only EMA, `S += (V − S)·(1 − e^(−h/0.15 s))` after every step, reset to `V` at inoculation and on a lattice change. The discrete agents' per-step deposits make tube edges flip grade on almost every frame (≈ 30 % of lit ASCII cells at 80×24); the filter keeps the pulsing and the growth but stills the edges. The agents sense `T`, so no model result depends on it. `S` uses `Role::Seq` via `ink::seq(0.2 + 0.8·u)`, with `u = min(1, √(S / (2.5·T_ref)))`. `T_ref` is reset at every inoculation (the first and each cycle boundary) to the bright-half mean of the new inoculum's `V`, so every bloom opens on the same soft drop, and is otherwise an EMA (τ = 1 s) of the mean `V` over sites above `V`'s global mean (the global total comes out of the diffusion pass). During the first 1.5 s of explore it follows `max(bright-half mean, 0.45·max V)` with τ = 0.25 s instead: a young inoculum is a flat stack of deposits about 2.5× its bright-half mean, which that reference alone paints as one saturated `#` disc (≈ 44 % of lit cells), and exposing for its peak leaves `#` to the hottest ≈ 10 % of the drop. It is frozen during fade and rest, so the dying network dims instead of being re-exposed. (Inheriting the old network's frozen reference instead opened every later bloom as an underexposed `.`/`:` haze with holes, ≈ 25 % fewer lit cells than the first and no `+`/`#`.) Starting the ramp at 0.2 skips the colormap's violet floor. In astro the organism shows crimson halos, amber tubes and pale-yellow junctions (Physarum is yellow), which reads differently from the full-range Seq fields of gray_scott, belousov_zhabotinsky and liesegang_rings. Pixels with `u < 0.45` (`V` below ≈ `T_ref/2`) stay `Ink::BLANK`, so the plate is the terminal's own dark; a lower floor lights the diffuse attractant around every tube and fattens the veins into blobs. Food is `Role::Structure` 255, pale cool oat flakes against the warm organism; because the pinned `T` is not drawn, a flake has no glow until the organism reaches it. The colony's flakes fade out over the last 1 s of the fade (`255·(1 − smoothstep)`), so rest opens on an empty plate, and the next colony's fade in from 0 to 255 over rest (`255·smoothstep(progress)`); a flake at level 0 is not drawn. Label `Role::Text` 110. Agents are not drawn: the trail is the organism.
- **ASCII**: half-block `Pixels` (CORE-R42) over the plate, `w × 2h_p`; each display pixel samples `S` through `grid::SampleMap` (CORE-R52) and is tone-mapped in `update`; the floor test runs on `S/(2.5·T_ref) < 0.45²`, so dark pixels skip the square root. The `blit` closure works per cell: food pixels count as blank; both blank ⇒ untouched; otherwise `m = max(u_top, u_bottom)` maps to `.` (< 0.60), `:` (< 0.75), `+` (< 0.90) or `#`, using the brighter pixel's Seq ink. In ASCII, `render` then puts a single `@` in the flake's Structure ink at each flake's centre cell; in Unicode a flake stays a 5-pixel plus. At 80×24 the snapshots show a soft `.`/`:`/`+` drop and five `@` at t = 0, filaments already reaching the two nearest flakes at t = 1 s, and a `.`/`:` lace with `+`/`#` trunks through the `@`s at t = 5 s.
- **Label**: `SA 22.5 RA 45 SO 9` (ASCII, rebuilt in `resize` when SO changes) right-aligned in the row below the plate (GEN-R13), drawn only when `w ≥ 40` and `h ≥ 12` and it fits right of the title, with a blank cell either side, so it never looks glued to a tube.
- **Perf**: at 200×50 (lattice 197×98, 965 agents, 1 Jones step per update): the shuffle, the motor and sensory passes, two separable diffuse-decay passes (`T` and `V`; the `V` pass also returns its total), one more pass over `V` for the bright-half mean and peak, food pinning (< 1 µs) and the display EMA of `S` (one pass), then the `SampleMap` tone map of 200×98 pixels, all in `update`; `render` blits 9.8 k cells. Criterion on a loaded machine (load average ≈ 15–26): 0.28–0.39 ms at 200×50 (0.64 ms at load ≈ 80–99 with the 197×98 plate) and 0.71–1.05 ms at 400×100, against the < 1 ms aim (GEN-R8 is measured at 200×50). At 400×100 the 80 k-pixel tone map is ≈ 0.45 ms of that; testing the floor on the square saves ≈ 5–10 % of it. The simulation cost is independent of terminal size above the cap; the tone map and the blit scale with the display. Steady state is allocation-free.
- **Acceptance**: `PHY-A1` `steer(f, fl, fr, coin)` returns exactly 0 / ±2 (by coin) / +2 / −2 / 0 in Z₁₆ units (+ = toward FR) for the five cases of Jones's rule, listed literally and then exhaustively over all orderings of three values with ties; every entry of the 16-direction table has unit length to 1e-6; `rotate` is addition in Z₁₆ (sixteen +1 rotations return to every start, `rotate(0, −RA) = 14`, `rotate(15, RA) = 1`) and + is clockwise on screen; ### protocell_assembly  (Life from Matter, M21)
- **Intent**: soap-like molecules in water hide their oily tails from it: scattered molecules gather into micelles, then flat bilayer sheets, and a sheet curls shut into a vesicle that traps a little water and solutes, a first cell-like compartment; it dissolves and the cycle repeats.
- **Model**: coarse-grained overdamped Brownian dynamics (implicit solvent) of `N` amphiphiles (scaled with area, capped ≈ 300), each a rigid head–tail dimer (position + orientation). Forces: soft excluded volume, short-range tail–tail attraction, weak head–head repulsion; translational and rotational Brownian kicks (`ctx.rng`); neighbours through `SpatialHash`. A looping `Timeline` (~40 s) ramps the attraction (`dispersed` → `micelles`), then a curvature bias (a preferred head spacing) that favours sheets (`bilayer`) and, as a sheet grows, closure (`vesicle`); `N_s` solutes diffuse freely (no force but excluded volume); when the sheet's slots are dealt a few are already where its lumen will be, and some end up trapped inside. Then the attraction switches off and the vesicle disperses.
- **Motion**: jittering molecules, clustering, sheet growth, closure, dispersal.
- **Resize**: box from the size; molecules kept in box units (positions rescaled); hash rebuilt.
- **Look** *(GEN-R26, molecules)*: each amphiphile a small `Hot` head atom (`Dots::stipple_puff`, small σ, solid core) with a short 1-px `Cold` tail thread; solutes small `Accent` atoms. No half-blocks; much black between clusters.
- **Title**: `Protocell · lipids self-assemble`; captions `dispersed`, `micelles`, `bilayer`, `vesicle`.
- **Stylisation**: a 2-D coarse-grained model, not real lipids; time in model units. Micelles form from the forces alone; the bilayer and vesicle are guided: chosen molecules are steered into slots on a sheet whose curvature ramps until it closes (a stand-in for a change in molecular packing, as fatty acids do when the pH falls), and molecules on their way pass through others (a stand-in for lipid exchange). The loop dissolves the vesicle by switching the attraction off.
- **Acceptance**: `PROTO-A1` with all forces off the translational displacement variance per step is `2DΔt` within 5 %; `PROTO-A2` during `micelles`, most tails' nearest neighbour is a tail and heads point outward from their cluster's centre; `PROTO-A3` during `vesicle`, a closed ring of heads (a cycle in the head-contact graph enclosing the vesicle's lumen; heads are in contact closer than two excluded-volume diameters, so nothing can pass between them) forms within the act for 3 seeds; `PROTO-A4` `caption()` follows the acts and loops; `PROTO-A5` head atoms are `Hot`, tails `Cold`, solutes `Accent`, nothing else lit.
- **As built**: acts 8/10/10/12 s; N = 0.07 per unit² clamped to 48–300 (a molecule is 1 unit, ≥ 4.5 dots); the sheet recruits max(55 % of N, 56) molecules into leaflet slots whose counts space both rings of the closed vesicle evenly and both leaflets span the whole mid-plane, flat or closed (so the flat sheet's two leaflets are equally long); in a shared braille cell a head's centre dot outranks a tail and a tail outranks a head's outer dots, so tails read `Cold` and heads `Hot`; tail beads touch at the mid-plane; a molecule in its slot diffuses at D = 0.3; curvature ramps to closure over 8 s; positions are drawn through a 0.35 s exponential average, so thermal jitter does not flicker cells.

### ribosome_translation  (Molecules of Life, M21)
- **Intent**: a ribosome reads a messenger RNA three letters at a time; transfer RNAs bring amino acids, the protein grows as a chain of beads and coils into a helix as it leaves.
- **Model**: real short coding sequences (a few dozen codons, `AUG` to a stop) translated with the standard genetic code (all 64 codons); residue classes: charged (D E K R H), hydrophobic (A V L I M F W C), polar (S T N Q G Y P). The elongation cycle on a looping `Timeline`: a tRNA atom carrying its amino acid flies in to the A site, peptidyl transfer adds the bead to the chain, translocation moves the mRNA exactly 3 nucleotides, the empty tRNA leaves from the E site. At the stop codon the chain is released and drifts off; the next loop translates the next sequence from a small table. The exiting chain coils into a projected α-helix (3.6 residues per turn), stylised.
- **Motion**: the mRNA ratchets codon by codon; tRNAs arrive and leave; the chain grows and coils.
- **Resize**: scene scale from the size; state in model units.
- **Look** *(GEN-R26, molecules: ball-and-stick, as `chemical_garden`)*: the mRNA a 1-px `Wave` thread with codon ticks, codon letters as small `Text` labels under the ribosome's sites when room allows; the ribosome two subunits, each a 1-px `Structure` outline over a faint stipple (peak ≤ 0.08); tRNAs small `Particle` atoms with a short stem thread; peptide beads small stipple atoms joined by 1-px bonds, `Hot` charged, `Cold` hydrophobic, `Accent` polar. Readout right-aligned on row `h − 1`: the tail of the growing one-letter sequence (e.g. `MALWMR`), shortened to fit.
- **Title**: `Ribosome · codons become protein`; captions `elongation`, `termination`, `folding`.
- **Stylisation**: one ribosome; no initiation, elongation or release factors drawn; real rates of ~5–20 codons/s shown slower; the fold is a drawn helix, not computed, and every chain is drawn folding into an α-helix whatever its real fold (EGFP's N-terminus is a β-strand).
- **Acceptance**: `RIB-A1` translating the scene's mRNA with its code table gives the expected protein (`AUG` → `M`; the three stops end translation), checked against the full 64-codon table; `RIB-A2` each elongation cycle advances the mRNA by exactly 3 nucleotides and adds exactly one bead; `RIB-A3` bead inks follow the residue classes; `RIB-A4` consecutive beads stay one bond length apart within 10 % in the model's 3-D frame, and no drawn bond is longer than 1.1 bonds; `RIB-A5` `caption()` follows the acts and loops.
- **As built**: mRNAs: human β-globin (`HBB`, 31 residues + `UGA`; codon 7 `GAG` is Glu6 in the usual numbering), EGFP (30 + `UAA`) and human preproinsulin's signal peptide (`INS`, 24 + `UAG`), one per loop in that order; acts 20/3/6 s; one cell per nucleotide, a bond is one codon (6 dots); the tunnel opens at 55 % of the height with the ribosome right of centre and the chain leaving to the left; the chain leaves the tunnel as a loose coil (140° per residue, radius 0.45 bond) and in `folding` winds into an α-helix (100° per residue, 3.6 per turn, radius 2.3/3.8 bond), the rise set by the bond length, drawn obliquely with far-side beads one step dimmer; the small subunit sits below the codon letters with one blank cell row between; the readout shows the last 12 residues.
